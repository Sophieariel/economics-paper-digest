# AI Economics Papers — 2026-07-26

## 1. Innovation without Borders? The Geography of Technological Diffusion [Adoption & Diffusion]
**Authors:** Ursel Baumann, Zoë B. Cullen, Ester Faia, Annalisa Ferrando, Ricardo Perez-Truglia, Judit Rariga
**Date:** June 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35314 (NBER Working Paper 35314); also CEPR Discussion Paper DP21601
**Abstract:** How well does innovation diffuse across geographic boundaries? To shed light on this question, we present a large-scale field experiment involving 3,300 firms across twelve European Union countries. We elicit firms' beliefs about the share of similar firms that have invested in AI, both domestically and among peers in Germany, France, and Italy, and randomly provide half of the sample with accurate information about domestic and foreign AI investment rates. We find that firms substantially underestimate competitors' current AI investment, both domestically and abroad, and that they update their expectations about competitors' future AI investment in response to the information treatment, subsequently raising their own expected AI investment rate. We document strong strategic complementarities within borders: a 1 percentage point increase in the expected share of domestic peers investing in AI raises a firm's own expected AI investment rate by 0.570 percentage points. These complementarities are absent across borders, where comparable effects are statistically insignificant. Overall, the evidence demonstrates that innovation diffusion and strategic complementarities in AI investment are much stronger domestically than internationally.

## 2. What is Generative AI Worth? [Trade & Welfare]
**Authors:** Erik Brynjolfsson, Avinash Collis, Felix Eggers, Sophia Kazinnik, David Nguyen
**Date:** April 13, 2026
**Source:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6569938 ; https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/what-is-generative-ai-worth/
**Abstract:** We estimate consumer welfare gains from the rapid adoption of generative AI tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and Copilot in the United States. Using online choice experiments, we elicit representative US adults' willingness to accept (WTA) compensation for giving up access to AI chatbot tools for one month, fielded in two waves in July 2025 and March 2026. Mean WTA increased from $98 in 2025 to $124.50 in 2026, a 27% increase, while median WTA rose from $3.40 to $11.40. Combined with growth in the adult user base from 98 million to 115 million, these estimates imply that aggregate consumer surplus increased from $116 billion to $172 billion over the period. This surplus substantially exceeds estimated revenues from generative AI in the United States, suggesting that consumers capture most of the welfare gains from these tools so far. We document significant variation in valuations by usage frequency, workplace use, subscription status, and demographic characteristics, concluding that generative AI already generates substantial, and rapidly growing, welfare gains that are largely uncaptured by conventional GDP and revenue statistics.

## 3. Mapping AI into Production: A Field Experiment on Firm Performance [Firms & Competition]
**Authors:** Hyunjin Kim, Dahyeon Kim, Rembrand Koning
**Date:** March 30, 2026
**Source:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6513481 ; https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=68814 (INSEAD/HBS Working Paper)
**Abstract:** AI can deliver productivity gains on individual tasks, yet evidence on whether these gains aggregate to firm performance remains limited. We study a central friction in AI adoption that we call the mapping problem: discovering where and how AI creates value within a firm's production process. Across 515 startups from around the world participating in a global, three-month virtual accelerator program, we run a field experiment in which treated firms receive information about how other firms have reorganized production around AI, exposing founders to use cases across a broader set of firm functions and prompting them to search beyond the familiar applications they would otherwise default to. Treated firms discover 44% more AI use cases, concentrated in product development and strategy. These changes translate into economically meaningful performance gains: treated firms complete 12% more tasks, are 18% more likely to acquire paying customers, and generate 1.9 times higher revenue. Revenue and investment gains are largest at the 90th percentile and above, consistent with AI expanding the upper range of what firms achieve rather than modestly improving marginal ventures.

## 4. Early AI Adoption and Firm Productivity Growth in a Middle-Income Economy [Productivity & Growth]
**Authors:** Juan Duran-Vanegas
**Date:** April 3, 2026
**Source:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6515438
**Abstract:** This paper provides early evidence on the relationship between AI adoption and firm-level productivity growth in a middle-income economy, combining data on AI use from the 2019 Colombian Enterprise ICT Survey with longitudinal manufacturing data. AI adoption is associated with a 16 percent cumulative increase in labor productivity over 2016–2019, equivalent to roughly 5 percent annualized growth. These productivity differentials appear to be driven by higher sales and value added rather than by reductions in costs or employment, are similar among firms that develop AI in-house versus those that outsource it, and increase for firms with higher preexisting technical capabilities. AI adoption is also associated with a small but statistically significant decline in the share of administrative workers, suggesting a reallocation of tasks away from administrative functions rather than aggregate labor-shedding. The results suggest that early AI adopters in developing and middle-income economies can realize productivity gains comparable in magnitude to those documented in advanced economies, though gains are concentrated among technically capable firms.

## 5. Generative AI and Firm Productivity: Field Experiments in Online Retail [Productivity & Growth]
**Authors:** Lu Fang, Zhe Yuan, Kaifu Zhang, Dante Donati, Miklos Sarvary
**Date:** Revised June 29, 2026 (originally posted October 2025)
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.12049
**Abstract:** We quantify the causal impact of generative AI (GenAI) on firm productivity through large-scale randomized field experiments involving millions of users and products at a leading cross-border online retail platform. Over six months in 2023–2024, GenAI-based enhancements were integrated into seven consumer-facing business workflows, including pre-sale customer service, product search and discovery, and advertising. GenAI adoption significantly increases sales, with treatment effects ranging from no detectable impact to 16.3%, depending on GenAI's marginal contribution relative to existing firm practices; customer-support applications such as pre-sale service chatbots generate the largest improvements, search and product-discovery tasks yield more modest gains, and advertising-related applications show no statistically significant effects. Because inputs and prices were held constant across experimental arms, these sales gains map directly into total factor productivity improvements, with an implied annual incremental value of approximately $5 per consumer across the four applications with positive effects. Gains operate primarily through higher conversion rates rather than larger cart values, are not associated with worse post-purchase outcomes (return rates and ratings do not deteriorate), and exhibit substantial heterogeneity, with disproportionately larger gains for smaller and newer sellers and for less-experienced consumers. The study provides novel, large-scale causal evidence on how GenAI shapes sales productivity in online retail.
