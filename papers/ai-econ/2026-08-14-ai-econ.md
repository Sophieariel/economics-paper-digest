# AI Economics Papers — 2026-08-14

## 1. Organizational Technology Ladders: Remote Work and Generative AI Adoption [Adoption & Diffusion]

**Authors:** Gregor Schubert
**Date:** 2026-08-12
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11626
**Summary:** The author tests whether firms' earlier adoption of remote-work technology during COVID reshaped their later uptake of generative AI, using U.S. job-posting data and an instrumental-variables strategy based on predicted local labor-market pressure to offer remote work. A 10-percentage-point rise in a firm's 2021-22 remote hiring share raises the share of its job postings mentioning generative AI in 2023-24 by 0.4 points across firms and 0.7 points within firms across occupations, with the effect concentrated where hiring shifted toward technical and managerial skills. Firms that later imposed return-to-office mandates — read as evidence of low remote-work productivity — show a much larger AI-adoption response, supporting an "organizational technology ladder" in which one technology's adoption lowers the cost of adopting the next.

## 2. Keep, Customize, or Exit: Default Design and Token Pricing in LLM Reasoning Services [Firms & Competition]

**Authors:** Ahmet Bugra Gundogan, Yigit Turkmen, Melih Bastopcu
**Date:** 2026-08-13
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13315
**Summary:** The authors model an LLM reasoning-service provider that sets a per-token price and a default amount of reasoning tokens, while users can accept the default, customize their allocation, or leave, framing the interaction as a Stackelberg game solved in closed form for the user's optimal customized allocation. They characterize the provider's optimal default through a three-regime rule, reduce finding the price-default equilibrium to a one-dimensional optimization, and show the default only matters for what gets implemented when users value avoiding the hassle of customizing. Experiments on open-weight reasoning models across math and science benchmarks confirm the underlying accuracy-token tradeoff and show how task and model characteristics move equilibrium prices and defaults, offering a market-design lens on how AI providers should package and price inference.

## 3. Error-Aware Reverse Auction Mechanism for Large Language Model Routing [Firms & Competition]

**Authors:** Haolong Chen, Zhengyuan Xin, Liang Zhang, Lei Xue, Guangxu Zhu
**Date:** 2026-08-13
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12719
**Summary:** The authors propose routing user queries among competing LLM providers via a reverse auction in which providers bid with self-predicted success probabilities and costs, replacing today's centralized routers that predict model performance from a single task center and scale poorly as the model pool grows. Because both provider bids and the router's own evaluations are noisy, they design an "error-aware" mechanism that they prove is Bayesian incentive-compatible and individually rational under this dual noise, and they derive an explicit bound on the welfare loss it can produce. Simulations and real benchmarks show the mechanism is robust to noisy bids and beats centralized routing on the cost-performance frontier, illustrating how market-style mechanism design could allocate AI inference more efficiently than centralized routers.
