# AI Economics Papers — 2026-08-16

## 1. Keep, Customize, or Exit: Default Design and Token Pricing in LLM Reasoning Services [Firms & Competition]

**Authors:** Ahmet Bugra Gundogan, Yigit Turkmen, Melih Bastopcu
**Date:** 2026-08-13
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13315
**Summary:** The authors model an LLM reasoning-service provider that sets a per-token price and a default reasoning-token allocation while users can accept the default, customize it, or exit, framing the interaction as a Stackelberg game and deriving the user's optimal customized allocation in closed form. They characterize the provider's optimal default through a three-regime rule and show defaults only bind reasoning outcomes when users value avoiding the hassle of customizing, validating the accuracy-token tradeoff on open-weight reasoning models across math and science benchmarks. The results show how equilibrium prices and default designs for AI inference services depend on model and task characteristics, giving a tractable account of how such market-design choices shape user welfare and provider profit.

## 2. How Organizations Use AI: Evidence from ChatGPT [Adoption & Diffusion]

**Authors:** Aaron Chatterji, David Holtz, Neel Rakholia, Prasanna Tambe, Gawesha Weeratunga
**Date:** 2026-08-12
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12236
**Summary:** The authors link ChatGPT Enterprise account records to worker roles, task classifications, and public-company financials through March 2026, covering more than 1,500 organizations and 17 million messages, to document how firms adopt and use frontier generative AI. They find enterprise usage has grown through both new-firm adoption and rising intensity among existing adopters, that adoption among public companies is concentrated in larger, more valuable, R&D- and SG&A-intensive firms, and that early-career workers show especially high usage intensity across a broad range of knowledge-work tasks. The findings show firms differ widely in the speed, breadth, and purpose of enterprise AI adoption and are still learning how to integrate it into workflows, offering some of the first large-scale, firm-linked evidence on generative AI's diffusion inside organizations.

## 3. Do People Follow AI Advice? Evidence from a Pension Portfolio Choice Experiment [Trade & Welfare]

**Authors:** Hongseok Choi, Jeongbin Kim, Matthew Kovach, Kyu-Min Lee, Euncheol Shin, Hector Tzavellas
**Date:** 2026-08-11
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11371
**Summary:** In an experiment with 400 South Korean workers enrolled in defined-contribution pension plans, the authors randomize whether participants receive an aggressive or conservative AI-generated portfolio recommendation, with or without a short rationale, and measure how much of that experimentally induced difference passes through to final allocations. About 37% of the gap between the two recommendations transmits into final portfolios, changing expected return, volatility, and risk-grade composition, and 81% of participants revise their allocation, with most who do moving substantially toward the AI's suggestion regardless of whether a rationale was given. The results show AI financial advice is only partially and selectively incorporated into real retirement-saving decisions, a directly relevant data point for regulators weighing how much weight algorithmic advice should carry in consumer-protection rules for financial AI.
