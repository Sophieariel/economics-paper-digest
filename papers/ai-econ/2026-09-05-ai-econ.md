# AI Economics Papers — 2026-09-05

## 1. From Prompt to Purchase: How AI Brand Recommendations Move Consumers on the Open Web [Firms & Competition]

**Authors:** Michael Iannelli, Alan Ai
**Date:** first posted 2026-06-09; revised 2026-08-31
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10907
**Summary:** Joining opt-in clickstream data to the same users' ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini conversations, the authors isolate the causal effect of an unprompted AI brand mention on off-platform consumer behavior, using a pre-trend event study, a stance classifier, and a within-response same-category control to separate genuine exposure from incidental name-drops of brands the user already patronizes. They find that a fresh AI recommendation raises same-brand search by 4.3 percentage points and drives measurable increases in visits to the brand's own site and to retailer pages, effects far larger than those from incidental mentions or unnamed same-category brands. The result identifies a new, currently unattributed acquisition channel at the head of the customer journey, with direct implications for how advertising markets and platform competition will be reshaped as conversational AI mediates brand discovery.

## 2. One Capability or Many? Testing the Economic Validity of Frontier AI Evaluation [Firms & Competition]

**Authors:** Louis Yiven Zhu
**Date:** 2026-08-29
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29420
**Summary:** Using a latent-variable model with pre-registered hypotheses fit to 421 model configurations across twelve benchmarks, the author tests whether the "economic" benchmarks that increasingly drive leaderboard rankings, and thus procurement and regulatory scrutiny, measure a capability distinct from general model progress. A single factor explains most of the variance and tracks release date almost as strongly as capability itself, so most of the gap between models released months apart is calendar time rather than a genuine capability difference, even though a multi-factor model still predicts held-out economic-task scores better than a single general index. The finding is a caution for how organizations and regulators should read frontier AI leaderboards: raw benchmark gaps between contemporaneous models should be date-adjusted before being treated as evidence of a real capability or market advantage.
