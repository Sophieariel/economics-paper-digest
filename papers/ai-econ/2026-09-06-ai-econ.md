## 1. AI worsens climate change, integrated assessment shows [Trade & Welfare]

**Authors:** Huiying Ye, Richard S. J. Tol, Fangzhi Wang
**Date:** 2026-08-25
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24670
**Summary:** The authors build a unified climate-economy framework by folding AI's effects on emissions, output, and climate damages into the DICE integrated assessment model, distinguishing a modest "ICT-like" AI trajectory from a more transformative "Industrial Revolution-like" one. Calibrated to available evidence, they find AI development is net-polluting under current low abatement: the ICT-like path adds about 0.1°C of warming by 2100 and the IR-like path about 0.8°C, with resulting climate damages offsetting roughly a fifth to a quarter of AI's economic gains. Because the AI-versus-abatement investment trade-off turns out to be driven mainly by AI's economic upside rather than its direct emissions footprint, the paper concludes that climate mitigation and AI investment are complements, offering one of the first attempts to place AI's aggregate welfare effects inside a standard climate-economy model.
