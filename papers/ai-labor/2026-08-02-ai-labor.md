# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-08-02

## 1. The Economic Benefits and Costs of AI and Policies to Mitigate AI's Impact on Inequality [Policy]

**Authors:** Matthew O. Jackson, Zafer Kanik
**Date:** Submitted July 1, 2026; revised July 11, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01101
**Summary:** The authors build a model of how increasingly productive AI reallocates labor and shifts wages across three types of workers: those building AI, those substituted by it, and those in final-goods production it does not touch. They find wages rise fastest for AI-building labor, fall for substituted labor in both absolute and relative terms, and track overall GDP growth for the unaffected group, with the transition's speed and impact depending on whether AI production is competitive or monopolistic. The paper then derives optimal tax and regulatory policies that achieve Pareto improvements, showing these differ depending on the competitive structure of AI production, which gives policymakers a concrete framework for weighing redistribution against monopoly-restricted deployment.
