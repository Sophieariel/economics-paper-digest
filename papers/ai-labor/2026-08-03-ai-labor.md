# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-08-03

## 1. A robust association between LLM use and scientific productivity: Assessing stopping-time selection [Skills & Tasks]

**Authors:** Keigo Kusumegi, Xinyu Yang, Paul Ginsparg, Mathijs de Vaan, Toby Stuart, Yian Yin
**Date:** Submitted July 31, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28968
**Summary:** Responding to a critique (Renault, Bergeaud, and Bosquet) that the measured link between LLM adoption and scientific productivity could be a statistical artifact of how researchers date an author's adoption month, the authors test whether this "stopping-time selection" bias is large enough to explain away the effect. Recalibrating the critique's own placebo test to the detector's actual flag rate, and re-running the analysis with four designs that never rely on a single adoption-date cutoff (a before/after split, a conservative difference-in-differences control group, an intensity-based specification, and a rank-based measure), they find a positive productivity association survives every specification, while the same tests applied to pre-ChatGPT placebo data show no effect. The result matters for the debate over whether generative AI is genuinely boosting researcher output: it confirms the timing artifact is real but shows it is far too small to account for the observed productivity gain, strengthening the case that LLM adoption and higher scientific output are causally linked rather than a measurement mirage.
