# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-08-06

## 1. Does generative AI narrow education-based productivity gaps? Evidence from a randomized experiment [Inequality]

**Authors:** Guillermo Cruces, Diego Fernandez Meijide, Sebastian Galiani, Ramiro Galvez, Maria Lombardi
**Date:** Submitted August 4, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04198
**Summary:** In a randomized online experiment with 1,174 adults who completed a workplace-style problem-solving task with or without a generative AI assistant, the authors find AI raises performance for everyone but disproportionately helps less-educated participants, cutting the education-based performance gap by roughly three-quarters. Chat logs show lower-education participants lean on the AI more heavily while higher-education participants use it more effectively, yet a follow-up module without AI access shows the gains are not pure delegation: treated participants do not do worse once AI is removed, though part of the narrowed gap re-emerges. The results suggest generative AI can compress skill-based productivity differences in task execution even as underlying human-capital gaps continue to shape unassisted performance and how effectively workers exploit the tool.
