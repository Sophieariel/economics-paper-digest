# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-08-14

## 1. The Impact of Generative AI on Collaborative Open-Source Software Development: Evidence from GitHub Copilot [Firm Adoption]

**Authors:** Fangchen Song, Ashish Agarwal, Wen Wen
**Date:** Submitted October 2, 2024; revised August 13, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02091
**Summary:** Using GitHub's proprietary Copilot usage telemetry linked to public open-source project data, the authors find that Copilot adoption raises project-level code contributions by 5.9%, driven both by more developers participating and by larger individual contributions. The gains come at a cost: Copilot use is associated with an 8% increase in coordination time and more code discussion, as AI-assisted output requires more review and negotiation among distributed collaborators, though the net effect on timely merged contributions remains positive. The benefits are uneven — peripheral, less-established developers see smaller output gains and larger coordination costs than core maintainers, suggesting AI pair-programming tools may reshape rather than flatten the structure of open-source collaboration.

## 2. Applied and Filtered: An End-to-End Algorithmic Fairness Audit of A Public Employment Agency [Hiring]

**Authors:** Gemma Galdón-Clavell
**Date:** Submitted August 13, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13022
**Summary:** The author conducts an independent, end-to-end fairness audit of a public employment agency's algorithmic hiring pipeline, tracing roughly 497,000 candidate-vacancy records from Barcelona's public job-placement service through its third-party matching platform from 2017 to 2022. Aggregate shortlisting rates look gender-balanced, but this masks substantial disparities by salary tier, age, and gender identity — women are shortlisted less often for mid-salary roles, non-binary candidates at less than a third the rate of men, and workers 55 and older are almost entirely absent from the pipeline despite being a sizable share of the local labor force. The agency itself lacks visibility into how the vendor's matching algorithm works, illustrating how fairness outcomes in deployed hiring systems depend on the interaction of automated screening, human discretion, and vendor opacity — not just the properties of the underlying model.

## Excluded, borderline

- **From Social Coding to Agentic Coding** (arXiv 2608.03585) — LLM multi-agent simulation of an open-source community, not observed field data on real workers; previously excluded on the same grounds in the 2026-08-08 and 2026-08-09 digests.
- **Hiring Discrimination and the Task Content of Jobs** (arXiv 2604.01933) — large-scale resume audit with a strong causal design, but no AI/automation dimension in the treatment or outcomes.
- **Toward Measuring AI's Effects on Skill Formation: The Stock-Formation Gap** (arXiv 2605.16283) — proposes a research agenda and measurement framework rather than reporting empirical findings.
- **The Main Barrier to AI Adoption in the Public Sector Is Lack of Training** (arXiv 2606.01517) — single-organization before/after case study with self-reported, vendor-style metrics and no comparison group.
- **AI-Increased Talent Retention Strategies** (arXiv 2607.19733) — conceptual literature-review framework paper, no empirical research design.
- **AI-AI co-creation outperforms human pairs in creative tasks** (arXiv 2608.09023) — controlled creativity experiment, but no worker, wage, or labor-market outcome.
- **Detecting Soft Skills in ML Engineering Roles CVs** (arXiv 2608.10046) — descriptive CV-mining study of candidate self-presentation, not a labor-market outcome.
- **Translating AI into scientific impact** (arXiv 2607.16780) — about scientists' career returns to AI use in research; better scoped to the ai-science digest.
- **Job Transformation, Specialization, and the Labor Market Effects of AI** (IZA DP 18565, Freund & Mann) — strong task-exposure/wage design, but posted April 2026, outside the 30-day window.
- **Generative AI and the Reorganization of Labor Demand** (arXiv 2605.23159) — strong firm-level hiring-reallocation design, but submitted May 22, 2026, outside the 30-day window.
- **Labor Demand in the Age of Generative AI** (World Bank Policy Research WP 11263, Liu, Wang & Yu) — large natural-experiment design on 285 million job postings, but dated 2025, outside the 30-day window.
