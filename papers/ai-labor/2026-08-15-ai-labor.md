# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-08-15

## 1. Applied and Filtered: An End-to-End Algorithmic Fairness Audit of A Public Employment Agency [Hiring]

**Authors:** Gemma Galdón-Clavell
**Date:** Submitted August 13, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13022
**Summary:** The author conducts what is described as the first independent, pipeline-wide fairness audit of a semi-automated public hiring system, tracing roughly 497,000 candidate-vacancy records from Barcelona's public employment agency (2017–2022) through all seven stages from algorithmic matching to final employer decision. Aggregate callback parity by binary gender masks sizable disparities underneath: women are shortlisted at only about three-quarters the rate of comparable men in mid-salary roles, non-binary candidates are shortlisted at under a third the rate of men, and workers 55 and older are almost entirely absent from the pipeline despite being over 15% of the local labor force. The audit shows that sociotechnical, stage-by-stage auditing catches disparities that model-only fairness checks miss, and exposes a vendor-deployer information gap in which the public agency cannot see how its algorithmic vendor's matching logic actually works.

## 2. The Impact of Generative AI on Collaborative Open-Source Software Development: Evidence from GitHub Copilot [Firm Adoption]

**Authors:** Fangchen Song, Ashish Agarwal, Wen Wen
**Date:** Submitted October 2, 2024; revised August 13, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02091
**Summary:** Combining GitHub's proprietary Copilot usage data with public open-source project records, the authors study how the AI pair-programmer changed collaborative software development. Copilot use raised project-level code contributions by 5.9% and individual contributions by 2.1%, but also raised coordination time by 8% as more code drew discussion before merging — a genuine productivity-coordination trade-off that still nets out to more code merged overall. The gains were uneven: peripheral contributors saw smaller output increases and larger coordination costs than core, already-established developers, suggesting Copilot's benefits concentrate among developers already embedded in a project rather than lowering the bar for newcomers.
