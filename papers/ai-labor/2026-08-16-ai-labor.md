# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-08-16

## 1. Applied and Filtered: An End-to-End Algorithmic Fairness Audit of A Public Employment Agency [Hiring]

**Authors:** Gemma Galdón-Clavell
**Date:** Submitted August 13, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13022
**Summary:** Using roughly 497,000 candidate-vacancy records from Barcelona Activa's AI-assisted hiring platform between 2017 and 2022, the author traces algorithmic and human decisions across all seven stages of the shortlisting pipeline rather than auditing the model in isolation. Overall gender parity masks large disparities beneath the surface: women face adverse shortlisting impact in mid-salary roles and at ages 46-55, non-binary candidates are shortlisted at less than a third the rate of men, and workers 55 and older are essentially absent from the pipeline despite being 15.6% of the local workforce. The audit also documents a vendor-deployer information gap, with the public agency unable to see how its third-party matching vendor scores candidates, underscoring that fairness in deployed hiring AI depends on organizational and contractual context, not just the model.

## 2. From Social Coding to Agentic Coding: Productivity and Relational Reconfiguration in Open-Source Communities [Firm Adoption]

**Authors:** Mengying Zhou, Yongjie Yin, Yang Chen
**Date:** Submitted August 4, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03585
**Summary:** The authors simulate an open-source software community — an LLM-based multi-agent model seeded with real GitHub data from 1,084 developers — and compare four-week trajectories with and without access to generative coding agents. Agent access raises completed tasks by 39% and cuts median completion time from 45 to 20 minutes, but adoption reaches only about a quarter of developers and gains concentrate among those already most active and well-connected, while direct human-to-human interaction falls from 32% to 12% of exchanges. The knowledge left behind for future contributors also degrades — the AI-assisted era's public discussion corpus covers only 22% of later tasks' informational needs versus 81% for the human-only era — pointing to a productivity/public-knowledge tradeoff as coding work shifts into private human-agent loops.
