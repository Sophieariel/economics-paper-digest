# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-08-17

## 1. The Impact of Generative AI on Collaborative Open-Source Software Development: Evidence from GitHub Copilot [Firm Adoption]

**Authors:** Fangchen Song, Ashish Agarwal, Wen Wen
**Date:** Submitted October 2, 2024; revised August 13, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02091
**Summary:** Using GitHub's proprietary Copilot usage telemetry linked to public open-source project data, the authors estimate how an AI pair-programmer changes distributed, voluntary collaboration. Copilot use raises project-level code contributions by 5.9% and broadens who contributes, but it also raises coordination time by 8% as more code discussions are needed to integrate the added output, with peripheral (non-core) developers seeing smaller contribution gains and proportionally larger coordination costs than core developers. The net effect on merged contributions is still positive, suggesting generative AI can expand participation in open digital public goods even as it complicates the coordination work needed to fold that participation into shared output.

## 2. Applied and Filtered: An End-to-End Algorithmic Fairness Audit of A Public Employment Agency [Hiring]

**Authors:** Gemma Galdón-Clavell
**Date:** Submitted August 13, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13022
**Summary:** This is an independent, end-to-end fairness audit of roughly 497,000 candidate-vacancy pipeline records from Barcelona Activa, a public employment agency whose semi-automated hiring system runs on the third-party TalentClue platform. Aggregate gender parity in outcomes masks large disparities within specific pipeline stages — women face lower odds of mid-salary shortlisting, the gap is widest for women aged 46-55, non-binary candidates are shortlisted at under a third the rate of men, and workers 55 and older are almost entirely absent from the pipeline despite being 15.6% of Barcelona's labor force. The audit also documents a vendor-deployer information gap, with the public agency itself lacking visibility into how the vendor's matching algorithm works, underscoring that fairness in deployed algorithmic hiring depends on organizational and procurement context and not just the underlying model.

## Excluded, borderline

- **From Social Coding to Agentic Coding: Productivity and Relational Reconfiguration in Open-Source Communities** (arXiv 2608.03585) — LLM multi-agent simulation of an open-source community, not observed field data on real workers; consistent with the same exclusion in the 2026-08-08/09 digests.
- **AI-AI co-creation outperforms human pairs in creative tasks** (arXiv 2608.09023) — genuine controlled experiment, but the outcome is judge-rated idea quality in a lab task, not a worker or labor-market outcome (wages, hiring, employment).
- **Toward Measuring AI's Effects on Skill Formation: The Stock-Formation Gap** (arXiv 2605.16283) — conceptual measurement-agenda paper; explicitly states it does not show AI has affected skill formation, only proposes how to study it.
- **The Main Barrier to AI Adoption in the Public Sector Is Lack of Training** (arXiv 2606.01517) — single-author, two-office before/after case study with self-reported statistics and no comparison group; repeat exclusion from 2026-08-08/09.
- **Hiring Discrimination and the Task Content of Jobs** (arXiv 2604.01933) — rigorous large-scale resume-audit causal design, but no AI/automation dimension in treatment or outcomes; repeat exclusion from 2026-08-08/09.
- **AI-Increased Talent Retention Strategies** (arXiv 2607.19733) — literature-review framework paper, no empirical research design; repeat exclusion from 2026-08-08/09.
- **Skill Substitution, Expectations, and the Business Cycle** (arXiv 2602.02483) — strong causal design on German administrative data, but studies business-cycle effects on college enrollment with no AI dimension.
- **Cost-of-Ethics Crisis: Job Searches of Computer Science Students** (arXiv 2605.09680) — real survey/interview data on job search, but the subject is ethics-education efficacy, not AI's effect on labor-market outcomes.
