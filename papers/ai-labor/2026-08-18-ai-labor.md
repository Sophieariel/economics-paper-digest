# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-08-18

## 1. The Impact of Generative AI on Collaborative Open-Source Software Development: Evidence from GitHub Copilot [Firm Adoption]

**Authors:** Fangchen Song, Ashish Agarwal, Wen Wen
**Date:** Submitted October 2, 2024; revised August 13, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02091
**Summary:** Using GitHub's proprietary Copilot usage logs matched to public open-source project data, the authors find that Copilot adoption raises project-level code contributions by 5.9%, driven by both more contributors participating and each contributing more individually. The AI assistant also slows coordination — adding roughly 8% more time to merge decisions and generating more code discussion — yet the net effect on timely merges stays positive, with peripheral developers gaining less in output while bearing larger coordination costs than core maintainers. The findings suggest generative AI pair programmers reshape open-source collaboration by lowering the bar to contribute while straining the review and coordination processes that hold distributed teams together.

## 2. From Social Coding to Agentic Coding: Productivity and Relational Reconfiguration in Open-Source Communities [Firm Adoption]

**Authors:** Mengying Zhou, Yongjie Yin, Yang Chen
**Date:** Submitted August 4, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03585
**Summary:** The authors build an LLM-based multi-agent simulation seeded with real GitHub activity from over 1,000 developers, then branch identical community states into parallel four-week runs with and without coding agents to isolate the technology's effect. Introducing coding agents raises completed tasks by 39% and cuts median completion time by more than half, but adoption concentrates among already-active, well-connected developers, and direct human-to-human interaction falls from 32% to 12% of task activity as work shifts into agent-assisted or agent-mediated loops. The knowledge these agent-assisted workflows leave behind is markedly less reusable for future contributors than human-generated records, pointing to a tension between short-run productivity gains and the erosion of the public knowledge base that sustains open-source communities.

## 3. Applied and Filtered: An End-to-End Algorithmic Fairness Audit of A Public Employment Agency [Hiring]

**Authors:** Gemma Galdón-Clavell
**Date:** Submitted August 13, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13022
**Summary:** This is an independent end-to-end audit of a semi-automated hiring pipeline run by Barcelona's public employment agency, tracing roughly 497,000 candidate-vacancy records from 2017 to 2022 through automated screening, human discretion, and employer decisions. Aggregate parity between men and women masks large disparities once the sample is cut by salary level and age: women face significantly lower shortlisting rates for mid-salary roles, candidates 55 and older are essentially absent from the pipeline despite comprising 16% of the local labor force, and non-binary candidates are shortlisted at less than a third the rate of men. The audit also documents a vendor-deployer information gap — the public agency lacks visibility into the matching logic of the third-party algorithmic vendor it relies on — illustrating how fairness in deployed hiring algorithms depends on organizational context, not just model design.
