# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-08-19

## 1. How Generative AI Adoption Alters the Demand for Cognitive and Social Skills Within Roles: A Skill-Centric Analysis [Skills & Tasks]

**Authors:** Piyush Gulati, Arianna Marchetti, Victoria Sevcenko, Phanish Puranam
**Date:** first posted 2025-03-12; revised 2026-08-18
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09212
**Summary:** The authors analyze seven million U.S. job postings from 595 public firms that adopted generative AI in 2022–2024, using difference-in-differences models to compare GenAI-adopting and non-adopting roles around ChatGPT's launch. Contrary to the common view that AI automation elevates the relative importance of social skills, they find social-skill demand in GenAI-adopting roles actually fell by 3.4% while cognitive-skill demand held steady, driven by roles outside a function absorbing that function's administrative tasks. The pattern is concentrated in non-managerial jobs and points toward GenAI enabling self-service task consolidation rather than intensified cross-functional coordination, complicating simple narratives about how AI reshapes job skill requirements.

## 2. The Impact of Generative AI on Collaborative Open-Source Software Development: Evidence from GitHub Copilot [Firm Adoption]

**Authors:** Fangchen Song, Ashish Agarwal, Wen Wen
**Date:** first posted 2024-10-02; revised 2026-08-13
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02091
**Summary:** Using GitHub's proprietary Copilot usage data linked to public open-source project records, the authors measure how an AI pair programmer affects distributed, voluntary software collaboration. Copilot use raises project-level code contributions by 5.9% and individual contributions by 2.1%, but also increases coordination time by 8% and generates more code discussion, with peripheral developers seeing smaller contribution gains and larger coordination costs than core developers; the net effect on timely code merges remains positive. The findings show generative AI tools reshape not just individual output but the structure of collaborative knowledge work, with unequal effects across contributors' roles in the community.

## 3. Applied and Filtered: An End-to-End Algorithmic Fairness Audit of A Public Employment Agency [Hiring]

**Authors:** Gemma Galdón-Clavell
**Date:** first posted 2026-08-13
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13022
**Summary:** The author conducts what she describes as the first independent, end-to-end fairness audit of a semi-automated public hiring system, analyzing roughly 497,000 candidate-vacancy pipeline entries from 2017–2022 at Barcelona Activa, a public employment agency using the third-party TalentClue platform across seven stages from automated processing to employer decisions. Aggregate parity between binary genders masks substantial disparities by salary level, age, and gender identity: women face adverse impact in mid-salary shortlisting, non-binary candidates are shortlisted at less than a third the rate of men, and candidates 55 and older are essentially absent from the pipeline despite being 15.6% of the local labor force, while a vendor-deployer information asymmetry leaves the agency unable to fully inspect the platform's matching logic. The audit demonstrates that fairness properties emerging from the interaction of automation, human discretion, and vendor opacity in deployed hiring systems can be invisible to model-level checks alone.

## 4. Stranded credentials: how a skill-signaling market absorbed generative AI [Skills & Tasks]

**Authors:** Song Yao
**Date:** first posted 2026-08-17
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17111
**Summary:** The author audits the 2010–2026 archive of Kaggle, the largest data-science competition platform, across 444,698 participations in two concurrent competition formats, to test whether competition-medal credentials still predict future performance after generative AI diffused through the field. Freshly earned medals retained most of their predictive power through the AI transition in both formats, but the stock of medals from one format lost 82% of its informativeness — mostly because that format had already been exiting the market for reasons predating AI, not because AI itself devalued the underlying skill. The results suggest credentialing institutions' value under AI is perishable and institution-specific rather than uniformly eroded, a distinction relevant to how labor-market signals like degrees and certifications may hold up as AI diffuses more broadly.
