# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-08-20

## 1. How Generative AI Adoption Alters the Demand for Cognitive and Social Skills Within Roles: A Skill-Centric Analysis [Skills & Tasks]

**Authors:** Piyush Gulati, Arianna Marchetti, Victoria Sevcenko, Phanish Puranam
**Date:** Submitted March 12, 2025; revised August 18, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09212
**Summary:** Using seven million job postings from 595 U.S. public firms that adopted generative AI between 2022 and 2024, the authors run difference-in-differences models comparing GenAI-adopting roles to non-adopting roles around ChatGPT's launch. Contrary to the common expectation that AI would push work toward social skills as it automates cognitive tasks, they find the opposite: relative demand for social skills in GenAI-adopting roles fell by 3.4%, while cognitive-skill demand held steady, driven by non-managerial roles absorbing new administrative tasks. The pattern looks more like GenAI enabling self-service than deeper cross-functional coordination, complicating simple automation-versus-augmentation narratives about how AI reshapes job content.

## 2. The Impact of Generative AI on Collaborative Open-Source Software Development: Evidence from GitHub Copilot [Firm Adoption]

**Authors:** Fangchen Song, Ashish Agarwal, Wen Wen
**Date:** Submitted October 2, 2024; revised August 13, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02091
**Summary:** Combining GitHub's proprietary Copilot usage logs with public OSS project data, the authors show Copilot adoption raises project-level code contributions by 5.9%, driven by both more developers participating and each contributing more. The gains come with a cost, though: Copilot use is linked to an 8% rise in coordination time and more code discussion, with peripheral (non-core) developers seeing smaller contribution gains and larger coordination costs than core developers. Despite the added coordination burden, the net effect on timely code merges stays positive, suggesting AI pair-programmers reshape OSS collaboration structure even as they lift aggregate output.

## 3. Stranded credentials: how a skill-signaling market absorbed generative AI [Skills & Tasks]

**Author:** Song Yao
**Date:** Submitted August 17, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17111
**Summary:** Auditing 444,698 participations from Kaggle's 2010–2026 competition archive, the author tests whether medals earned in data-science contests still predict future performance now that generative AI can do much of the underlying work. Freshly earned medals retain most of their predictive power through the AI transition in both scoring formats, but older medals from a competition format that had already been winding down for reasons unrelated to AI lost most of their signaling value — an institutional "stranding" rather than an AI-driven one. The findings suggest credential value depends on continual replenishment by an active institution rather than eroding automatically once AI can perform the credentialed skill, with real stakes for how labor markets design and maintain signaling systems.

## 4. Applied and Filtered: An End-to-End Algorithmic Fairness Audit of A Public Employment Agency [Hiring]

**Author:** Gemma Galdón-Clavell
**Date:** Submitted August 13, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13022
**Summary:** The author audits roughly 497,000 candidate-vacancy records from Barcelona Activa, a public employment agency that uses a third-party AI platform for candidate search and shortlisting, tracing outcomes across all seven pipeline stages rather than just the algorithm itself. Aggregate parity between men and women masks real disparities: women face adverse shortlisting impact at mid-salary levels, non-binary candidates are shortlisted at under a third the rate of men, and candidates 55 and older are essentially absent from the pipeline despite being over 15% of the local labor force, though the gender gap narrowed from 2017 to 2022. A key structural finding is a vendor-deployer information gap — the employment agency itself lacks visibility into the vendor's matching logic — showing that fairness in deployed hiring AI depends on organizational and vendor context, not just the model.

## 5. CentaurBench: Benchmarking LLM Capabilities on Augmenting vs. Automating Real-World Work Tasks [Skills & Tasks]

**Authors:** Pattaraphon Kenny Wongchamcharoen, Kris Gulati, Min Min Fong, Abhishek Nagaraj
**Date:** Submitted August 19, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18554
**Summary:** The authors build a benchmark that scores AI models not just on producing work output directly (automation) but on how much they improve a weaker agent's output when acting as an assistant (augmentation), across seven economically grounded real-world tasks. They find the two rankings are only modestly correlated — the model that wins at automation loses at augmentation on five of seven tasks — and assistance is often not even helpful: the unaided worker outperforms every assisted condition on three tasks, with only one model's guidance beating no guidance on average. The result cautions against treating automation benchmarks as a stand-in for how much value AI adds when working alongside people, which matters directly for predicting AI's effect on task-level productivity.
