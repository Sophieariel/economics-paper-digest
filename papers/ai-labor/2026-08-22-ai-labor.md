# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-08-22

## 1. Bye-bye, Bluebook? Automating Legal Drudgery With AI-Augmented Rule Following [Skills & Tasks]

**Authors:** Matthew Dahl, Eric Martínez
**Date:** first posted 2025-05-05; revised 2026-08-16
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02763
**Summary:** Dahl and Martínez build a new benchmark of 2,058 legal-citation-formatting queries under the Bluebook — a ubiquitous, rule-bound task considered classic legal "drudgery" — and separately have a top "reasoning" model compete against human editors from five leading law reviews on the same skill. Frontier models produce a fully compliant citation only 42.6% of the time zero-shot, simply supplying the rules as text helps only modestly, and even the best model falls well short of the human editors' scores. A neuro-symbolic pipeline that uses a model only to parse citations into structured elements and delegates formatting to a deterministic rule engine raises accuracy by 32.4 points to 85.5%, suggesting near-term automation of even highly formulaic professional tasks depends on combining language models with symbolic systems rather than on model capability alone.

## 2. The Value of Prompting Skills [Skills & Tasks]

**Authors:** Lara Fleck, Dominik Becker, Marie-Christine Fregin, Andries de Grip, Harald Pfeifer, Kathrin Weis
**Date:** first posted 2026-08 (IZA Discussion Paper No. 18871; exact day not stated by source)
**Source:** https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/18871/the-value-of-prompting-skills
**Summary:** Using a discrete choice experiment, the authors ask real hiring decision-makers at German firms across all sectors to choose between job applicants whose profiles vary in prompting skills, occupation-specific skill gaps, social skills, gender, and salary expectations. Prompting skills raise an applicant's hiring probability by 4% on average and employers are willing to pay about 2% above a comparable skilled worker's salary for them — a modest premium next to the value of matching occupation-specific skills or strong social skills — but the premium roughly doubles to 9% at large firms and at firms that have adopted or plan to adopt AI, and prompting skills can partly substitute for weak-to-moderate social skills. Because higher prompting skills do not compensate for occupation-specific skill gaps, the authors read the pattern as AI adoption raising the general skill bar in jobs rather than simply reallocating value away from other skills.

## Excluded, borderline

- **Toward Measuring AI's Effects on Skill Formation: The Stock-Formation Gap** (arXiv 2605.16283) — conceptual measurement-agenda paper; explicitly states it does not show AI has affected skill formation, only proposes how to study it.
- **AI-AI co-creation outperforms human pairs in creative tasks** (arXiv 2608.09023) — genuine controlled experiment, but the outcome is judge-rated idea quality in a lab task, not a worker or labor-market outcome.
- **A Conceptual Framework for Enhancing Workforce Readiness for Smart Manufacturing in the AI Era** (arXiv 2608.11540) — proposes a competency-stage framework with no empirical research design or data.
- **Detecting Soft Skills in ML Engineering Roles CVs** (arXiv 2608.10046) — NLP method paper for mining CV text; no labor-market outcome or causal design.
- **The Accuracy Trap: Structural Scarcity Amplifies Relative Inequality in Algorithmic Allocation** (arXiv 2608.11491) — rigorous algorithmic-rationing analysis, but the applications are child welfare and cancer-treatment referral, not a labor-market setting.
- **Hiring Discrimination and the Task Content of Jobs** (arXiv 2604.01933) — rigorous large-scale resume-audit causal design, but no AI/automation dimension in treatment or outcomes.
- **Targeting Support Using Job Seekers' Biases / Biases-Informed Job Search Guidance** (arXiv 2608.16849, 2608.16827) — real RCT and structural modeling on job-search assistance targeting, but no AI/automation dimension.
- **Judicious Use of AI Can Help Vietnam Become a High-income Country** (IZA DP 18867) — speculative macro growth-policy essay on national AI strategy, not a worker- or firm-level empirical finding.
- **Financial Technologies, Labor Markets, and Wage Inequality: Evidence from Instant Payment Systems** (arXiv 2608.13871) — rigorous triple-difference design on wage inequality, but the technology studied (Brazil's Pix instant-payment system) is not AI.
