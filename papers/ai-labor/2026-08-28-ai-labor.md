# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-08-28

## 1. Small Changes, Big Impact: Demographic Bias in LLM-Based Hiring Through Subtle Sociocultural Markers in Anonymised Resumes [Hiring]

**Authors:** Bryan Chen Zhengyu Tan, Shaun Khoo, Bich Ngoc Doan, Zhengyuan Liu, Nancy F. Chen, Roy Ka-Wei Lee
**Date:** Submitted March 5, 2026; revised August 27, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05189
**Summary:** The authors build a stress-test for resume-screening LLMs in the Singapore context, expanding 100 neutral resumes into 4,100 variants that differ only in job-irrelevant sociocultural markers such as languages, hobbies, and volunteering, then evaluate 18 LLMs in head-to-head and shortlisting settings. Even with explicit demographic identifiers redacted, the models infer ethnicity and gender from these markers with high accuracy and systematically favor candidates coded as Chinese and Caucasian males, with language cues driving ethnicity inference and hobbies or activities driving gender inference. Prompting the models to explain their reasoning does not reduce the bias and can even amplify it, showing that anonymization alone cannot keep proxy discrimination out of automated hiring pipelines.

## 2. The Pulse Beneath the Job Title: Monthly Readings of Requirements and Tasks from 750 Million Chinese Job Ads [Skills & Tasks]

**Authors:** Qin Chen, Ying Fang, Xiangyu Wang, Leo Yang Yang
**Date:** Submitted August 27, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26924
**Summary:** Using 752.6 million job postings from China's five largest recruitment platforms between 2022 and 2026, the authors build two continuously updated catalogs — roughly 20,700 candidate requirements and 44,500 job tasks — that separate what employers ask of applicants from what the job itself entails, a distinction that occupation titles and existing skill taxonomies blur together. Because each task is scored for how readily a language model could perform it, the catalogs track AI exposure at the task level rather than the occupation level, and the authors show this distinction matters: occupation-level counts suggest the most LLM-exposed work is disappearing, while task-level counts show a much smaller decline once the shifting mix of tasks within an occupation is accounted for. The approach offers a higher-frequency, more granular alternative to slow-moving official occupational directories for tracking how AI reshapes labor demand as it happens.

## Excluded, borderline

- **Targeting Support Using Job Seekers' Biases: A Randomized Experiment** (arXiv 2608.16849) and its companion **Biases-Informed Job Search Guidance** (arXiv 2608.16827) — rigorous RCT and structural analysis of French job-search assistance, but no AI/algorithmic dimension in treatment or outcomes.
- **Toward Measuring AI's Effects on Skill Formation: The Stock-Formation Gap** (arXiv 2605.16283) — directly on-topic but a measurement-agenda paper; explicitly states it does not show AI has affected skill formation, only proposes how to study it.
- **AI-AI co-creation outperforms human pairs in creative tasks** (arXiv 2608.09023) — genuine controlled experiment, but the outcome is judge-rated idea quality in a lab task, not a worker or labor-market outcome.
- **A Conceptual Framework for Enhancing Workforce Readiness for Smart Manufacturing in the AI Era** (arXiv 2608.11540) — proposes a competency-stage framework with no empirical research design.
- **Financial Technologies, Labor Markets, and Wage Inequality: Evidence from Instant Payment Systems** (arXiv 2608.13871) — strong triple-difference causal design on Brazil's Pix rollout, but no AI/automation dimension.
- **The Accuracy Trap: Structural Scarcity Amplifies Relative Inequality in Algorithmic Allocation** (arXiv 2608.11491) — algorithmic-fairness scaling law, but framed around public-resource rationing (child welfare, cancer treatment), not employment or hiring.
- **The Traffickers' Pitch: Detecting Deceptive Recruitment in Online Job Boards** (arXiv 2605.25416) — recruitment-adjacent detection system, but the contribution is a fraud classifier, not a labor-market or worker outcome.
- **Building and Governing AI Systems: Advancing Social Workers' Roles** (arXiv 2608.04273) — vision paper on a profession's role in AI governance, no empirical research design.
