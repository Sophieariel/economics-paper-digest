# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-08-31

## 1. Small Changes, Big Impact: Demographic Bias in LLM-Based Hiring Through Subtle Sociocultural Markers in Anonymised Resumes [Hiring]

**Authors:** Bryan Chen Zhengyu Tan, Shaun Khoo, Bich Ngoc Doan, Zhengyuan Liu, Nancy F. Chen, Roy Ka-Wei Lee
**Date:** Submitted March 5, 2026; revised August 27, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05189
**Summary:** The authors build a stress-test framework for LLM-based resume screening, generating 4,100 resume variants from 100 neutral, job-aligned templates that vary only in job-irrelevant sociocultural markers (languages, hobbies, volunteering) across four ethnicities and two genders, then evaluate 18 LLMs in head-to-head comparison and shortlisting tasks. Even with explicit identifiers redacted, models infer ethnicity and gender from these markers with high accuracy and systematically favor candidates coded as Chinese and Caucasian males, and prompting models to explain their reasoning can paradoxically amplify the bias. The findings show that anonymisation alone does not protect automated hiring pipelines from demographic discrimination, since innocuous-seeming resume content can act as a reliable proxy for protected characteristics.

## 2. The Pulse Beneath the Job Title: Monthly Readings of Requirements and Tasks from 750 Million Chinese Job Ads [Skills & Tasks]

**Authors:** Qin Chen, Ying Fang, Xiangyu Wang, Leo Yang Yang
**Date:** Submitted August 27, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26924
**Summary:** Using 752.6 million job ads from China's five leading recruitment platforms (2022–2026), the authors extract and standardize the actual phrases employers use to build two catalogs — 20,721 candidate requirements and 44,479 job tasks — updated monthly, and score each task for how exposed it is to large language models. Separating requirements from tasks reveals that occupational titles conceal real heterogeneity, such as a single "accountant" title masking a wage staircase tied to certification level, and that counting occupations versus counting tasks gives sharply different pictures of AI exposure: occupation-level counts suggest the most LLM-exposed work is disappearing, while task-level counts show far less of it actually is. The dataset offers a higher-frequency, less noisy alternative to static occupational directories like O*NET for tracking how AI is reshaping labor demand in real time.

- **Toward Measuring AI's Effects on Skill Formation: The Stock–Formation Gap** (arXiv:2605.16283) — a measurement-agenda paper proposing a future research program; explicitly does not report an empirical finding on skill formation.
- **A Conceptual Framework for Enhancing Workforce Readiness for Smart Manufacturing in the AI Era** (arXiv:2608.11540) — a conceptual readiness-index framework validated on one university teaching lab's capstone projects; no causal or market-level design.
- **The Accuracy Trap: Structural Scarcity Amplifies Relative Inequality in Algorithmic Allocation** (arXiv:2608.11491) — concerns algorithmic rationing of child-welfare and cancer-care resources, not employment or labor-market outcomes.
- **Targeting Support Using Job Seekers' Biases: A Randomized Experiment** and **Biases-Informed Job Search Guidance** (arXiv:2608.16849, arXiv:2608.16827) — strong field-experiment labor economics (French job-search RCTs) but no AI or automation angle, out of this digest's scope.
- **Financial Technologies, Labor Markets, and Wage Inequality: Evidence from Instant Payment Systems** (arXiv:2608.13871) — rigorous fintech labor-market study (Brazil's Pix), but concerns payment technology rather than AI.
- **Does the Gender Wage Gap Originate at Labor Market Entry? Evidence from South Korea** (arXiv:2608.03153) — solid labor economics, no AI/automation angle.
- **Rebuilding Startups: An Empirical Study on Remote Work and Skill Complementarity** (arXiv:2608.02666) — remote-work hiring study with no AI angle.
- **The Value of a Chance: Task Concentration and Talent Discovery in Team Production** (arXiv:2511.07218) — natural-experiment labor economics (MLB injuries), no AI/automation angle.
