# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-09-01

## 1. Performance Manipulation: Labor Market Implications in AI-assisted Era [Hiring]

**Authors:** Xiaoyun Qiu, Yang Yu, Haifeng Xu
**Date:** Submitted April 24, 2026; revised August 29, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22230
**Summary:** The authors build a game-theoretic model in which workers competing for employer evaluation split effort between scarce, expertise-driven "creative" work and routine "mechanistic" work that AI can heavily augment, then show that performance-based screening stays informative only as long as evaluations retain enough of a creative component, collapsing into an uninformative pooling equilibrium once AI grows capable enough to substitute for creative effort. They find this over-investment in AI-augmentable busywork is driven systematically by low-ability workers rather than high-ability ones, and that sharpening the skew of reward structures can counteract the problem by pushing workers back toward genuine creative effort. Using a novel LLM-based method to measure both effort types in nearly 1,500 real Kaggle competition submissions, they find empirical support for the model's predictions, suggesting AI-assisted "performance manipulation" is already eroding the signal value of competitive screening in labor markets.

- **Generalized AKM: Flexible Controls and Interactions in Wage Decompositions** (arXiv:2608.09686) — strong econometric method for wage decomposition using Portuguese employer-employee data, but a pure methods paper with no AI angle.
- **Building and Governing AI Systems: Advancing Social Workers' Roles** (arXiv:2608.04273) — a conceptual/vision paper mapping technology-governance roles for social workers; no causal design or empirical labor-market finding.
- **Cost-of-Ethics Crisis: ... Job Searches of Computer Science Students** (arXiv:2605.09680) — survey of 129 CS students' job-search ethics decisions; about ethics education, not AI's effect on labor-market outcomes.
- **How does hazard exposure influence job choice?** (arXiv:2608.24811) — discrete-choice job-search model with salary/hazard/crime tradeoffs; no AI angle.
