# AI & Economics of Science Papers — 2026-08-02

## 1. SciSchema.org: A Multidisciplinary Collection of Schemas for Structured Scientific Process Descriptions [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Jennifer D'Souza, Sameer Sadruddin, Anisa Rula, Ana Bossler, Andrés Fullana, Enric Bas, Syed Ather, Defne Circi, Anlan Chen, L. Catherine Brinson, Alyssa Columbus, George Demetriou, Dongjun Jeong, Tarun Kumar, Frank Krüger, Sascha Genehr, Kai Budde-Sagert, Anamaria Leonescu, Francesco Lodola, Chiara Florindi, Gagana Balasubramanya Murthy, Samson Oluwapelumi Olagbile, Nazia Riasat, Yan Sha, Kevin Shen, Shaokai Yang
**Date:** July 30, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27955
**Summary:** The authors release SciSchema.org, a first collection of 16 expert-vetted schemas covering fields from biology to physics that standardize how scientific process details (inputs, materials, procedures, measurements) get described across papers. The schemas were built with a human-in-the-loop pipeline in which LLMs proposed candidate structures from papers and protocols, which domain experts then refined into final master schemas. By making process descriptions machine-readable, the resource is meant to support reproducibility checks, cross-study comparison, and automated extraction of scientific knowledge at scale.

## 2. "We'll have to see how it works": An interview study to understand collaborative practices in interdisciplinary artificial intelligence and healthcare research [Econ of Science]

**Authors:** Rafael Henkin, Elizabeth Remfry, Duncan J. Reynolds, Megan Clinch, Michael R. Barnes
**Date:** November 30, 2023 (v1); revised July 28, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18424
**Summary:** Drawing on 13 semi-structured interviews with participants in early-stage UK AI-in-healthcare research consortia, the authors examine how data scientists, clinicians, and patients navigate the friction of building AI systems together across disciplinary lines. They find that successful collaboration required constantly adapting both the tools used for sharing work and how findings were communicated depending on whether the audience was technical, clinical, or patient-facing. The study argues that introducing AI into interdisciplinary research adds distinct new coordination costs on top of the usual challenges of cross-disciplinary science.
