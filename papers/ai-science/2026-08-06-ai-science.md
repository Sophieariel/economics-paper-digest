# AI & Economics of Science Papers — 2026-08-06

## 1. AI-Assisted Peer Review Across Research Communities: From Reviewer AI Policies to LLM Review Quality [Peer Review & Publication]

**Authors:** Alexander M. Fichtl, Lukas Ellinger, Josefin Kelber, Kryštof Olík, Georg Groh
**Date:** August 4, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03581
**Summary:** The authors survey reviewer-facing AI policies at 111 leading AI/NLP conferences and medical journals, finding the two communities regulate AI-assisted review very differently, then benchmark AI-generated reviews from open-source and proprietary models against human reviews at ICLR 2026 and Nature Communications. They find current LLMs write fluent, detailed reviews but systematically skew positive, offer generic criticism, and ground their claims unevenly in the manuscript's actual evidence. Because aggregate quality scores alone can mask these weaknesses, the paper argues venues need multi-dimensional evaluation frameworks before leaning on AI review more heavily.

## 2. Adversarial Fast-Moving Real-World Domains as Test Beds for Benchmarking AI Scientist Capabilities [AI for Science]

**Authors:** William Bolton, Philip Torr
**Date:** August 4, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03569
**Summary:** To avoid the confounds of retrospective or synthetic benchmarks, the authors test AI models' scientific idea-generation against real, verifiable expert outputs in two fast-moving domains: Formula 1 car-design innovation for the 2026 season and Magic: The Gathering deck-building against actual Pro Tour results. Models produced plausible, on-trend ideas—the best F1 model matched only 10 of 40 real innovations, while the best MTG model's card choices correlated strongly with what top players actually adopted—but few outputs matched real expert solutions exactly. The authors conclude the main bottleneck for AI scientists is not generating ideas but filtering and prioritizing them into genuinely novel, coherent choices.

## 3. An Autonomous Scientific Knowledge Generation Framework for AI-Driven Scientific Discovery [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Dibakar Datta
**Date:** July 9, 2026 (v1); revised August 3, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09806
**Summary:** The paper proposes a pipeline that autonomously converts unstructured scientific literature into a structured, AI-ready knowledge base, combining ontology-guided literature retrieval, hybrid information extraction, semantic harmonization, and provenance-preserving validation in one workflow. As a proof of concept in electro-optic materials, the system retrieved and validated roughly 1,000 publications and fully processed a subset of eight, distilling them into 29 structured records harmonized into 7 canonical entries. The results are small-scale but demonstrate a domain-independent template for turning scientific papers into machine-usable knowledge for AI-driven discovery.
