# AI & Economics of Science Papers — 2026-08-07

## 1. War in the Abstract: The Rise and Consequences of Militarized Language in Scientific Communication [Peer Review & Publication]

**Authors:** Sovesh Mohapatra, David Lydon-Staley, Dani S. Bassett
**Date:** June 22, 2026 (v1); revised August 4, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23462
**Summary:** The authors quantify how often warfare vocabulary appears in 21.4 million scientific abstracts from OpenAlex and PubMed (2010–2025) and run a controlled experiment on how such language affects readers. They find militaristic terms rose roughly 30–48% over the period, tracking real-world conflict intensity and accelerating after 2019 and again in the LLM era, with the fastest growth in the Global South and in engineering and computer science. Their experiment shows framing results in war metaphors reduces readers' sense of credibility, willingness to fund the work, and policy support, suggesting the drift toward combative science writing may undercut the very persuasion it aims for.

## 2. BIP! Ranker: A Software Library for Citation-Based Impact Indicators on Large-Scale Graphs [Econ of Science]

**Authors:** Ilias Kanellos, Serafeim Chatzopoulos, Thanasis Vergoulis
**Date:** August 3, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02004
**Summary:** The authors introduce BIP! Ranker, an open-source, Spark-based library for computing several citation-based impact indicators—capturing overall influence, current popularity, early citation momentum, and field-relative performance—rather than collapsing impact to a single count. It is built to scale to citation graphs with billions of edges across hundreds of millions of publications, filling a gap in open tools for multidimensional impact measurement at the size of major scholarly databases. Better, more scalable impact metrics like these could sharpen how research productivity and influence get weighed in funding and hiring decisions.
