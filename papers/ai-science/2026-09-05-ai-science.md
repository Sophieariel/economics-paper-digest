# AI & Economics of Science Papers — 2026-09-05

## 1. A Visionary Look at Vibe Researching [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Yebo Feng, Yang Liu
**Date:** September 3, 2026 (v2, updated; first posted April 1, 2026)
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00945
**Summary:** The authors define "vibe researching," a paradigm in which human scientists set direction and exercise judgment while LLM-based agents carry out literature review, experiments, data analysis, and drafting, and they lay out the multi-agent architectures, memory, and tool-use patterns that make this possible. They catalog seven technical limitations of current systems and weigh the paradigm's likely benefits (faster iteration, lower barriers to entry) against risks (deskilling, unchecked errors propagating through automated pipelines). The paper is framed as a map for the research community rather than an evaluation, aiming to ground debate about responsible adoption of AI-driven research production in shared terminology.

## 2. Large-Scale Qualitative Research with AI: Infrastructure, Management and Operation of the Socioscope Data Pipeline [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Saadi Lahlou, Juan Pablo Caicedo, Shriya Sekhsaria, Valentine Fournand, Paulius Yamin, Helga Nowotny
**Date:** August 30, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29751
**Summary:** The authors document the Socioscope project's technical infrastructure for conducting large-scale qualitative research on the food system, describing how AI is used to make hundreds of open-ended multimedia field interviews analyzable at scale rather than relying on small, manually coded samples. The paper details the sampling frame, transcription/translation/curation pipeline, and provenance and consent rules built over three years, reporting costs and yield: 686 cases from 31 countries comprising 1,430 recording hours and 12.6 million transcript words. It matters as a concrete demonstration that AI can extend qualitative social science to a scale previously reserved for quantitative methods, while surfacing the operational and ethical costs of doing so.

## 3. LeMat-Synth: a multi-modal toolbox to curate broad synthesis procedure databases from scientific literature [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Magdalena Lederbauer, Siddharth Betala, Valerie Gentzke, Anamaria Leonescu, Amine Sehaba, Faris Flaifil, Ayush Jain, Alfonso Amayuelas, Nikhil Yelamarthy, Xiyao Li, Grégoire Germain, Stefano Ribes, Stefan P. Schmid, Alexandre Nozadze, Anna Kelmanson, Sudheesh Kumar Ethirajan, Mohd Zaki, Elton Pan, Georgia Channing, Connor W. Coley, Philippe Schwaller, Rocío Mercado, Alexandre Duval, Mathilde L. D. Franckel, Samuel P. Gleason
**Date:** August 30, 2026 (v2, updated; first posted October 28, 2025)
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26824
**Summary:** The team built an LLM- and vision-language-model-based toolbox that reads text and figures from 81,000 open-access materials science papers and structures the buried procedural knowledge into a standardized synthesis database, yielding 58,000 inorganic materials synthesis records across 35 methods. They validate extraction quality against expert annotations and an LLM-as-judge framework, then demonstrate reuse by linking synthesis conditions to catalytic performance and by cross-checking superconductivity transition temperatures reported in text versus figures. The work shows how AI can convert decades of unstructured experimental literature into a queryable resource, directly accelerating downstream materials discovery.

## 4. FOXDEN: FAIR Services for AI-Ready Scientific Datasets [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Valentin Kuznetsov, Werner M. Sun, Keara Soloway, Rolf Verberg, Katherine S. Shanks, Suchismita Sarker, David S. Butcher, Kelly E. Nygren
**Date:** September 2, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03105
**Summary:** The authors describe FOXDEN, cyberinfrastructure built at a synchrotron light source facility that attaches structured and unstructured metadata and provenance records to raw, reduced, and analyzed datasets so they can be published with DOIs and consumed by AI workflows. They report its deployment at two large user facilities as a shared foundation for future "agentic" scientific workflows that would query and act on this data automatically. The paper matters as an example of the unglamorous data-infrastructure investment that determines whether AI can actually be applied to experimental science at scale, rather than only to already-curated benchmark datasets.

## 5. SoniMet — A tool for sonifying and visualizing the performance of single researchers [Econ of Science]

**Authors:** Tim Waterfield, Lutz Bornmann
**Date:** August 31, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30274
**Summary:** The authors introduce SoniMet, a web tool that pulls an individual scientist's publication and citation history from OpenAlex and renders it as both a timeline and an audio track, mapping citation-impact indicators to pitch, volume, and echo strength. The premise is that sonification can surface temporal patterns in a researcher's career trajectory — bursts of impact, decline, recovery — that are harder to notice in conventional bibliometric charts. It is offered as a new instrument for research evaluation and career-productivity analysis, though the authors note it currently covers only individual profiles and lacks empirical validation of whether sonified metrics actually improve evaluators' judgments.
