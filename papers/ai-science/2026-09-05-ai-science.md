# AI & Economics of Science Papers — 2026-09-05

## 1. The Value of Peer Review and the Reward to Reputation [Peer Review & Publication]

**Authors:** Johan Fourie
**Date:** July 15, 2026 (updated August 31, 2026)
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13844
**Summary:** This theory paper models an editor's choice when submissions outpace reviewer time: reject the papers no one can referee, or screen them with a faster but less accurate tool such as an AI classifier, knowing readers cannot distinguish a screened paper from a fully reviewed one. The model shows which choice makes the "reviewed" stamp more valuable depends on how common good unreviewed work is, and derives the exact threshold where rejection stops paying off; it also shows that once authors can run the screening tool themselves, the stamp's value collapses to reflect what gaming the screen costs rather than what it actually detects. The result is a caution for journals leaning on AI-assisted screening: it can preserve or erode the credibility of publication depending on the underlying quality mix and how gameable the tool is.

## 2. Do Large Language Models Favour Any Research Topics? [Peer Review & Publication]

**Authors:** Mike Thelwall
**Date:** August 31, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00323
**Summary:** Testing two LLMs (GPT-OSS-120B and Gemma 3 27B) as quality scorers on 73,489 health and life sciences articles, the author compares which topics attract systematically higher or lower scores. Both models show topic-linked scoring patterns — one favors virus, gene, and cell research over surveys, patients, and students, while the two models disagree with each other, and scores also shift depending on whether the model sees the full text or just the title and abstract. Because it is unclear whether these patterns track real quality differences or model bias, the findings argue that any use of LLMs for research evaluation needs bias auditing before deployment.

## 3. A Visionary Look at Vibe Researching [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Yebo Feng, Yang Liu
**Date:** April 1, 2026 (updated September 3, 2026)
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00945
**Summary:** The authors name and define "vibe researching," a paradigm modeled on "vibe coding" in which a human sets direction and judgment while LLM-based agents handle literature review, experiments, analysis, and drafting. The paper lays out the technical architecture (multi-agent systems, memory, tool use, retrieval-augmented generation) behind current AI-for-research systems, identifies seven concrete technical limitations, and weighs the paradigm's likely benefits against risks such as eroded researcher skill and unchecked automation. It is offered as a shared map for the field to debate responsible adoption of AI research agents before the practice becomes widespread.

## 4. Large-Scale Qualitative Research with AI: Infrastructure, Management and Operation of the Socioscope Data Pipeline [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Saadi Lahlou, Juan Pablo Caicedo, Shriya Sekhsaria, Valentine Fournand, Paulius Yamin, Helga Nowotny
**Date:** August 30, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29751
**Summary:** This paper documents the Socioscope project's pipeline for AI-augmented analysis of large-scale qualitative field data on the global food system, covering 686 documented cases across 31 countries, roughly 1,430 hours of recordings, and 12.6 million transcript words gathered since 2023. It details the sampling design, interview and transcription workflow, provenance and quality-control rules, and the operational costs of running AI-assisted qualitative research at this scale. The authors report lessons and limitations so other teams can reuse or adapt the approach, offering a rare public account of what it actually costs to make AI-scale qualitative social science work.

## 5. LeMat-Synth: a multi-modal toolbox to curate broad synthesis procedure databases from scientific literature [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Magdalena Lederbauer, Siddharth Betala, Valerie Gentzke, Anamaria Leonescu, Amine Sehaba, Faris Flaifil, Ayush Jain, Alfonso Amayuelas, Nikhil Yelamarthy, Xiyao Li, Grégoire Germain, Stefano Ribes, Stefan P. Schmid, Alexandre Nozadze, Anna Kelmanson, Sudheesh Kumar Ethirajan, Mohd Zaki, Elton Pan, Georgia Channing, Connor W. Coley, Philippe Schwaller, Rocío Mercado, Alexandre Duval, Mathilde L. D. Franckel, Samuel P. Gleason
**Date:** October 28, 2025 (updated August 30, 2026)
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26824
**Summary:** The authors build LeMat-Synth Parser, an open-source LLM/VLM toolbox that extracts structured synthesis protocols and performance metrics from text and figures in materials science papers, and apply it to 81K open-access publications to produce a 58K-procedure dataset spanning 35 synthesis methods and 16 material classes. They validate extraction quality against expert annotation and an LLM-as-judge framework, then demonstrate reuse by linking synthesis conditions to catalytic performance and by cross-validating superconductivity transition temperatures reported across text and figures in 1,384 papers. The work shows how LLM-based extraction can turn decades of scattered procedural knowledge in the literature into a structured, reusable resource for downstream scientific research.
