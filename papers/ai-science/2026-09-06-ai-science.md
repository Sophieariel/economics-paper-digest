# AI & Economics of Science Papers — 2026-09-06

## 1. LeMat-Synth: a multi-modal toolbox to curate broad synthesis procedure databases from scientific literature [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Magdalena Lederbauer, Siddharth Betala, Valerie Gentzke, Anamaria Leonescu, Amine Sehaba, Faris Flaifil, Ayush Jain, Alfonso Amayuelas, Nikhil Yelamarthy, Xiyao Li, Grégoire Germain, Stefano Ribes, Stefan P. Schmid, Alexandre Nozadze, Anna Kelmanson, Sudheesh Kumar Ethirajan, Mohd Zaki, Elton Pan, Georgia Channing, Connor W. Coley, Philippe Schwaller, Rocío Mercado, Alexandre Duval, Mathilde L. D. Franckel, Samuel P. Gleason
**Date:** October 28, 2025 (v1); revised (v2) August 30, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26824
**Summary:** The authors build LeMat-Synth Parser, an open-source pipeline that uses LLMs and vision-language models to pull structured synthesis procedures and performance metrics out of both text and figures in materials-science papers, applying it to 81,000 open-access publications to produce a 58,000-procedure inorganic synthesis dataset spanning 35 methods and 16 material classes. They validate extraction quality against expert annotations and an LLM-as-judge framework, then demonstrate reuse in two downstream tasks: linking catalyst synthesis routes to thermocatalytic performance and cross-checking superconductivity transition temperatures reported across text and figures. The project shows how AI can turn decades of scattered, unstructured procedural knowledge into a reusable, machine-actionable resource for accelerating materials discovery.

## 2. Large-Scale Qualitative Research with AI: Infrastructure, Management and Operation of the Socioscope Data Pipeline [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Saadi Lahlou, Juan Pablo Caicedo, Shriya Sekhsaria, Valentine Fournand, Paulius Yamin, Helga Nowotny
**Date:** August 30, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29751
**Summary:** The authors document the Socioscope project's technical infrastructure for "large-scale qualitative research" — a pipeline that uses AI to make hundreds of open-ended, multimedia field interviews with food-system actors analyzable at a scale conventional qualitative methods cannot reach. The paper details the sampling frame, interview and transcription workflow, provenance and quality-control rules, and participant incentive structure that together produced 686 documented cases across 31 countries, comprising 1,430 hours of recordings and 12.6 million words of transcript over 2023–2026. By reporting costs, operational lessons, and limitations transparently, the authors aim to make this AI-augmented research infrastructure reusable by other teams attempting similarly large qualitative studies.

## 3. FOXDEN: FAIR Services for AI-Ready Scientific Datasets [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Valentin Kuznetsov, Werner M. Sun, Keara Soloway, Rolf Verberg, Katherine S. Shanks, Suchismita Sarker, David S. Butcher, Kelly E. Nygren
**Date:** September 2, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03105
**Summary:** The authors describe FOXDEN, a cyberinfrastructure system built at the Cornell High Energy Synchrotron Source that attaches rich, machine-readable metadata and provenance records to raw and processed experimental datasets and publishes them with DOIs so they meet FAIR standards. They report its deployment across two facilities — CHESS and the National High Magnetic Field Laboratory — as a shared foundation for annotating and exchanging scientific data. The paper frames this metadata layer as a prerequisite for future "agentic" scientific workflows, in which AI systems need well-described, discoverable data to operate directly on experimental science rather than just text.

## 4. A Visionary Look at Vibe Researching [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Yebo Feng, Yang Liu
**Date:** April 1, 2026 (v1); revised (v2) September 3, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00945
**Summary:** The authors coin "vibe researching" — by analogy to "vibe coding" — for an emerging mode of work in which human scientists set high-level direction and exercise critical judgment while LLM-based multi-agent systems handle literature review, experimentation, data analysis, and manuscript drafting. They lay out the methodology behind such systems (multi-agent architectures, memory, tool use, retrieval-augmented generation) and catalog a set of technical limitations, mapping each to a concrete open research direction. The paper weighs the paradigm's potential productivity benefits against risks from ceding more of the research process to automation, offering the community a shared starting point for debating responsible adoption.
