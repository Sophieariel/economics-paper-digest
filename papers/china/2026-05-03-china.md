## 1. AI Patents in the United States and China: Measurement, Organization, and Knowledge Flows

**Authors:** Hanming Fang, Xian Gu, Hanyin Yan, Wu Zhu
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** NBER Working Paper w35022
**URL:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35022

**Abstract:** The authors develop a machine-learning classifier (97.0% precision, 91.3% recall,
94.0% F1) to identify AI patents in USPTO data and transfer it to Chinese patent records.
Both the US and China experienced substantial growth in AI patenting from the mid-1970s through
2023 with converging subfield distributions. Structurally, American innovation concentrates
among established corporations and regional tech hubs, while China's is more dispersed across
universities and state-owned enterprises. AI-focused patents generate measurable stock-market
value in both countries. Citation-network analysis shows ongoing technological collaboration
rather than decoupling: Chinese inventors rely more heavily on American innovations than
vice versa.

## 2. The Chinese Current Account Imbalances: Puzzles, Patterns, and Possible Causes

**Authors:** Chang Ma, Shang-Jin Wei
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** NBER Working Paper w35056
**URL:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35056

**Abstract:** This paper examines China's persistent current-account surplus and its global
implications. The authors argue that industrial and trade policies often produce sector-level
imbalances but have limited effect on economy-wide imbalances. Instead, they identify
structural factors — demographic sex-ratio imbalances and unequal financing access between
state-owned and private firms — as the primary drivers. Short-term stimulus can temporarily
reduce the surplus through higher imports, but long-run resolution requires structural economic
reforms rather than temporary policy interventions.

## 3. China's Global Ownership

**Authors:** Jennie Bai, Luc Laeven, Yaojun Ke, Hong Ru
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** NBER Working Paper w35106
**URL:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35106

**Abstract:** Using a dataset of 161,773 firms across 159 countries (2012–2021), the authors
reconstruct multi-layered ownership chains to identify ultimate Chinese control — revealing
holdings far exceeding official FDI figures. Chinese-controlled foreign assets grew ~20%
annually, reaching $2.1 trillion (≈3% of global corporate assets) by 2021. State-owned
enterprises prioritise research-intensive and supply-chain-connected targets. Post-acquisition,
target firms increase capital investment and R&D spending, yet patent output stagnates and
profitability declines. Chinese parent firms, by contrast, accelerate patent grants following
developed-economy acquisitions ("spillback" effect). Chinese investment crowds out R&D at
competing non-target firms, though overall industry innovation persists. The paper
characterises China's approach as "state-driven" ownership prioritising long-term domestic
technological capacity over near-term financial returns.

## 4. Where Has All the Dynamism Gone? Productivity Growth in China's Manufacturing Sector, 1998–2013

**Authors:** Loren Brandt, Johannes Van Biesebroeck, Luhang Wang, Yifan Zhang
**Date:** 2026 (JDE Volume 181 — exact month unconfirmed)
**Source:** *Journal of Development Economics*, Vol. 181(C)
**URL:** https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/deveco/v181y2026ics0304387826000039.html

**Abstract:** The paper examines productivity growth in China's manufacturing sector using
firm-level data from two sources, addressing data-quality issues in the National Bureau of
Statistics survey by drawing on State Taxation Administration records (2007–2013) and
reconstructing the full sampling scheme. The analysis reveals a marked slowdown in
revenue-based total factor productivity growth that cuts across all industries, ownership
types, and regions, with notable declines in the private sector and reduced contributions
from new-firm entry. The findings raise questions about the sources of China's future
growth potential.

## 5. Higher Education Expansion and the Rural–Urban Gap in Secondary Schooling and Labor Outcomes: Evidence from China's 1999 Reform

**Authors:** Ruoming Zhang
**Date:** 2026 (JDE Volume 181 — exact month unconfirmed)
**Source:** *Journal of Development Economics*, Vol. 181(C)
**URL:** https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/deveco/v181y2026ics0304387826000040.html

**Abstract:** This paper analyses how China's 1999 university admissions expansion influenced
rural and urban students' high-school academic-track choices and subsequent employment
outcomes. Using 2015 Population Sample Survey microdata with a difference-in-differences
design, the study finds rural students gained 2.9 percentage points in academic high-school
completion and 0.26 additional years of schooling. Rural workers also shifted away from
low-skill jobs by 5.1 percentage points. The findings highlight a strong indirect channel
through upper-secondary choices, suggesting that expanding tertiary capacity altered
educational expectations for rural cohorts and partially closed the rural–urban labor-market gap.
