# China Economics Papers — 2026-05-07

## 1. China's Global Ownership
**Authors:** Jennie Bai, Luc Laeven, Yaojun Ke, and Hong Ru
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35106
**Abstract:** This paper studies the global footprint and real effects of Chinese overseas corporate ownership by assembling a comprehensive micro-level dataset of 161,773 firms across 159 countries from 2012–2021. Ownership chains are traced through tax havens to identify ultimate Chinese control, revealing a much larger footprint than official foreign direct investment statistics show. Chinese-controlled foreign assets expanded at 20% annually, reaching $2.1 trillion—approximately 3% of global corporate assets by 2021. State-owned enterprises prioritized acquiring firms with strong R&D capabilities and supply-chain connections. While acquired firms boosted capital investment and research spending, these inputs fail to generate higher patent output and are accompanied by a significant decline in profitability. Chinese parent companies experienced accelerated patent growth following their first developed-economy acquisitions, demonstrating an "innovation spillback" mechanism. The study documents that increased Chinese ownership crowds out R&D at competing firms, though overall industry innovation levels remained stable, reflecting a distinctively state-driven model prioritizing long-term technological capacity accumulation over short-term gains.

## 2. Homemade Foreign Trading
**Authors:** Zhiguo He, Yuehan Wang, and Xiaoquan Zhu
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35095
**Abstract:** This paper examines cross-border holding data from China's Stock Connect program and finds that Chinese mainland insiders evade see-through surveillance by round-tripping via the program. Following a 2018 reform improving investor identification, the predictive power of northbound flows diminished, particularly among less prestigious custodians. The analysis demonstrates that the reform erodes price informativeness, particularly in stocks with high exposure to homemade foreign investors. The paper provides causal evidence—using the 2018 regulatory reform as a natural experiment—that regulatory improvements in investor identification, while reducing illicit trading, come with the cost of reducing the information content of cross-border capital flows in Chinese equity markets.

## 3. AI Patents in the United States and China: Measurement, Organization, and Knowledge Flows
**Authors:** Hanming Fang, Xian Gu, Hanyin Yan, and Wu Zhu
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35022
**Abstract:** This paper develops a machine-learning classifier to identify artificial intelligence patents with high accuracy (97.0% precision, 91.3% recall, and a 94.0% F1 score on USPTO data), and successfully transfers the tool to Chinese patents. Analyzing patents from 1976–2023 (U.S.) and 2010–2023 (China), the authors find rapid AI patenting growth in both nations with converging intensity and subject areas, though China recently surpassed the U.S. in annual filings. Organizational structures diverge significantly: American AI innovation concentrates among established corporations and tech hubs, while Chinese innovation spreads across universities, state enterprises, and diverse regions. Both countries' publicly-traded firms show market premiums for AI patents. Citation patterns reveal sustained technological interdependence, with Chinese inventors drawing more heavily on American frontier research than reciprocally. The findings shed light on the nature of U.S.–China technological competition and the organizational underpinnings of AI innovation in each country.

## 4. The Unseen Costs of Blue Skies: Pollutant Substitution and Biodiversity Loss
**Authors:** Joshua S. Graff Zivin, Siyuan Li, Huanhuan Wang, and Zhiqiang Zhang
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35087
**Abstract:** This paper examines how incomplete performance metrics guide environmental policy, creating distorted incentives. Using China's staggered rollout of air monitoring networks as a natural experiment, the authors find that PM₂.₅ fell significantly following monitoring expansion, yet O₃ surged as local governments prioritized particulate abatement while neglecting ozone precursors. While PM₂.₅ reductions lowered related deaths, the resulting ozone increase raised ozone-related mortality and harmed bird populations, reducing net policy benefits by approximately 23.8%. The study documents a classic case of pollutant substitution driven by narrow regulatory targets and contributes to the literature on the unintended consequences of performance-based environmental regulation in China.

## 5. The Chinese Current Account Imbalances: Puzzles, Patterns, and Possible Causes
**Authors:** Chang Ma and Shang-Jin Wei
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35056
**Abstract:** This paper examines China's substantial and persistent current account surplus and its impact on trading partners. While sector-specific industrial and trade policies create targeted imbalances, they contribute minimally to the overall economy-wide surplus. Instead, structural factors—particularly demographic imbalances in gender ratios and disparities in financing access between state-owned versus non-state enterprises—emerge as primary drivers of the current account imbalance. The authors find that while short-term macroeconomic stimulus can temporarily increase imports and reduce the surplus, addressing the fundamental imbalance requires comprehensive structural reforms targeting these underlying causes. The paper uses Chinese firm-level and household data alongside cross-country evidence to identify and quantify the drivers of the surplus, with implications for ongoing debates over global imbalances and China's trade relationships.
