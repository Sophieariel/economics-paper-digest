# China Economics Papers — 2026-05-12

## 1. The Chinese Current Account Imbalances: Puzzles, Patterns, and Possible Causes
**Authors:** Chang Ma, Shang-Jin Wei
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35056 (NBER Working Paper No. 35056)
**Abstract:** This paper examines why China maintains a sizable current account surplus that has created tensions with trading partners. While sector-specific industrial and trade policies contribute to imbalances in particular industries, they represent a relatively minor factor in the overall economy-wide surplus. Instead, the research identifies structural factors as the primary drivers, particularly China's unbalanced sex ratio and unequal financing access between state-owned enterprises and non-state firms. The authors construct a model connecting these structural distortions to aggregate saving-investment imbalances and test its predictions against Chinese provincial and national data. They conclude that while temporary macroeconomic stimulus measures can increase imports and temporarily reduce surpluses, addressing the economy-wide imbalance requires fundamental reforms targeting these underlying structural issues rather than sector-specific trade policy interventions.

## 2. A Tale of Two Countries – The Real Estate Crises in 1990s Japan and Contemporary China
**Authors:** Kenneth S. Rogoff, Yuanchen Yang
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35054 (NBER Working Paper No. 35054)
**Abstract:** This paper examines how real estate has become destabilizing for China's economy. Since 2018, the sector has declined sharply, turning from a key engine of expansion into a contraction driver. With housing representing approximately 70 percent of household wealth, price corrections have triggered substantial negative wealth effects that suppress consumer spending and investment. The researchers document how China's real estate downturn propagates through the broader economy and assess risks of prolonged stagnation. Using Chinese household survey data, property transaction records, and national accounts, they compare China's experience with Japan's 1990s real estate collapse, identifying striking parallels in investment dynamics and consumption responses despite profound institutional differences. Their analysis emphasizes real-side transmission channels beyond banking that generate persistent output losses following real estate downturns, and evaluates the effectiveness of recent Chinese policy interventions aimed at stabilizing the housing sector.

## 3. AI Patents in the United States and China: Measurement, Organization, and Knowledge Flows
**Authors:** Hanming Fang, Xian Gu, Hanyin Yan, Wu Zhu
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35022 (NBER Working Paper No. 35022)
**Abstract:** The researchers created an advanced classification system for identifying artificial intelligence patents by refining PatentSBERTa using manually reviewed USPTO data. Their method achieved 97.0% precision, 91.3% recall, and a 94.0% F1 score, with successful application to Chinese patents. Analyzing U.S. patents from 1976–2023 and Chinese patents from 2010–2023, they found both nations experienced rapid AI patenting growth with convergent patterns. However, organizational structures differed markedly: American innovation concentrated among established corporations in established hubs, while Chinese patenting showed greater geographic dispersion and institutional variety, including universities and state enterprises. The study also found AI patents generated measurable market premiums for listed companies in both countries, and citation patterns indicated ongoing technological interdependence rather than complete separation between nations, with Chinese innovators drawing more heavily on American frontier research. These findings speak directly to debates about industrial policy effectiveness and the degree of technological decoupling between the two economies.

## 4. Household Migration and Collateral Constraint: Cash-based Housing Resettlement in China
**Authors:** Zhiguo He, Zehao Liu, Xinle Pang, Yang Su, Kunru Zou
**Date:** March 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34982 (NBER Working Paper No. 34982)
**Abstract:** This research demonstrates how collateral constraints restrict household relocation to pricier areas by limiting access to home-purchase financing. The authors leverage China's shantytown renovation initiative (2015–2018) as a natural experiment, where cash distributions converted illiquid properties into liquid assets for displaced households. Their findings show cash resettlement enabled households to upgrade locations and increased property values in costlier markets. A dynamic spatial model incorporating collateral constraints validates the observed household migration responses. The quantitative analysis reveals that endogenous migration amplifies housing spending responses by roughly 40%, explaining over 20% of housing price expansion during 2016–2020. The paper contributes to understanding of urbanization dynamics in China, showing that financial frictions—not just preferences or income—are a binding constraint on internal migration patterns, with implications for the design of resettlement and housing finance policies.

## 5. From Free Rider to Innovator: The Rise of China's Drug Development
**Authors:** Panle Jia Barwick, Hongyuan Xia, Tianli Xia
**Date:** March 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34977 (NBER Working Paper No. 34977)
**Abstract:** This research examines how China transformed from a pharmaceutical "free rider" to a global innovator over the past decade. The authors compiled a comprehensive database tracking scientific publications, clinical trials, drug development milestones, sales data, and policies across China, the U.S., and Europe from 2010–2020. The study reveals China's clinical trial volume surpassed the United States by 2020, up from less than 8% of global trials in 2010. The National Reimbursement Drug List (NRDL) reform emerged as the primary driver, expanding the market for innovative pharmaceuticals. Following the reform, trial quantity increased by 86%, with growth concentrated in reform-exposed disease categories and among domestic firms developing first- or best-in-class drugs. The analysis demonstrates the NRDL reform accounted for 43% of oncology trial growth, substantially exceeding contributions from knowledge accumulation and talent migration. Notably, long-term innovation gains from the reform exceeded immediate consumer access improvements by threefold, emphasizing the importance of considering sustained incentive effects alongside near-term affordability benefits. The paper provides rigorous empirical evidence on the effectiveness of demand-side industrial policy in fostering domestic innovation.
