# China Economics Papers — 2026-05-14

## 1. Strategic Emissions in the Rain
**Authors:** Runhao Zhao, Ye Yuan
**Date:** April 15, 2026
**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdeveco.2026.103731
**Abstract:** Industrial firms in China strategically time wastewater discharge during heavy rainfall to exploit natural dilution effects and evade water quality monitoring systems. The research documents this "polluting-in-the-rain" phenomenon using daily water quality data from 147 monitoring stations (2014–2019), revealing that firms employ "store-then-discharge" tactics to release untreated effluent when rainfall obscures pollution concentrations. Estimated annual covert emissions offset 43% of national COD reduction targets, demonstrating how threshold-based monitoring systems remain vulnerable to sophisticated temporal evasion despite continuous oversight. *Published in Journal of Development Economics* 181: 103731 (2026).

## 2. China's Global Ownership
**Authors:** Jennie Bai, Luc Laeven, Yaojun Ke, Hong Ru
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35106
**Abstract:** This paper examines Chinese overseas corporate ownership across 161,773 firms in 159 countries from 2012–2021. By tracing ownership through offshore tax havens to ultimate origins, the authors find that Chinese-controlled foreign assets expanded at 20% annually, reaching $2.1 trillion or roughly 3% of global corporate assets by 2021. Chinese investors, particularly state-owned enterprises, strategically acquire R&D-intensive firms. While target firms increase capital investment and R&D spending post-acquisition, they experience declining profitability and stagnant patent output. Notably, Chinese parent companies accelerate patent generation following their first developed-economy acquisition—an "innovation spillback" mechanism. The study reveals that increased Chinese ownership crowds out R&D at competitor firms, though overall industry-level innovation remains stable. This represents a distinctive state-driven ownership model prioritizing long-term technological capacity accumulation over immediate performance gains. *NBER Working Paper No. 35106.*

## 3. The Geography of Market Power: Evidence from the Chinese Steel Industry
**Authors:** Loren Brandt, Feitao Jiang, Yao Luo, Yingjun Su
**Date:** May 6, 2026
**Source:** https://www.economics.utoronto.ca/public/workingPapers/tecipa-820.pdf
**Abstract:** This paper examines China's steel industry consolidation through a spatial economics lens. Rather than relying on national concentration metrics, the researchers demonstrate that geographic factors fundamentally shape market power, finding that "the geography of supply and demand—and the frictions that separate them—are central to understanding market power." Using a spatial competition model calibrated to Chinese firm-level data, they show that regionally-focused mergers led by efficient producers yield superior welfare outcomes (24% improvement) compared to actual 2018–2024 consolidation policy, which captured less than a quarter of the potential welfare gains. The findings have direct implications for Chinese industrial policy design. *University of Toronto Department of Economics Working Paper No. 820 (2026).*

## 4. Sex and the City: Demographics, Spatial Sorting, and the Marriage Market
**Authors:** Min Fang, Zibin Huang, Yushi Wang, Yu Alan Yang
**Date:** April 28, 2026
**Source:** https://voxchina.org/show-3-451.html
**Abstract:** This paper examines why China's marriage rate has declined sharply in recent decades. The researchers identify three interconnected factors: women now exceed men in educational attainment, highly educated women concentrate in service-sector jobs in developed cities, and traditional marriage norms (hypergamy) persist despite these shifts. This creates a spatial mismatch where educated women in prosperous cities and less-educated men in poorer regions struggle to find suitable local partners. Using data from China's 2020 and 2015 censuses, China Statistical Yearbooks, and the China Family Panel Studies (CFPS) 2018 and 2020 surveys, the analysis finds that gender-specific spatial sorting accounts for approximately 30% of the rise in female singlehood nationwide, with projections indicating conditions could worsen significantly by 2030. *Working paper (2026).*

## 5. The Chinese Current Account Imbalances: Puzzles, Patterns, and Possible Causes
**Authors:** Chang Ma, Shang-Jin Wei
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35056
**Abstract:** This paper examines China's persistent current account surplus and its structural causes. While trade and industrial policies create sector-specific imbalances, the authors find they have limited impact on the overall economy-wide surplus. Using a combination of empirical analysis and structural modeling, they identify two structural factors as more significant drivers: an unbalanced sex ratio (linked to precautionary household saving) and unequal access to financing between state-owned and non-state enterprises (which distorts domestic investment). The research concludes that macroeconomic stimulus may temporarily reduce the surplus through increased imports, but any long-term solution would need to address these underlying structural distortions. *NBER Working Paper No. 35056.*
