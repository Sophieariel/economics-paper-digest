# China Economics Papers — 2026-05-15

## 1. Ray of Hope? China and the Rise of Solar Energy
**Authors:** Ignacio Banares-Sanchez, Robin Burgess, Dávid László, Pol Simpson, John Van Reenen, Yifan Wang
**Date:** February 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34893 (NBER Working Paper No. 34893)
**Abstract:** We examine whether industrial policies supporting clean energy can simultaneously boost growth and welfare while reducing emissions. We study Chinese solar subsidies implemented across city-regions from 2004–2020 using synthetic-difference-in-differences and newly constructed city and firm panel datasets covering policies, patents, and output. We find that production and innovation subsidies were more effective than demand-side (installation) subsidies in generating large and persistent increases in local innovation, net entry, production, and exports, while demand-side policies reduced local pollution. A structural quantitative spatial model with endogenous innovation shows that local effects explain 40–50% of the aggregate changes in solar innovation, prices, and revenues. Social benefits to Chinese citizens exceeded subsidy costs by 65%, and this ratio doubles once environmental gains are included. Innovation subsidies emerge as the most cost-effective subsidy type for increasing welfare.

## 2. A Few Bad Apples? Academic Dishonesty, Political Selection, and Institutional Performance in China
**Authors:** Zhuang Liu, Wenwei Peng, Shaoda Wang
**Date:** January 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34747 (NBER Working Paper No. 34747)
**Abstract:** We apply plagiarism-detection algorithms to approximately 500,000 Chinese graduate dissertations and document that academic dishonesty is pervasive and predicts adverse political selection: plagiarists are more likely to enter and advance in the public sector. Examining judicial performance, we find that judges with plagiarism histories issue more preferential rulings and attract a greater number of appeals relative to their clean-record peers; livestreaming of trials partially mitigates these distortions. We also document spillover effects onto colleagues, consistent with a peer-influence channel, and find that enforcement of academic integrity standards produces modest but meaningful improvements in future professional conduct. The findings speak to how hidden early-life misconduct can propagate through the career pipeline to shape institutional quality in a large developing-country bureaucracy.

## 3. Culture, Risk-Taking, and Public Leadership: Evidence from Chinese Villages
**Authors:** Justin Jihao Hong
**Date:** 2026 (Journal of Development Economics, Vol. 181)
**Source:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304387826000453
**Abstract:** This paper studies the causal impact of risk-taking on public leadership quality by exploiting Chinese zodiac culture, which generates within-individual variation in risk appetite based on birth year. Using a representative village panel, the analysis finds that lower risk-taking by village heads leads to improved governance processes and greater political responsiveness. Risk-averse leaders also shift expenditures toward public goods and away from administration spending that is prone to misuse. However, these leaders are less likely to promote policy innovation. The results suggest that risk avoidance can improve leader performance when incentives for responsiveness are limited, while simultaneously creating a trade-off between accountability and public entrepreneurship—with implications for how cultural traits shape local governance outcomes in rural China.

## 4. Rural-Urban Migration and Market Integration
**Authors:** Dennis Egger, Benjamin Faber, Ming Li, Wei Lin
**Date:** August 2025
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34098 (NBER Working Paper No. 34098)
**Abstract:** We examine how reductions in rural-urban migration barriers affect trade and investment flows between cities and rural areas. Using Chinese microdata and a natural policy experiment that increased worker eligibility for urban residence registration (Hukou reform), we find that higher rural migration market access increases rural-urban exports, imports, capital inflows, and capital outflows—both in transaction values and buyer-seller matches. Exploiting a spatial equilibrium model, we estimate that a 10% increase in rural migration market access generates approximately 1.5% growth in trade market access and 2% growth in investment market access, with integration effects generally more pronounced in urban destinations than in rural origins. The results highlight migration barriers as a driver of broader market segmentation between China's rural and urban economies beyond labor markets alone.

## 5. Judicial Institution and Innovation: Evidence from China's Intellectual Property Courts Reform
**Authors:** Liyang Wan, Qian Wan, Zichao Yang, Ying Zhao
**Date:** 2026 (Journal of Development Economics, Vol. 179)
**Source:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304387825001816
**Abstract:** We examine the effect of intellectual property (IP) judicial institutions on innovation by studying China's establishment of specialized intellectual property courts (IPCs) starting in 2014, which expanded to 26 courts across 19 provinces by 2020. Using city-level panel data, we find that the IPC reform leads to a 22.6% increase in invention patents—an average of 215 additional patents annually per city. We rule out inter-region and intra-conglomerate patent transfers, establishing that the effect is not a zero-sum reallocation among regions. The reform shifts the composition of patents toward higher-quality invention patents but does not significantly improve patent quality within that category. Mechanism analysis indicates that the reform raises social satisfaction with IP judicial protection, reduces case duration, and increases plaintiff winning rates, lowering the cost of IP enforcement and encouraging greater innovation effort.
