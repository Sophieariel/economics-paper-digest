# China Economics Papers — 2026-05-21

## 1. Strategic Emissions in the Rain
**Authors:** Runhao Zhao, Ye Yuan
**Date:** April 2026 (Journal of Development Economics, Volume 181)
**Source:** https://voxchina.org/show-3-454.html (VoxChina column, April 15, 2026); Journal of Development Economics, Vol. 181, 2026
**Abstract:** This paper documents a sophisticated form of regulatory evasion where industrial firms in China strategically discharge untreated wastewater during heavy rainfall to exploit the natural dilution effect and circumvent water quality monitoring systems. Combining daily water quality readings from 147 automated monitoring stations with precipitation data spanning 2014–2019, the analysis classifies stations by upstream industrial density and compares pollution patterns across thousands of rainfall episodes. Firms employ "store-and-discharge" strategies—stockpiling effluent during dry periods and releasing it when precipitation increases river runoff—to exploit threshold-based monitoring systems designed around dry-weather baselines. Back-of-envelope calculations estimate approximately 75,000 tons of annual chemical oxygen demand emissions on rain days, offsetting roughly 43% of China's national industrial pollution reduction targets for the 13th Five-Year Plan. The findings reveal fundamental limitations of threshold-based monitoring and recommend deploying real-time end-of-pipe monitoring directly at emission sources.

## 2. Shifting Forces Behind RMB Internationalization: Evidence from the 2025 Triennial Survey
**Authors:** Juliana Robbert, Vladyslav Sushko, Frank Westermann
**Date:** April 29, 2026
**Source:** https://www.bis.org/publ/work1345.htm (BIS Working Papers No. 1345)
**Abstract:** This paper investigates the factors driving Renminbi trading internationalization using data from the 2025 BIS Triennial Survey. The researchers analyze both short-term growth and long-term turnover patterns across jurisdictions, studying the role of bilateral trade and financial links with China as well as policy-driven variables, and examining unique trading dynamics in Asian financial centres and differences between spot and derivatives trading. Key findings indicate that financial factors, especially banking links with China, play a key role in driving RMB internationalization, reinforcing market-driven convergence patterns—though at slower rates than previously documented. Policy-driven variables such as qualified investor licenses are significant long-term drivers, and bilateral trade effects are stronger for cross-border RMB trading. The research addresses prior gaps in understanding real and financial linkage effects and documents how the forces behind RMB internationalization have shifted relative to the 2022 survey.

## 3. Political Accountability and Local Government Debt: Evidence from China
**Authors:** Runheng Li, Yao Tang, Xi Weng, Li-An Zhou
**Date:** April 28, 2026
**Source:** https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/128626/ (MPRA Paper No. 128626)
**Abstract:** This paper examines how political accountability interacts with local officials' career goals to influence China's Municipal Corporate Bond (MCB) market. Using a 2017 debt responsibility regulation as a natural experiment, the authors develop a bargaining model showing how the rule transformed officials' characteristics into credible signals about bailout likelihood. Analysis of prefecture-level bond data spanning 2008–2020 reveals that each additional year of a local party secretary's remaining time to retirement reduces MCB spreads by approximately 2.5 basis points and increases issuance volume by about 2.0%, with effects intensifying in fiscally stressed regions. Notably, the 2017 regulation intended to control borrowing paradoxically enabled officials with stronger bailout incentives to expand bond issuance beyond intended limits, illustrating how accountability reforms can generate unintended consequences in decentralized political economies.

## 4. Sowing Seeds of Mobility: The Gendered Impact of Land Reform
**Authors:** Ting Chen, Jiajia Gu, L. Rachel Ngai, Jin Wang
**Date:** April 2026 (IZA Discussion Paper No. 18569; announced in NEP-CNA May 4, 2026)
**Source:** https://docs.iza.org/dp18569.pdf (IZA Discussion Paper No. 18569)
**Abstract:** This paper examines how land market frictions create gender-differentiated effects during economic transformation in China. Analyzing two land reforms that expanded farmers' rental rights—the 2003 Rural Land Contracting Law and the 2009 land titling reform—the authors use large-language-model text analysis of over 4,000 government documents to construct a county-level reform index, combined with extensive panel data to identify causal effects. The findings show that the reforms accelerated rural women's exit from agriculture more than men's, while simultaneously reducing urban women's employment and wages relative to men. A multisector economic model incorporating household labor allocation explains these patterns: gender-neutral land market frictions act as gender-specific mobility barriers because families traditionally designate one member—typically the wife—to remain on the farm to protect land rights under insecure tenure. Removing these frictions therefore disproportionately reshapes women's labor allocation across the rural-urban spectrum.

## 5. Reduced Profitability of Green Bond Issuance: Evidence from China
**Authors:** Yilin Cai, Meng Feng, Yueming (Lucy) Qiu, Yi David Wang
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/WP/Issues/2026/04/10/Reduced-Profitability-of-Green-Bond-Issuance-Evidence-from-China-571834 (IMF Working Paper No. 2026/082)
**Abstract:** This paper employs a multi-period difference-in-differences methodology to examine how green bond issuance affects the profitability of heavily polluting firms traded on China's A-share market. The average treatment effect of green bond issuance on heavy-polluting firms' return on equity is significantly negative, indicating that pursuing green transformation through bond issuance requires firms to accept reduced near-term profitability. Heterogeneity analyses reveal that these effects vary with firm ownership structure (state-owned versus private), geographic location, and industry classification. The results are consistent with green bonds inducing polluting enterprises to reconsider environmental practices and undertake costly abatement investments, with implications for the design of market-based green finance instruments in emerging economies.
