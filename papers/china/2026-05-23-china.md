# China Economics Papers — 2026-05-23

*Note: No new China-focused NBER working papers have been posted since the previous digest (2026-05-01). The five papers below are the most recently published China empirical working papers from priority sources not yet featured in this digest series. They range from January–February 2026 (NBER) and August 2025 (NBER), all presented at the NBER Chinese Economy Working Group Spring 2026 meeting.*

## 1. Ray of Hope? China and the Rise of Solar Energy
**Authors:** Ignacio Banares-Sanchez, Robin Burgess, Dávid László, Pol Simpson, John Van Reenen, Yifan Wang
**Date:** February 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34893 (NBER Working Paper No. 34893)
**Abstract:** We investigate whether industrial policies promoting clean energy can simultaneously enhance growth and welfare while reducing emissions. We examine Chinese solar subsidies implemented across city-regions during 2004–2020, constructing new data on policies, patents, and output. Using synthetic-difference-in-differences, we find that production and innovation subsidies were more effective than demand-side (installation) subsidies in generating large and persistent increases in local innovation, net entry, production, and exports; demand policies reduced local pollution. We build a quantitative spatial model with endogenous innovation that reveals local effects explain 40%–50% of aggregate changes in solar innovation, prices, and revenues. Social benefits exceeded subsidy costs by 65%, doubling when environmental gains are included. Innovation subsidies proved most cost-effective across subsidy types, and productivity gains from subsidized entry account for a large share of China's dramatic cost reductions in solar manufacturing.

## 2. Currency Pegs, Trade Imbalances and Unemployment: A Reevaluation of the China Shock
**Authors:** Bumsoo Kim, Marc De la Barrera, Masao Fukui
**Date:** February 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34823 (NBER Working Paper No. 34823)
**Abstract:** We develop a dynamic quantitative model with nominal wage rigidity and consumption-saving decisions to study how China's exchange-rate peg shaped US labor-market and trade outcomes during China's rapid growth. By preventing currency appreciation, the peg temporarily accelerated Chinese export growth, intensifying adverse consequences for US labor markets and widening the trade deficit through elevated Chinese savings. Our calibrated model shows that the currency peg played a quantitatively important role in manufacturing decline, trade-deficit expansion, and unemployment patterns in the United States. Notably, the overall welfare impact of the China shock remains significant and positive, underscoring how exchange-rate dynamics shape the distributional and aggregate consequences of large trade shocks.

## 3. A Few Bad Apples? Academic Dishonesty, Political Selection, and Institutional Performance in China
**Authors:** Zhuang Liu, Wenwei Peng, Shaoda Wang
**Date:** January 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34747 (NBER Working Paper No. 34747)
**Abstract:** We apply plagiarism-detection algorithms to approximately 500,000 Chinese graduate dissertations to identify hidden academic misconduct and trace its consequences. We establish four results: plagiarism correlates with entry into and advancement within government service; judges with plagiarism histories issue more biased rulings and attract more appeals; plagiarizing judges negatively influence the behavior of peer judges and legal professionals; and enforcement of academic integrity standards modestly improves future professional conduct. Our findings suggest that while honesty is considered essential to societal functioning, substantial undetected misconduct persists in the pipeline to public office, with career sorting masking broader institutional degradation from dishonest behavior.

## 4. The Ins & Outs of Chinese Monetary Policy Transmission
**Authors:** Silvia Miranda-Agrippino, Tsvetelina Nenova, Hélène Rey
**Date:** January 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34626 (NBER Working Paper No. 34626)
**Abstract:** We develop a novel indicator for the People's Bank of China's monetary policy stance and estimate a policy rule that reflects its dual mandate of domestic price stability and exchange-rate management. Domestic transmission follows textbook patterns, with exceptions arising from the active management of the renminbi and the financial account. Turning to international spillovers, we find that Chinese monetary policy shocks affect commodity markets, global production and trade, with substantial pass-through to foreign prices. Financial spillovers are of secondary importance and largely derive from trade spillovers, pointing to the real channel as the primary conduit for the international transmission of Chinese monetary policy.

## 5. Rural-Urban Migration and Market Integration
**Authors:** Dennis Egger, Benjamin Faber, Ming Li, Wei Lin
**Date:** August 2025
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34098 (NBER Working Paper No. 34098)
**Abstract:** We combine new Chinese microdata with a natural policy experiment to study how reducing rural-urban migration barriers affects trade and investment flows between cities and rural areas. We find that expanded worker eligibility for urban residence registration (Hukou) increased bilateral exports, imports, and capital movements between rural counties and urban destinations. Using a spatial equilibrium model, we show that a 10% increase in a rural county's migration market access on average leads to a 1.5% increase in the county's trade market access and a 2% increase in investment market access. The results suggest migrants help reduce buyer-seller matching frictions, with urban destinations experiencing larger gains than rural origins from China's recent Hukou reforms, highlighting migration liberalization as an important driver of market integration in developing economies.
