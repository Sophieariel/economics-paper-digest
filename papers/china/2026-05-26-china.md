# China Economics Papers — 2026-05-26

## 1. The Chinese Current Account Imbalances: Puzzles, Patterns, and Possible Causes
**Authors:** Chang Ma, Shang-Jin Wei
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35056 (NBER Working Paper No. 35056)
**Abstract:** China has run large current account surpluses for much of the last two decades, and such surpluses are set to rise again. The surpluses generate significant frictions with trading partners and yet they are puzzling in many ways. We discuss key features and review the literature on potential causes. While sector-specific trade and industrial policies can create bilateral trade imbalances and sector-level imbalances, they have limited impacts on the aggregate, economy-wide current account surplus. Structural factors—including demographic imbalances reflected in a distorted sex ratio and limited access to corporate financing for private versus state-owned enterprises—are identified as primary drivers of the economy-wide current account surplus. Temporary fiscal expansion can reduce the surplus in the short run by increasing imports, but addressing the underlying structural issues is essential to achieve lasting reductions in the surplus.

## 2. Ray of Hope? China and the Rise of Solar Energy
**Authors:** Ignacio Banares-Sanchez, Robin Burgess, Dávid László, Pol Simpson, John Van Reenen, Yifan Wang
**Date:** February 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34893 (NBER Working Paper No. 34893)
**Abstract:** Can industrial policies that promote clean energy boost growth while reducing emissions? We study China's solar subsidies, which were rolled out across city-regions from 2004 to 2020. Using new data on policies, patents, and production, we find that production and innovation subsidies were more effective than demand-side (installation) subsidies in generating large and persistent increases in local innovation, net entry, production, and exports. The social benefits to Chinese citizens surpassed the subsidy costs by 65%, a figure that doubles once environmental gains are accounted for, with innovation subsidies being the most cost-effective overall. Given the global public goods nature of innovation and the global environmental externalities of fossil fuel substitution, the international social benefits are substantially larger than the domestic benefits. China's solar subsidies thus provide a prominent example of industrial policy that appears to have worked.

## 3. Currency Pegs, Trade Imbalances and Unemployment: A Reevaluation of the China Shock
**Authors:** Bumsoo Kim, Marc De la Barrea, Masao Fukui
**Date:** February 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34823 (NBER Working Paper No. 34823)
**Abstract:** We develop a dynamic quantitative model of trade and labor adjustment to study how China's currency peg interacted with its rapid growth in shaping the U.S. economy. The peg temporarily accelerated China's export growth by preventing the exchange rate from appreciating, amplifying the labor-market disruptions in the United States. The induced export surge also raised China's savings and thereby contributed to the widening of the U.S. trade deficit. When calibrated to data on trade and labor markets, the peg is quantitatively important in explaining U.S. manufacturing decline, the widening trade deficit, and unemployment dynamics. Exchange-rate adjustment—or the lack thereof—is therefore central to understanding the labor-market effects of trade shocks. Despite these disruptions, the overall welfare impact of the China shock remains significant and positive.

## 4. A Few Bad Apples? Academic Dishonesty, Political Selection, and Institutional Performance in China
**Authors:** Zhuang Liu, Wenwei Peng, Shaoda Wang
**Date:** January 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34747 (NBER Working Paper No. 34747)
**Abstract:** We apply plagiarism-detection algorithms to approximately 500,000 graduate dissertations in China to measure hidden academic misconduct. Plagiarism is pervasive and predicts adverse political selection: plagiarists are more likely to enter and advance in the public sector. Leveraging variation in judges' career histories, we find that judges with plagiarism records issue more preferential rulings and attract a greater number of appeals, indicating worse judicial performance. Anti-plagiarism enforcement—triggered by the staggered rollout of a national plagiarism-checking platform—modestly improves professionals' subsequent conduct, and generates positive spillovers onto colleagues in the legal profession. Together, these findings suggest that academic dishonesty in graduate education is both a symptom and a cause of institutional dysfunction, linking educational integrity to the quality of governance and public institutions.

## 5. Anti-corruption Shocks, Political Incentives, and Regional Economic Development in a Developmental State
**Authors:** Zhenyu Shen, Ruichao Si, Gang Xu
**Date:** 2026
**Source:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304387825001579 (Journal of Development Economics, Vol. 179, DOI: 10.1016/j.jdeveco.2025.103606)
**Abstract:** We study how anti-corruption campaigns affect regional economic development in China's political economy. Using the downfall of high-ranking officials as shocks to city leaders' political networks, we find that cities with leaders connected to investigated officials experience significant declines in economic performance. We attribute these declines to diminished corruption-induced efforts by local officials in government-led economic activities, including land sales, government procurement, and public infrastructure investment. At the same time, we document that local governments place greater emphasis on environmental protection and welfare indicators improve following anti-corruption shocks. Further analysis reveals that career incentives discourage local leaders from prioritizing economic growth after their patrons' downfall. Overall, our findings indicate that China's anti-corruption campaign has helped transform local government development models from corruption-driven, growth-oriented ones toward high-quality development centered on welfare, at the cost of weaker near-term economic performance.
