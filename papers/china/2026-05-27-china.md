# China Economics Papers — 2026-05-27

> **Note on date range:** The 30-day window (April 27 – May 27, 2026) yielded two confirmed April 2026 NBER papers (w35106, w35056). Three additional papers from January–March 2026 are included to complete the set of five; all are from the NBER Chinese Economy Working Group's Spring 2026 programme.

## 1. China's Global Ownership
**Authors:** Jennie Bai, Luc Laeven, Yaojun Ke, Hong Ru
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35106
**Abstract:** This paper examines Chinese corporate ownership across 161,773 firms in 159 countries (2012–2021), reconstructing ownership chains through offshore entities. Chinese-controlled foreign assets expanded at 20% annually, reaching $2.1 trillion or roughly 3% of global corporate assets by 2021. Chinese investors, particularly state-owned enterprises, targeted technology-intensive and supply-chain-connected firms. While target companies increased capital investment and R&D spending following acquisition, these investments did not boost patent output and were linked to reduced profitability. The analysis reveals an "innovation spillback" pattern: acquired firms showed stagnant innovation, yet Chinese parent companies experienced accelerated patent growth after entering developed markets. Additionally, increased Chinese ownership crowded out R&D at competitor firms, though overall industry innovation remained stable. The paper characterises China's approach as a state-directed ownership model prioritising long-term technological capacity building domestically over short-term financial performance.

## 2. The Chinese Current Account Imbalances: Puzzles, Patterns, and Possible Causes
**Authors:** Chang Ma, Shang-Jin Wei
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35056
**Abstract:** This paper examines China's persistent current account surplus and its economic drivers. While trade and industrial policies create sector-specific imbalances, they contribute minimally to the overall surplus. Instead, structural factors prove more influential — specifically, the country's skewed sex ratio and disparate financing access between state-owned and private enterprises. The researchers conclude that while short-term macroeconomic stimulus can increase imports and temporarily reduce the surplus, addressing the underlying structural issues is necessary for any lasting solution to the imbalance.

## 3. When Do Financial Frictions Matter for Misallocation?
**Authors:** Yan Bai, Dan Lu, Xu Tian, Yajie Wang
**Date:** March 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34930
**Abstract:** This research examines how financial frictions contribute to capital misallocation by developing a model informed by firm-level borrowing expenses and average revenue product of capital metrics. Using Chinese manufacturing data, the authors document significant variation in capital returns alongside a correlation between firm productivity and borrowing costs — a pattern not observed in U.S. data. Their heterogeneous-firm model shows that eliminating financial frictions alone raises total factor productivity by 25 percentage points, but only 2 percentage points remains when other distortions are absent. The study demonstrates that "financial frictions can be highly distortionary when other sources of misallocation are present," suggesting these constraints intensify when combined with permanent firm-level impediments.

## 4. Ray of Hope? China and the Rise of Solar Energy
**Authors:** Ignacio Banares-Sanchez, Robin Burgess, Dávid László, Pol Simpson, John Van Reenen, Yifan Wang
**Date:** February 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34893
**Abstract:** This paper examines whether industrial policies supporting clean energy can simultaneously boost economic growth and reduce emissions. Using synthetic-difference-in-differences analysis on city and firm data from China, 2004–2020, the authors find that production and innovation subsidies were more effective than demand-side (installation) subsidies in driving local innovation, firm entry, production, and exports. However, demand policies did reduce pollution locally. A quantitative spatial model incorporating endogenous innovation reveals that local effects account for 40–50% of aggregate changes in solar innovation, prices, and revenues. The findings demonstrate that social benefits to Chinese citizens exceeded subsidy costs by 65% (doubling when environmental gains are included), with innovation subsidies proving most cost-effective overall.

## 5. A Few Bad Apples? Academic Dishonesty, Political Selection, and Institutional Performance in China
**Authors:** Zhuang Liu, Wenwei Peng, Shaoda Wang
**Date:** January 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34747
**Abstract:** This paper employs plagiarism-detection algorithms on approximately 500,000 Chinese graduate dissertations to examine academic misconduct's broader consequences. Key findings include: plagiarism is widespread and correlates with entry into public sector roles; plagiarists perform worse in positions of authority, particularly judges who issue more preferential rulings and face greater appeal rates; plagiarising judges create negative spillover effects on colleagues and legal professionals; and enforcement of academic integrity standards produces modest improvements in future professional behaviour. The paper provides novel empirical evidence on how educational misconduct shapes the quality of China's administrative and judicial institutions.
