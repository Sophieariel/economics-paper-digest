# China Economics Papers — 2026-05-29

## 1. China's Global Ownership
**Authors:** Jennie Bai, Luc Laeven, Yaojun Ke, and Hong Ru
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35106
**Abstract:** This research examines how Chinese corporations have expanded their ownership footprint globally. The authors analyzed data covering 161,773 firms across 159 countries from 2012–2021, tracing ownership through offshore structures to identify ultimate Chinese control. Chinese-controlled foreign assets grew at approximately 20% annually, totaling $2.1 trillion or roughly 3% of global corporate assets by 2021. State-owned enterprises particularly targeted research-intensive and supply-chain-connected companies. Following acquisition, target firms expanded capital investment and R&D spending but failed to generate proportional patent increases and experienced declining profitability. An "innovation spillback" occurred where Chinese parent companies accelerated patent generation after acquiring developed-economy firms, while target firm innovation stagnated. Increased Chinese ownership crowded out R&D at competitor firms, though overall industry innovation remained stable. The researchers characterize this pattern as a state-directed ownership model prioritizing long-term technological capacity accumulation over immediate financial returns.

## 2. The Chinese Current Account Imbalances: Puzzles, Patterns, and Possible Causes
**Authors:** Chang Ma and Shang-Jin Wei
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35056
**Abstract:** The paper examines China's substantial current account surplus and its effects on trading partners. While sector-specific industrial and trade policies create localized imbalances, they contribute minimally to the overall economy-wide surplus. Instead, the research identifies structural factors—particularly an unbalanced sex ratio and unequal access to financing between state-owned and non-state enterprises—as primary drivers of the current account imbalance. The authors conclude that although short-term macroeconomic stimulus can increase imports and reduce the surplus temporarily, addressing the fundamental imbalance requires reforms aiming at addressing the structural problems.

## 3. AI Patents in the United States and China: Measurement, Organization, and Knowledge Flows
**Authors:** Hanming Fang, Xian Gu, Hanyin Yan, and Wu Zhu
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35022
**Abstract:** The researchers created an advanced classifier to identify artificial intelligence patents, enhancing the existing USPTO methodology. The tool achieved 97.0% precision, 91.3% recall, and a 94.0% F1 score, and was successfully applied to Chinese patents through validation methods. Their analysis of U.S. patents from 1976–2023 and Chinese patents from 2010–2023 reveals swift expansion in AI patenting across both nations with comparable intensity and field distribution. China now generates more annual patents than the United States, though organizational patterns diverge significantly: American innovation concentrates among major corporations and established innovation centers, while Chinese activity distributes more broadly through universities and state enterprises. Listed companies in both countries showed strong market-value rewards for AI patents. Cross-border citation patterns suggest continued technological interdependence rather than decoupling, with Chinese innovators drawing more substantially on American advances than reciprocally.

## 4. Ray of Hope? China and the Rise of Solar Energy
**Authors:** Ignacio Banares-Sanchez, Robin Burgess, Dávid László, Pol Simpson, John Van Reenen, and Yifan Wang
**Date:** February 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34893
**Abstract:** This research examines whether industrial policies promoting clean energy can boost economic growth and welfare while reducing emissions. The authors studied Chinese solar subsidies implemented across city-regions between 2004–2020, using synthetic-difference-in-differences methodology with new city and firm-level panel data. Production and innovation subsidies were more effective than demand-side (installation) subsidies in generating large and persistent increases in local innovation, net entry, production and exports. Demand policies did reduce local pollution. A quantitative spatial model accounting for business stealing and knowledge spillovers revealed that local effects explain 40–50% of aggregate changes in solar innovation, prices, and revenues. The study concludes social benefits exceeded subsidy costs by 65% (doubling when environmental gains are included), with innovation subsidies proving most cost-effective.

## 5. American Investment in Chinese Renminbi
**Authors:** Bruno Cavani, Christopher Clayton, Amanda Dos Santos, Matteo Maggiori, and Jesse Schreger
**Date:** February 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34792
**Abstract:** The research examines U.S. mutual fund and ETF holdings of Chinese renminbi-denominated bonds using SEC Form NPORT data. The authors document that U.S. holdings of RMB bonds fell sharply in 2024, with most of this decline stemming from funds completely exiting their RMB positions. The findings indicate a meaningful shift in which foreign investors hold Chinese bonds, specifically showing reduced participation by American institutional investors. The study demonstrates how granular portfolio microdata can illuminate patterns in international currency markets and cross-border investment flows.
