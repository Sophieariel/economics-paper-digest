# China Economics Papers — 2026-06-01

## 1. China's Global Ownership
**Authors:** Jennie Bai, Luc Laeven, Yaojun Ke, Hong Ru
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35106
**Abstract:** This paper examines Chinese overseas corporate investments through a comprehensive dataset covering 161,773 firms across 159 countries from 2012–2021. The authors reconstruct ownership chains to identify ultimate capital origins, finding that Chinese-controlled foreign assets expanded at 20% annually, reaching $2.1 trillion by 2021. Key findings indicate that state-owned enterprises strategically acquire R&D-intensive and supply-chain-connected companies. While target firms boost capital and R&D spending post-acquisition, they experience stagnant patent output and declining profitability. Notably, the research identifies an "innovation spillback" effect: Chinese parent companies accelerate patent grants following their first developed-economy acquisition, even as target firm innovation remains flat. The study describes this as "a distinctively state-driven model of global ownership that accepts weaker near-term performance to internalize technological capacity at home."

## 2. The Chinese Current Account Imbalances: Puzzles, Patterns, and Possible Causes
**Authors:** Chang Ma, Shang-Jin Wei
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35056
**Abstract:** This paper examines China's substantial current account surplus and its origins. The researchers find that while sector-specific trade policies create localized imbalances, they account for relatively modest portions of the nation's overall surplus. Instead, deeper structural conditions—particularly demographic factors like skewed gender ratios and differential access to credit between state-owned versus private enterprises—emerge as more significant drivers. The authors conclude that temporary stimulus measures may temporarily reduce the surplus through increased imports, but addressing the long-term surplus requires tackling these fundamental structural challenges through comprehensive reforms.

## 3. From Free Rider to Innovator: The Rise of China's Drug Development
**Authors:** Panle Jia Barwick, Hongyuan Xia, Tianli Xia
**Date:** March 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34977
**Abstract:** This paper examines how China transformed from a pharmaceutical "free rider" into a global innovator during the past decade. The authors compiled a comprehensive database tracking scientific publications, clinical trials, drug development milestones, sales data, and government policies across China, the U.S., and Europe. Key findings show that China's clinical trial volume surpassed the United States by 2020, up from less than 8% of global share in 2010. The National Reimbursement Drug List (NRDL) reform was identified as the primary driver, expanding the market for innovative drugs. Post-reform, trial quantity increased by 86% with growth concentrated among novel drugs and domestic firms. A decomposition analysis attributes 43% of oncology trial growth to the NRDL reform. Notably, the study demonstrates that "dynamic gains from induced innovation exceed the reform's static gains" by threefold, highlighting the critical long-term importance of innovation incentives.

## 4. When Do Financial Frictions Matter for Misallocation?
**Authors:** Yan Bai, Dan Lu, Xu Tian, Yajie Wang
**Date:** March 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34930
**Abstract:** This paper evaluates how financial frictions contribute to inefficient capital allocation using a framework informed by firm-level borrowing expenses and capital productivity measures. Analyzing Chinese manufacturing data, the scholars observe substantial variance in returns across firms, coupled with a correlation between productivity and borrowing constraints—a pattern not evident in American data. The team developed a theoretical model incorporating variable borrowing costs and additional capital distortions. Their simulations reveal that removing financial constraints alone yields modest productivity gains (under 2 percentage points). However, when other distortions exist simultaneously, eliminating financial frictions produces substantially larger improvements (25 percentage points). This occurs because permanent firm-level distortions create selection effects where the most productive enterprises face the tightest financing constraints, amplifying misallocation effects.

## 5. Ray of Hope? China and the Rise of Solar Energy
**Authors:** Ignacio Banares-Sanchez, Robin Burgess, Dávid László, Pol Simpson, John Van Reenen, Yifan Wang
**Date:** February 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34893
**Abstract:** This paper examines whether clean energy policies can simultaneously boost economic growth and reduce emissions. The authors analyze Chinese solar subsidies implemented across city-regions from 2004–2020, finding that "production and innovation subsidies were more effective than demand-side (installation) subsidies in generating large and persistent increases in local innovation, net entry, production and exports." Their structural economic model reveals that local policy effects explain 40%–50% of aggregate changes in solar innovation, pricing, and revenues. Social benefits to Chinese citizens exceed subsidy costs by 65% (and double this when environmental benefits are included), though innovation-focused subsidies proved most cost-effective overall. The paper provides causal evidence on China's industrial policy effectiveness using subnational variation in subsidy rollout as a natural experiment.
