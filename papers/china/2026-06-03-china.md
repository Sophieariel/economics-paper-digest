## 1. A Tale of Two Countries – The Real Estate Crises in 1990s Japan and Contemporary China
**Authors:** Kenneth S. Rogoff, Yuanchen Yang
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35054
**Abstract:** Real estate has been fundamental to China's economic expansion, but since 2018 its contribution has diminished substantially, shifting the sector from a growth driver into a significant constraint on economic activity. Policy tightening may have initiated the downturn, yet it stems from deeper structural problems in a sector that—including related upstream, downstream, and infrastructure activities—represents roughly one-third of total demand. With housing representing approximately 70 percent of household wealth, the ongoing price decline has produced substantial negative wealth impacts, intensifying the contraction through decreased consumption, investment, and sentiment. The authors document how China's real estate contraction propagates through the broader economy and assess prolonged stagnation risks if deterioration persists. They compare China's situation with Japan's 1990s real estate collapse, identifying noteworthy similarities in investment patterns and consumption reactions despite significant institutional variations. The research emphasizes real-side transmission channels, including amplification mechanisms beyond banking systems, in producing sustained economic losses following real estate downturns.

## 2. AI Patents in the United States and China: Measurement, Organization, and Knowledge Flows
**Authors:** Hanming Fang, Xian Gu, Hanyin Yan, Wu Zhu
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35022
**Abstract:** The researchers developed a sophisticated classifier to identify artificial intelligence patents by refining PatentSBERTa using manually labeled USPTO data, achieving 97.0% precision, 91.3% recall, and a 94.0% F1 score, and successfully transferred the classifier to Chinese patents. Examining U.S. patents (1976–2023) and Chinese patents (2010–2023), they find rapid AI patenting expansion in both nations with converging intensity and subfield composition, though China recently surpassed the U.S. in annual patent counts. A critical distinction emerges: U.S. AI innovation concentrates among established corporations and regional clusters, whereas Chinese AI patenting shows greater geographic distribution and institutional variety, featuring prominent roles for universities and state enterprises. Listed companies in both countries benefit from AI patent market premiums. Cross-border citations reveal continued technological interdependence rather than decoupling, with Chinese inventors depending more substantially on American frontier developments than the reverse. The findings document how China's industrial structure and state-ownership patterns shape its innovation trajectory relative to the United States.

## 3. Household Migration and Collateral Constraint: Cash-based Housing Resettlement in China
**Authors:** Zhiguo He, Zehao Liu, Xinle Pang, Yang Su, Kunru Zou
**Date:** March 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34982
**Abstract:** This paper examines how collateral constraints shape household migration decisions and urban housing prices, using China's large-scale shantytown renovation program (2015–2018) as a natural experiment. The program replaced illiquid housing assets with cash compensation, relaxing collateral constraints for affected households. The authors find that households receiving cash—rather than in-kind housing resettlement—relocated to more expensive urban areas, generating upward pressure on housing prices in destination neighborhoods. A dynamic spatial equilibrium model with collateral constraints demonstrates that the endogenous migration response amplifies household housing expenditure by approximately 40 percent and accounts for over 20 percent of housing price growth in Chinese cities during 2016–2020. The results provide causal evidence on how asset liquidity and collateral access shape internal migration patterns and urban price dynamics in China's rapidly urbanizing economy.

## 4. From Free Rider to Innovator: The Rise of China's Drug Development
**Authors:** Panle Jia Barwick, Hongyuan Xia, Tianli Xia
**Date:** March 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34977
**Abstract:** This paper examines how China transformed from a pharmaceutical "free rider"—relying on generic copies of foreign drugs—to an emerging global innovator over the past decade, surpassing the United States in annual registered clinical trial volume by 2020 from a base of under 8% of global share in 2010. The authors compile a comprehensive database tracking scientific publications, clinical trials, drug development milestones, and sales data across China, the U.S., and Europe, alongside detailed government policy records. Using the National Reimbursement Drug List (NRDL) reform as a quasi-natural experiment, the study finds that the reform dramatically expanded the effective market size for innovative drugs, resulting in an 86% increase in drug trials concentrated in reform-exposed disease categories and domestic firms developing first- or best-in-class compounds. The NRDL reform accounts for 43% of growth in oncology trial activity, substantially exceeding contributions from knowledge accumulation and talent flows (24%). The authors conclude that dynamic gains from induced innovation exceed the reform's static gains in consumer access to innovative drugs by threefold, highlighting the importance of long-run innovation incentives alongside immediate affordability when evaluating pharmaceutical industrial policy.

## 5. A Few Bad Apples? Academic Dishonesty, Political Selection, and Institutional Performance in China
**Authors:** Zhuang Liu, Wenwei Peng, Shaoda Wang
**Date:** January 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34747
**Abstract:** This paper applies plagiarism-detection algorithms to approximately 500,000 Chinese graduate dissertations and documents that academic dishonesty is pervasive and predicts adverse political selection: plagiarists are more likely to enter and advance in the public sector. Examining judicial performance, the authors find that judges with plagiarism histories issue more preferential rulings and attract a greater number of appeals relative to their clean-record peers; livestreaming of court proceedings partially mitigates these distortions. The study also documents spillover effects onto colleagues, consistent with a peer-influence channel, and finds that enforcement of academic integrity standards produces modest but meaningful improvements in future professional conduct. The findings speak to how hidden early-life misconduct can propagate through the career pipeline to shape institutional quality in a large developing-country bureaucracy, with implications for understanding the determinants of state capacity and judicial impartiality in China.
