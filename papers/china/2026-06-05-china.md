## 1. Anti-corruption Shocks, Political Incentives, and Regional Economic Development in a Developmental State
**Authors:** Zhenyu Shen, Ruichao Si, Gang Xu
**Date:** 2026
**Source:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304387825001579 (Journal of Development Economics, Vol. 179, 2026)
**Abstract:** This paper investigates how anti-corruption campaigns affect regional economic performance in developmental states lacking robust market institutions. The authors exploit staggered investigations of senior Chinese officials as shocks to the career incentives of politically connected city leaders. Cities whose party secretaries or mayors had patronage ties to investigated officials experienced a notable decline in economic performance, attributable primarily to reduced corruption-enabled activity in land sales, government procurement, and public infrastructure investment. Yet environmental protection efforts intensified and air quality improved significantly in the same cities following the shock. A key finding is that local leaders who achieved strong economic results after their patron's fall faced diminished promotion prospects, indicating that China's anti-corruption campaign fundamentally altered the incentive structure for local officials—shifting the development model from a corruption-driven, growth-oriented one toward a model centered on high-quality development and welfare improvement. The paper rules out market-mechanism or reduced-transfers channels and documents spillover effects to neighboring jurisdictions.

## 2. Consumer Tobacco Product Choices in China: The Impact of a Ban on Flavored E-cigarettes
**Authors:** Hua Wang, Yuhan Deng, Donald S. Kenkel, Alan D. Mathios, Sen Zeng
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35048 (NBER Working Paper No. 35048)
**Abstract:** This study examines how China's 2022 ban on flavored e-cigarettes affected consumer tobacco product choices. The authors conducted two discrete choice experiments—one in 2021 (pre-ban) and one in 2023 (post-ban)—each with approximately 600 adult current smokers, varying product prices and e-cigarette attributes to identify the role of flavor availability in shaping preferences. The findings reveal that banning flavored e-cigarettes reduces stated demand for vaping but paradoxically increases preferences for traditional combustible cigarettes, suggesting substitution toward more harmful products. When the ban is only loosely enforced—consistent with evidence that flavored products remained accessible through informal channels post-regulation—the predicted share of illegal flavored e-cigarettes reaches 53% of the counterfactual share in the absence of a ban, though fewer consumers obtain these products via authorized retailers. The results inform the design of tobacco regulation in China, the world's largest tobacco market, with implications for harm-reduction policy more broadly.

## 3. American Investment in Chinese Renminbi
**Authors:** Bruno Cavani, Christopher Clayton, Amanda Dos Santos, Matteo Maggiori, Jesse Schreger
**Date:** February 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34792 (NBER Working Paper No. 34792)
**Abstract:** This paper examines U.S. mutual fund and ETF holdings of Chinese renminbi-denominated bonds using granular SEC Form NPORT microdata, which provides portfolio-level visibility into cross-border investment positions. The authors document that U.S. holdings of RMB bonds fell sharply in 2024, even as total foreign holdings of Chinese bonds partially recovered, with the decline driven largely by American funds exiting their RMB positions entirely rather than scaling back gradually. The findings reveal a compositional shift in China's foreign investor base—away from U.S. institutional investors—and show that divergent geopolitical risk perceptions or regulatory constraints can fragment global bond markets along investor-nationality lines. The paper demonstrates the value of granular portfolio microdata for measuring financial decoupling and for understanding how bilateral tensions transmit through international capital flows.

## 4. Currency Pegs, Trade Imbalances and Unemployment: A Reevaluation of the China Shock
**Authors:** Bumsoo Kim, Marc De la Barrea, Masao Fukui
**Date:** February 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34823 (NBER Working Paper No. 34823)
**Abstract:** This paper develops a dynamic quantitative model of trade and labor market adjustment to revisit the China shock, focusing on a factor not addressed in earlier work: the role of China's exchange-rate peg. The authors show that by preventing currency appreciation during China's rapid export boom, the peg temporarily accelerated Chinese export growth beyond what would have occurred under a floating rate, intensifying the adverse employment effects on U.S. labor markets and widening the U.S. trade deficit by stimulating Chinese savings. Calibrating the model to actual trade and employment data, they find the peg played a quantitatively important role in the U.S. manufacturing decline, the widening U.S. trade deficit, and unemployment dynamics in import-competing regions. Despite these distributional costs, the overall welfare effect of the China shock—evaluated at the aggregate level—remains substantially positive. The results highlight the importance of exchange-rate policy for understanding how trade integration shocks transmit to labor markets.

## 5. The Ins & Outs of Chinese Monetary Policy Transmission
**Authors:** Silvia Miranda-Agrippino, Tsvetelina Nenova, Hélène Rey
**Date:** January 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34626 (NBER Working Paper No. 34626)
**Abstract:** This paper constructs a novel indicator of the People's Bank of China's monetary policy stance and estimates a policy rule that accounts for the PBC's dual mandate—targeting domestic inflation and exchange rate stability—as well as changes in its operational framework over time. Using the indicator to identify monetary policy shocks in a structural VAR, the authors find that domestic transmission follows textbook patterns, with notable exceptions arising from the active management of the renminbi and China's partially closed capital account, which alter the interest rate and exchange rate channels. Internationally, the spillovers from Chinese monetary policy are substantial: shocks affect commodity markets, global production and trade volumes, and generate considerable pass-through to U.S. goods prices. Financial spillovers are comparatively secondary and arise largely as a byproduct of the trade channel rather than through direct cross-border financial linkages. The results document the growing systemic importance of Chinese monetary policy for global macroeconomic conditions, a dimension previously understudied relative to Federal Reserve spillovers.
