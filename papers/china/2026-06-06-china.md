# China Economics Papers — 2026-06-06

## 1. Equity Financing and Exports: Evidence from IPO Approvals in China
**Authors:** Robin Kaiji Gong, Yao Amber Li, Stephen Teng Sun, Shang-Jin Wei
**Date:** June 3, 2026 (VoxChina); forthcoming, *Journal of International Economics*
**Source:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5387443
**Abstract:** This paper provides the first empirical analysis of how access to equity financing affects firm exports. The authors exploit China's approval-based IPO system as a natural experiment, comparing the export performance of successful IPO applicants with near-misses—firms rejected at the final review stage. A difference-in-differences analysis reveals that IPO approval leads to a significant increase of more than 6% in firm exports annually over the subsequent six years, representing a cumulative gain of roughly 37%. Unlike prior findings on debt financing, IPO approval primarily affects the extensive margin: firms enter new destination-product markets rather than expanding sales in existing ones. Further evidence suggests that IPOs enhance exports by financing export-related intangible investments and risk-taking activities—investments that banks are unwilling or unable to support—enabling expansion into sophisticated, R&D-intensive markets where success depends on innovation and customization.

## 2. Expanding Access or Reshaping Markets? Evidence from Public–Private Partnership Health Insurance in China
**Authors:** Hui Ding, Xintong Wang, Xian Xu
**Date:** May 26, 2026 (VoxChina); published 2026, *Journal of Development Economics*, Volume 182, Article 103780
**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdeveco.2026.103780
**Abstract:** This paper examines China's city-customized supplemental medical insurance (CCSMI), a government-endorsed public–private partnership insurance program launched in 2020 that expanded to cover over 300 million individuals by 2023. While CCSMI succeeded in broadening coverage through low premiums (60–150 CNY) and unrestricted enrollment, the analysis reveals significant substitution effects. Using transaction-level data from a major private insurer and exploiting the staggered rollout of CCSMI across cities in a difference-in-differences design, the authors find that private insurance purchases declined by 13.7% following CCSMI introduction. Among remaining private insurance buyers, coverage levels fell approximately 13.6% and average premiums dropped 4.6%. A back-of-envelope calculation suggests that the decline in private insurance purchases offsets roughly 25% of the increase in CCSMI enrollment. The paper recommends improved PPP design with clearer target definitions, enhanced coordination with existing insurance programs, and better consumer information to optimize coverage alignment with individual needs.

## 3. Industrial Subsidies along Domestic Value Chains and Their Impacts on China's Exports
**Authors:** Wenyin Cheng, David Liang, Bo Meng, Hongyong Zhang
**Date:** May 20, 2026 (VoxChina); March 25, 2026 (VoxEU); RIETI Discussion Paper 25-E-119
**Source:** https://voxchina.org/show-3-458.html; https://www.rieti.go.jp/en/columns/v01_0237.html
**Abstract:** This paper investigates how government industrial subsidies in China propagate through domestic production networks to affect export performance. Using data from 1998 to 2013, the authors combine firm-level survey data, customs records, and input-output tables to construct measures of both direct subsidy receipt and indirect exposure through upstream suppliers. Key findings include: a one standard-deviation increase in direct subsidies boosts export volumes by approximately 10%; upstream subsidies generate meaningful spillover effects primarily by expanding existing exporters' sales into new markets; subsidies correlate with higher product quality rather than simple price competition; and quality-adjusted prices decline even as nominal prices remain stable. The research demonstrates that industrial policy has multiplier effects through domestic value chains, with a sharp acceleration in subsidy intensity visible following the launch of the "Made in China 2025" programme in 2015 and the onset of the US–China trade war in 2018. The findings suggest that policymakers and trade-remedy investigators should account for value-chain propagation when assessing the reach of industrial subsidies.

## 4. A Few Bad Apples? Academic Dishonesty, Political Selection, and Institutional Performance in China
**Authors:** Zhuang Liu, Wenwei Peng, Shaoda Wang
**Date:** January 2026 (NBER Working Paper w34747); BFI Working Paper 2026-17 (March 30, 2026); featured on VoxChina late May 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34747
**Abstract:** This paper examines the relationship between academic misconduct and professional conduct in public office. The authors apply advanced plagiarism-detection algorithms to more than 500,000 publicly available Chinese graduate dissertations. They find that plagiarism is pervasive—public officials show a 19% plagiarism rate compared to 14% in the private sector—and that it predicts adverse political selection: plagiarists are significantly more likely to enter and advance in the public sector, advancing 10–15% faster in government ranks. Focusing on the judiciary and exploiting quasi-random case assignments, the authors find that judges with plagiarism histories issue more preferential rulings and attract a greater number of appeals. Effects are partly mitigated by trial livestreaming. Dishonesty spills over across judges and between judges and lawyers. Finally, exploiting the staggered adoption of plagiarism-detection tools, the study demonstrates that enforcing academic integrity leads to modest improvements in future professional conduct. Survey validation confirms that dissertation plagiarism captures underlying propensities for dishonesty.

## 5. The Geography of Market Power: Rethinking Consolidation in China's Steel Industry
**Authors:** Loren Brandt, Feitao Jiang, Yao Luo, Yingjun Su
**Date:** May 6, 2026 (VoxChina); University of Toronto Department of Economics Working Paper No. 820
**Source:** https://voxchina.org/show-3-456.html; https://www.economics.utoronto.ca/public/workingPapers/tecipa-820.pdf
**Abstract:** This paper examines market power and the welfare effects of consolidation in China's steel industry through a spatial lens. Because steel is heavy and costly to transport, product markets are largely regional, making geographic concentration ratios more informative than national ones. The authors develop an equilibrium model that incorporates regional supply-demand patterns, transportation frictions, and firm productivity differences. The model is estimated using detailed plant-level data spanning the 2018–2024 merger wave. Counterfactual simulations show that regionally focused mergers led by efficient producers would reduce costs by 12.5% and improve total welfare by 24%. Conversely, consolidation that prioritizes the creation of national champions—as has occurred in practice through state-owned enterprise mergers led by groups like Baowu Steel—raises prices and delivers minimal consumer benefits. The actual merger wave captured less than one-quarter of potential welfare gains, suggesting that industrial policy prioritized producer interests and failed to account for shifting regional demand patterns. The findings carry direct implications for the design of competition and industrial policy in heavy industries with high transport costs.
