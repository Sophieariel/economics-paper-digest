## 1. China's Global Ownership

**Authors:** Jennie Bai, Luc Laeven, Yaojun Ke, Hong Ru
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** NBER Working Paper No. 35106 — https://www.nber.org/papers/w35106

**Abstract:** This paper examines Chinese overseas corporate ownership across 161,773 firms in 159 countries over 2012–2021, tracing ownership chains through offshore tax havens to identify the true origins of capital. Chinese-controlled foreign assets expanded at 20% annually, reaching $2.1 trillion — roughly 3% of global corporate assets — by 2021. Chinese investors, especially state-owned enterprises, targeted R&D-intensive and supply-chain-linked firms. While acquired firms increased capital stock and R&D spending, these investments did not boost patent output and were associated with declining profitability. Notably, Chinese parent companies accelerated their own patent grants following acquisitions (an "innovation spillback" mechanism), while increased Chinese ownership reduced R&D at competitor firms. The research reveals a state-driven ownership model prioritizing long-term technology acquisition over near-term returns.

## 2. From Free Rider to Innovator: The Rise of China's Drug Development

**Authors:** Panle Jia Barwick, Hongyuan Xia, Tianli Xia
**Date:** March 2026
**Source:** NBER Working Paper No. 34977 — https://www.nber.org/papers/w34977

**Abstract:** In 2010, China represented less than 8% of global clinical trials; by 2020, it had surpassed the United States in annual registered trial volume. Using a comprehensive database covering publications, clinical trials, drug milestones, sales, and government policies across China, the US, and Europe, this paper finds that China's pharmaceutical transformation was primarily driven by the National Reimbursement Drug List (NRDL) reform, which dramatically expanded the effective market size for innovative drugs. Post-reform, trial quantity increased by 86%, with gains concentrated in reform-exposed disease categories and first-or-best-in-class drugs developed by domestic firms. A decomposition analysis shows the NRDL reform accounts for 43% of oncology trial growth, exceeding the combined contribution of upstream knowledge and talent flows (24%). Dynamic innovation gains from the reform exceeded its static consumer-access gains by threefold, highlighting the long-run importance of demand-side industrial policy for pharmaceutical innovation.

## 3. Household Migration and Collateral Constraint: Cash-based Housing Resettlement in China

**Authors:** Zhiguo He, Zehao Liu, Xinle Pang, Yang Su, Kunru Zou
**Date:** March 2026
**Source:** NBER Working Paper No. 34982 — https://www.nber.org/papers/w34982

**Abstract:** Collateral constraints limit household migration to expensive locations by restricting financing for home purchases. Such endogenous location choice amplifies the impact of relaxing household borrowing constraints. Using China's cash-based shantytown renovation program (2015–2018) as a natural experiment, this paper provides evidence that cash resettlement — by converting illiquid shanty houses into cash — facilitated household location upgrading and raised house prices in more expensive locations. A dynamic spatial model with collateral constraints confirms household migration responses to the cash transfer. Quantitatively, endogenous migration amplifies household housing expenditure responses by around 40%, and is able to explain more than 20% of the housing price growth in 2016–2020.

## 4. Where Has All the Dynamism Gone? Productivity Growth in China's Manufacturing Sector, 1998–2013

**Authors:** Loren Brandt, Johannes Van Biesebroeck, Luhang Wang, Yifan Zhang
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** *Journal of Development Economics*, Volume 181(C) — https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdeveco.2026.103720

**Abstract:** This paper examines China's manufacturing productivity using alternative data sources to address reliability concerns with official government statistics after 2007. The authors reconstruct the sampling methodology of a State Taxation Administration survey to create datasets comparable to the National Bureau of Statistics annual survey, covering 2007–2013. Their findings document a marked slowdown in revenue-based total factor productivity (TFP) growth that cuts across all industries, ownership types, and regions. The loss of dynamism in the private sector and the reduced contribution of new firm entry to aggregate productivity growth are especially prominent. The results challenge optimistic narratives about China's continued productivity catch-up and suggest that structural headwinds appeared earlier than commonly recognized.

## 5. Raising Grievances to the State: The Political Economic Effects of Anti-Corruption Crackdowns on Labor Activism in China

**Authors:** Huiyi Chen
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** *Journal of Development Economics*, Volume 181(C) — https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdeveco.2026.103758

**Abstract:** This paper studies how government actions shape workers' expected returns to striking, exploiting China's anti-corruption campaign as a natural experiment. Using a city-level dataset linking corruption inspections to strikes from 2011 to 2020, the author shows that strike incidents rise sharply after a city's first high-profile inspection, driven largely by wage arrears from private firms and concentrated in the construction and manufacturing sectors. Strikes also spread through network channels, with later-treated and neighboring cities responding to inspections elsewhere. Evidence indicates that inspections weakened firm–government ties and shifted government attention, raising workers' expected returns to labor activism and revealing pre-existing grievances. The results illuminate how anti-corruption enforcement can have unintended labor-market consequences by reconfiguring the political economy of firm–worker–state relations.
