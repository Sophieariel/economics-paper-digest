## 1. China's Global Ownership
**Authors:** Jennie Bai, Luc Laeven, Yaojun Ke, Hong Ru
**Date:** April 27, 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35106
**Abstract:** This paper assembles a micro-level dataset covering 161,773 firms across 159 countries (2012–2021) to examine Chinese overseas corporate ownership. By reconstructing multi-layered ownership chains through offshore tax havens, the authors reveal that Chinese-controlled foreign assets reached $2.1 trillion by 2021, expanding at roughly 20% annually. Key findings: state-owned enterprises strategically acquire R&D-intensive and supply-chain-linked firms; target firms increase capital investment and R&D spending but show no higher patent output and declining profitability; an "innovation spillback" mechanism operates whereby Chinese parent companies accelerate patent grants following developed-economy acquisitions; and Chinese ownership crowds out R&D at peer firms, though industry-level innovation remains stable. The study concludes China pursues "a distinctively state-driven model of global ownership that accepts weaker near-term performance to internalize technological capacity at home."

## 2. The Chinese Current Account Imbalances: Puzzles, Patterns, and Possible Causes
**Authors:** Chang Ma, Shang-Jin Wei
**Date:** April 13, 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35056
**Abstract:** This paper examines China's persistent current account surplus and its origins. While sector-specific trade policies create localized imbalances, they contribute minimally to the overall economy-wide surplus. Instead, the research identifies structural factors as primary drivers: demographic challenges such as skewed sex ratios and disparities in financing access between state-owned enterprises and private firms significantly influence China's external imbalance. The authors conclude that "macroeconomic stimulus can boost imports and reduce the surplus in the short run," yet addressing this issue comprehensively demands deeper reforms targeting the underlying structural impediments rather than temporary policy interventions.

## 3. When Do Financial Frictions Matter for Misallocation?
**Authors:** Yan Bai, Dan Lu, Xu Tian, Yajie Wang
**Date:** March 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34930
**Abstract:** This paper reassesses how financial frictions contribute to capital misallocation by constructing a model informed by firm-level borrowing costs and average revenue product of capital metrics. Using Chinese manufacturing data, the researchers document significant variation in capital productivity alongside a strong correlation between productivity and borrowing expenses — patterns not observed in U.S. data. A heterogeneous-firm framework with firm-specific borrowing costs reveals that removing financial frictions alone increases total factor productivity by 25 percentage points. However, without concurrent distortions in capital allocation, this improvement drops to under 2 percentage points. This disparity emerges from interactions between financial constraints and permanent firm-level distortions, creating endogenous financial variation and selection mechanisms that disproportionately restrict high-productivity firms.

## 4. From Free Rider to Innovator: The Rise of China's Drug Development
**Authors:** Panle Jia Barwick, Hongyuan Xia, Tianli Xia
**Date:** March 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34977
**Abstract:** This paper investigates China's transformation in pharmaceutical development over the past decade. China's clinical trial participation expanded dramatically, "from less than 8% of global clinical trials in 2010" to surpassing the US in annual volume by 2020. The researchers compiled synchronized data across the drug development supply chain, including publications, trials, milestones, and sales. They find that the National Reimbursement Drug List (NRDL) reform was the primary driver, substantially increasing the market for innovative drugs. Post-reform results show an "86% increase" in trial quantity and novelty, concentrated among domestic firms developing novel therapies. A decomposition analysis reveals the NRDL reform accounted for 43% of oncology trial growth, exceeding combined contributions from knowledge accumulation and talent flows. The study concludes that dynamic innovation gains from the reform exceeded static consumer access benefits threefold, emphasizing long-run effects on innovation incentives alongside near-term affordability improvements.

## 5. Ray of Hope? China and the Rise of Solar Energy
**Authors:** Ignacio Banares-Sanchez, Robin Burgess, Dávid László, Pol Simpson, John Van Reenen, Yifan Wang
**Date:** February 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34893
**Abstract:** This paper examines whether industrial policies supporting clean energy boost growth and welfare while reducing emissions. The study analyzes Chinese solar subsidies implemented across city-regions from 2004–2020, using synthetic-difference-in-differences methods on new city and firm panel data. Key findings: production and innovation subsidies proved more effective than demand-side installation subsidies for generating local innovation, net entry, production, and exports; demand policies successfully reduced local pollution; a structural quantitative spatial model revealed local effects persist at the macro level, explaining 40–50% of aggregate changes in solar innovation, prices, and revenues; social benefits to Chinese citizens exceeded subsidy costs by 65% (doubling when environmental gains are included); and innovation subsidies emerged as the most cost-effective subsidy type for increasing overall welfare.
