## 1. A Nascent International Financial Channel of China's Monetary Policy Transmission
**Authors:** Chang Ma, Alessandro Rebucci, and Sili Zhou
**Date:** September 2025 (NBER Working Paper No. 34291); published in *Journal of International Economics*, 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34291
**Abstract:** Chinese private portfolio equity outflows, though small compared to other Chinese outflows, are growing rapidly because of capital account liberalization and capital flight. Using granular stock-holding data on Qualified Domestic Institutional Investor (QDII) mutual funds, the authors identify a nascent financial channel of international transmission of Chinese monetary policy to world stocks. Employing event analysis around monetary policy announcements, they find that tightening depresses returns for country equity indexes and individual U.S. stocks with QDII fund exposure compared to non-exposed stocks, and that these results remain robust after controlling for real transmission channels and confounders. Effects are concentrated among smaller, less liquid firms rather than large China-concept stocks, suggesting that the mechanism operates through household portfolio rebalancing from riskier to safer assets following tighter policy rather than through direct Chinese corporate investment. While these outflows currently remain modest in scale, the growing liberalization of China's capital account means this channel is likely to expand in importance, providing a new international financial linkage through which Chinese monetary policy affects global equity markets.

## 2. How Credible Is Hong Kong's Currency Peg?
**Authors:** Urban Jermann, Bin Wei, and Vivian Yue
**Date:** September 2025 (NBER Working Paper No. 34300); featured on VoxChina April 8, 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34300
**Abstract:** The authors develop an asset-pricing model to evaluate the credibility of Hong Kong's Linked Exchange Rate System (LERS), allowing for the possibility that the peg may not survive with certainty. Deriving closed-form expressions for exchange rates and option prices under a stochastic peg-survival probability, they estimate market-implied probabilities of peg survival and the fundamental value of the Hong Kong dollar using HKD option data. The estimated credibility varies systematically with U.S. interest rate hikes, local liquidity conditions, and Chinese currency dynamics, suggesting that the peg's perceived durability is not unconditional but reflects macroeconomic and geopolitical pressures from both the United States and mainland China. Their option-pricing approach outperforms standard Black-Scholes benchmarks in fitting observed market prices, and the resulting peg-survival probabilities provide a real-time, market-based measure of financial risk in one of Asia's most important currency arrangements. The findings have implications for understanding cross-strait and mainland-Hong Kong financial linkages and the broader credibility of exchange rate commitments in a changing geopolitical environment.

## 3. The Ripple Effects of China's College Expansion on American Universities
**Authors:** Ruixue Jia, Gaurav Khanna, Hongbin Li, and Yuli Xu
**Date:** October 2025 (NBER Working Paper No. 34391)
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34391
**Abstract:** China's unprecedented expansion of higher education in 1999 increased annual college enrollment from 1 million to 9.6 million by 2020. The authors trace the global ripple effects of that expansion by examining its impact on U.S. graduate education and local economies surrounding college towns. Combining administrative data from China's college admissions system and U.S. visa data, they leverage the centralized quota system governing Chinese college admissions for identification and present three key findings. First, the expansion of Chinese undergraduate education drove graduate student flows to the U.S.: every additional 100 college graduates in China led to 3.6 Chinese graduate students in the U.S. Second, Chinese master's students generated positive spillovers, driving the birth of new master's programs and increasing the number of other international and American master's students, particularly in STEM fields. Third, the influx of international students supported local economies around college towns, raising job creation rates outside the universities. The findings highlight how domestic education policy in one country can reshape the academic and economic landscape of another through student migration and its broader spillovers, with implications for understanding how China's domestic human capital investments have shaped the global distribution of scientific talent and educational capacity.

## 4. High-Speed Rail and China's Electric Vehicle Adoption Miracle
**Authors:** Hanming Fang, Ming Li, Long Wang, and Zoe Yang
**Date:** February 2025 (NBER Working Paper No. 33489)
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w33489
**Abstract:** China has achieved one of the fastest electric vehicle adoption rates in the world, with EV market share growing dramatically from 2010 to 2023. The authors examine how China's concurrent expansion of high-speed rail contributed to this adoption miracle using comprehensive vehicle registration data spanning the same period. Combining difference-in-differences approaches with instrumental-variable techniques that exploit plausibly exogenous variation in HSR network expansion, they find that HSR connectivity can account for up to one third of the increase in EV market share observed over this period, with effects strongest in cities served by faster HSR lines. The analysis controls for industrial policies, charging infrastructure investment, supply factors, and economic development. A key mechanism is that HSR connectivity enhances awareness of and familiarity with electric mobility technologies among travelers, reducing range anxiety and shifting preferences toward EVs. The paper also shows that HSR connectivity strengthened the impact of charging infrastructure investment and consumer purchase subsidies on EV adoption rates, suggesting important complementarities between transportation network development and green technology transitions that have broader implications for climate policy design in developing economies.

## 5. Policy Interventions and China's Stock Market in the Early Stages of the COVID-19 Pandemic
**Authors:** Steven J. Davis, Dingqian Liu, Xuguang Simon Sheng, and Yan Wang
**Date:** February 2025 (revised May 2025) (NBER Working Paper No. 33485)
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w33485
**Abstract:** China's stock market greatly outperformed other national equity markets during the first several months of the COVID-19 pandemic, a puzzling pattern given the severity of China's initial outbreak. The authors investigate whether aggressive monetary and credit easing by the People's Bank of China propped up Chinese equity values during early 2020. Analyzing high-frequency data on six months of policy actions in 2020 using an event-study design, they find that interventions raised implied stock market volatility but provide little evidence that they influenced stock price levels. Efforts by Chinese authorities to restrict short-selling and boost equity purchases by state entities also appear modest in scale and limited in duration relative to the scale of market movements. The paper concludes that neither monetary easing nor direct market interventions provide a satisfying explanation for the extraordinary outperformance of China's equity market in early 2020, leaving open the question of what drove the divergence between China's stock market performance and its economic fundamentals during this period. The findings speak to broader questions about the efficacy of market interventions in crisis periods and the distinctive features of China's state-influenced financial markets.
