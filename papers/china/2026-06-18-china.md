## 1. Trade Policy Dynamics: Evidence from 60 Years of US-China Trade
**Authors:** George Alessandria, Shafaat Yar Khan, Armen Khederlarian, Kim J. Ruhl, and Joseph B. Steinberg
**Date:** 2025 (Journal of Political Economy, Vol. 133, No. 3, pp. 713–749)
**Source:** https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/733420 (NBER Working Paper No. 29122: https://www.nber.org/papers/w29122)
**Abstract:** This paper studies China's export growth to the United States from 1950 to 2008, using a structural model to disentangle the effects of past tariff changes from the effects of changes in expectations of future tariffs. The authors find that the effects of China's 1980 Normal Trade Relations (NTR) grant lasted past its 2001 accession to the World Trade Organization (WTO), and the likelihood of losing NTR status decreased significantly during 1986–92 but changed little thereafter. US manufacturing employment trends support their findings: industries more exposed to the 1980 reform have shed workers steadily since then without acceleration around China's WTO accession. A key quantitative result is that reduced policy uncertainty lowered US consumer prices and raised consumer income by the equivalent of a 13-percentage-point permanent tariff decrease—evidence of economically large effects of policy uncertainty on trade volumes and welfare even when nominal tariff rates are held fixed. The paper highlights that standard models attributing China's export surge solely to WTO accession and resulting tariff cuts miss a large fraction of the adjustment that had already occurred through reduced uncertainty in the years before 2001.

## 2. Tickets to the Global Market: First US Patent Awards and Chinese Firm Exports
**Authors:** Robin Kaiji Gong, Yao Amber Li, Kalina Manova, and Stephen Teng Sun
**Date:** 2025 (Journal of International Economics, Vol. 157)
**Source:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022199625000807 (DOI: 10.1016/j.jinteco.2025.104124; SSRN: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4874407)
**Abstract:** This paper investigates how obtaining a US patent enables firms from emerging economies to access global markets, using Chinese customs data matched to US patent records. The identification strategy exploits quasi-random assignment of USPTO patent examiners to applications to isolate the causal effect of a US patent grant on the subsequent export performance of Chinese firms. Successful first-time applicants achieve significantly higher export growth than otherwise-similar applicants whose applications were denied. Strikingly, this effect operates only in small part through market protection for technologically related products in the US market and is instead driven by expansion across a broad set of other destinations and product categories. The response pattern—geographic and product-market breadth rather than US-specific depth—reveals that a US patent award functions primarily as a credibility signal, certifying the Chinese firm's ability to produce high-quality goods and reliably honor contracts, thereby mitigating information frictions that impede emerging-market exporters in global markets. The paper contributes to debates about intellectual property regimes, technology ladders, and the commercial benefits of the international patent system for developing-country firms seeking to build export market shares.

## 3. Trade Policy Uncertainty and Innovation: Evidence from China
**Authors:** Federica Coelli
**Date:** December 2025 (Journal of International Economics, Vol. 158, Article 104170)
**Source:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022199625001278 (DOI: 10.1016/j.jinteco.2025.104170; SSRN: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4169514)
**Abstract:** This paper analyzes the effect of resolving trade policy uncertainty on innovation in China from 1990 to 2007. The identification strategy exploits China's accession to the WTO in 2001, which eliminated the annual congressional votes that had created uncertainty about the continuation of Normal Trade Relations between the US and China. Firms in industries with greater exposure to the elimination of tariff uncertainty—measured by the difference between the NTR tariff rate and the much higher non-NTR rate that would have applied under adverse congressional votes—show significantly larger increases in patent filings and in innovation quality. The innovation response represents genuine technical progress and not merely strategic patenting: independent quality indicators confirm that the patents generated are substantively novel. Timing heterogeneity is informative: lower-quality innovations respond quickly, while higher-quality and higher-opportunity-cost innovations respond more gradually, consistent with dynamic returns to innovative investment. Innovation increases along three margins: entry into new technologies, patents with broader technological scope, and innovation within firms' existing technological portfolios. The findings provide clean causal evidence that uncertainty about market access is a first-order impediment to innovative investment and that resolving it—even without changing actual tariff levels—delivers large gains in productivity-enhancing activity.

## 4. Understanding China's 2024–25 Frontloading from the Lens of Product-Level Export Baskets
**Authors:** Jason Lu and Dimitre Milkov
**Date:** January 23, 2026 (IMF Working Paper No. 2026/013)
**Source:** https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2026/01/23/understanding-chinas-202425-frontloading-from-the-lens-of-product-level-export-baskets-573445
**Abstract:** A defining feature of the 2024–25 US-China trade tensions was China's acceleration of exports to the US ahead of anticipated tariff increases—a phenomenon the paper terms export frontloading. To understand how this was achieved and at what scale, the authors develop a factor model analytical framework that characterizes China's product-level exports across time and destinations using a set of latent export baskets, calibrated to data from China's General Administration of Customs. The paper documents the channels behind the 2024–25 frontloading episode and benchmarks them against the 2018–19 US-China trade tensions. The analysis reveals broad-based adjustments across multiple dimensions not observed in 2018: shipments to the US began accelerating in the second half of 2024, possibly supported by the retention of intermediate inputs that facilitated a domestic production ramp-up; from January 2025 onward, domestic production slowed and shipments of intermediate inputs to Vietnam and other ASEAN economies accelerated, suggesting rapid supply-chain rerouting. The product-basket decomposition shows that frontloading was not confined to a narrow set of vulnerable sectors but spanned a wide range of Chinese manufacturing export categories, indicating both deep productive capacity and organizational flexibility in China's export sector. The results have implications for interpreting aggregate trade statistics and for calibrating the expected medium-term impact of the tariffs.

## 5. Reforms to Reduce China's High Household Savings
**Authors:** Yizhi Xu, Fan Zhang, Rongyu Cui, and Ding Hua
**Date:** December 2025 (IMF Working Paper No. 2025/259)
**Source:** https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2025/12/12/reforms-to-reduce-chinas-high-household-savings-572507 (SSRN: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5961275)
**Abstract:** Household savings in China are markedly higher than in peer economies, and they have been channeled into financing excessive investment rather than supporting private consumption—a structural imbalance at the heart of China's macroeconomic adjustment challenge. This paper examines both the structural and cyclical factors contributing to China's elevated household savings rate. Using empirical analysis of regional variation in government social spending, Hukou-system restrictions on urban settlement, and housing market dynamics, the authors find that low government social spending in rural areas and Hukou restrictions in urban areas play a significant role in sustaining high precautionary savings by households facing uncertain access to health, education, and retirement services. In addition, the paper provides evidence that fluctuations in real estate prices significantly affect household savings through two channels: a wealth effect (higher prices increase the net worth and reduce savings of existing homeowners) and a downpayment effect (higher prices force non-homeowners to save more to accumulate sufficient downpayments). The wealth-effect channel has strengthened relative to the downpayment channel following the recent real estate market correction, as falling prices have depleted homeowner wealth while reducing the downpayment hurdle for new buyers. The paper concludes that durable reductions in household savings require structural reforms: stronger social safety nets and continued Hukou liberalization to reduce precautionary motives, alongside policies that support an orderly transition in the housing market.
