## 1. Geopolitics in the Evaluation of International Scientific Collaboration
**Authors:** Alexander C. Furnas, Ruixue Jia, Margaret E. Roberts, Dashun Wang
**Date:** February 2026 (NBER Working Paper No. 34789); featured on VoxChina March 11, 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34789
**Abstract:** This paper examines whether and how geopolitical considerations distort funding evaluations of international research proposals. The authors conduct two large-scale randomized experiments—one with 1,249 U.S. government policymakers and a second with 7,154 active U.S.-based scientists—presenting proposals for U.S.-based collaboration with either a Chinese or a German institution, holding all other proposal characteristics constant. Unconditional support for U.S.-China collaborations falls to 27.6% among policymakers and 29.7% among scientists, compared with 68.2% and 47.5% respectively for otherwise identical U.S.-Germany proposals—a China penalty of roughly 40 and 18 percentage points. Rather than outright rejection, the primary behavioral response is a shift toward conditional approval, with evaluators imposing additional oversight requirements around national security and intellectual property. The penalty is uniform across scientific fields—basic and applied sciences alike—and persists among Asian-American respondents, indicating geopolitically driven rather than bias-driven evaluation. The findings establish that geopolitics systematically shapes gatekeeping in government science funding, with potentially large downstream consequences for U.S.-China scientific exchange, knowledge flows, and the innovation frontier in both countries.

## 2. Firm Selection and Growth in Carbon Offset Markets: Evidence from the Clean Development Mechanism
**Authors:** Qiaoyi Chen, Nicholas Ryan, Daniel Xu
**Date:** April 2025 (NBER Working Paper No. 33636); featured on VoxChina October 29, 2025
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w33636
**Abstract:** This paper evaluates the effectiveness of the Clean Development Mechanism (CDM)—the world's largest carbon offset market—using comprehensive administrative data on Chinese manufacturing firms. The authors document a striking paradox: firms that obtained CDM offset projects increased their emissions by approximately 49% over the four years following project approval, while the program had projected a 20% reduction. A decomposition analysis reveals that 35 percentage points of the increase reflect selection effects, as higher-growth firms were systematically more likely to apply for and receive CDM projects, and the remaining 14 percentage points reflect direct project-level effects, whereby the offset revenues and technology investments enabled production expansion. The results imply that carbon offsets generated by Chinese CDM participants were largely non-additional: the associated emissions reductions would not have occurred without the program. Welfare calculations indicate that CDM participation by Chinese manufacturers reduces global surplus, with climate damages of approximately $89 per ton of CO₂ equivalent far exceeding the private gains from offset trade of $7–14 per ton—a net global welfare loss on the order of $65 billion. The findings point to fundamental design flaws in project-based offset schemes and call for shifting toward comprehensive, direct emissions regulation.

## 3. Integrating Jurisdictions, Dividing Workers: Consolidation, Labor Markets, and the Migrant-Local Wage Gap in China
**Authors:** Shiyu Bo, Yi Wang
**Date:** 2025 (Journal of Urban Economics 149: 103788); featured on VoxChina December 24, 2025
**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jue.2025.103788
**Abstract:** This paper examines how China's city-county merger reforms—which consolidated 142 counties into adjacent prefectural cities between 2000 and 2018—affected labor market outcomes for local residents and rural-to-urban migrants. Using a difference-in-differences design comparing merged with unaffected counties and exploiting the staggered timing of administrative consolidations, the authors find that mergers increased average real wages overall. However, the gains are distributed highly unequally: local hukou holders benefit by 10.4% while migrants gain only 3.1%, widening the migrant-local wage gap by roughly 7 percentage points. The reform strengthened administrative integration—improving road connectivity, raising tax collection efficiency, and expanding public services—while concentrating economic growth in construction and infrastructure sectors that disproportionately employ local residents. Sectors with higher migrant employment shares experienced smaller wage improvements, consistent with labor market segmentation being reinforced rather than reduced by jurisdictional consolidation. The results illustrate a broader tension in China's urbanization strategy: administrative integration can promote aggregate productivity while simultaneously deepening labor market dualism between documented local workers and undocumented migrant populations.

## 4. Comply-or-Explain Regulation and Investor Protection: Evidence from China
**Authors:** Thomas Bourveau, Xingchao Gao, Rongchen Li, Frank S. Zhou
**Date:** 2025 (Journal of Accounting and Economics 79 (2–3): 101765); featured on VoxChina November 12, 2025
**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacceco.2024.101765
**Abstract:** This paper studies the effects of China's 2012 Shanghai Stock Exchange "comply-or-explain" dividend regulation, which required listed firms either to distribute at least 30% of net profits as dividends or to provide a public explanation at annual investor conference calls. Exploiting cross-firm variation in pre-regulation dividend behavior and using a difference-in-differences design, the authors show that the regulation achieved near-universal behavioral compliance (approximately 98% of firms adjusted payouts or explained non-compliance), and that dividend payout ratios increased substantially among previously non-compliant firms. The central finding is that tunneling—the extraction of private benefits by controlling shareholders through related-party transactions—declined by approximately 38% following the regulation's introduction. Two complementary mechanisms account for this result: firms making mandatory dividend payments have less discretionary cash available for expropriation; firms that chose to explain non-compliance faced heightened regulatory scrutiny and more active investor engagement, reducing the scope for self-dealing. Importantly, managers' stated explanations proved credible, with subsequent investment decisions aligning with disclosed retention rationales, which preserved investor trust and reduced the cost of capital. The findings demonstrate that flexible, incentive-compatible compliance regimes can protect minority shareholders in concentrated ownership environments without sacrificing corporate investment and innovation.

## 5. When Industrial Policy Meets Comparative Advantage: Lessons from "Made in China 2025"
**Authors:** Xiuping Hua, Yong Wang, Junjie Xia, Haochen Zhang
**Date:** 2025 (Research Policy 54 (8): 105298); featured on VoxChina January 28, 2026
**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.respol.2025.105298
**Abstract:** This paper investigates how China's flagship "Made in China 2025" (MC2025) industrial policy interacts with firm-level structural congruence—the alignment between firms' factor input requirements and regional factor endowments. Using a panel dataset of small and medium-sized high-technology firms listed on China's National Equities Exchange and Quotations (NEEQ) from 2014 to 2020, the authors construct a firm-region congruence index and examine its interaction with MC2025 policy exposure. In the absence of industrial policy, higher congruence is associated with significantly better innovation outcomes, measured by both patent applications and forward citations, consistent with comparative advantage principles. However, MC2025 exposure weakens this positive congruence-innovation relationship, suggesting that blanket support for capital-intensive sectors overrides market-based incentives that would otherwise direct resources to their highest-value uses. The distortive effects are most pronounced in 2015–2016, the policy's initial implementation years, and attenuate thereafter, indicating that the primary mechanism is resource reallocation toward policy-targeted firms rather than sustained capability building. The paper provides micro-level evidence that industrial policy effects are heterogeneous and depend critically on the match between policy targets and local comparative advantages, with poorly matched support reducing aggregate innovation efficiency.
