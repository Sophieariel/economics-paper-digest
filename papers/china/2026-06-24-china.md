# China Economics Papers — 2026-06-24

## 1. Empowering Inclusive Work
**Authors:** Yanyou Chen, Mitchell Hoffman, Huilan Xu, and Zhe Yuan
**Date:** June 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35372
**Abstract:** The researchers investigated whether AI technology could enhance workplace performance for employees with disabilities, specifically deaf and hard of hearing (DHH) workers on a major Chinese food-delivery platform. Prior to implementing AI, DHH workers demonstrated slower productivity and lower customer satisfaction ratings compared to non-disabled colleagues, despite working more hours and showing greater retention. The platform deployed an AI-based intelligent outbound calling system designed to improve customer communication for DHH workers midway through the study period. Using difference-in-differences methodology, the authors found that the AI tool significantly improved DHH worker speed and productivity in customer-facing tasks, reduced negative ratings, increased labor supply and worker retention, and eliminated roughly one-third of the disability-related hourly pay disparity while boosting platform profitability for these workers.

## 2. Empathy, Social Networks, and Academic Achievement: Experimental Evidence on Cross-Productivity in Skill Formation
**Authors:** Flavio Cunha, Qinyou Hu, Yiming Xia, and Naibao Zhao
**Date:** June 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35370
**Abstract:** The researchers evaluated a parent-directed empathy development program for middle school students in China through a randomized trial involving 2,246 students. The four-month mobile app intervention yielded short-run gains in empathy and reduced bullying but no immediate academic effects. Over the longer term, treatment students demonstrated substantially better outcomes: they were 3.2 percentage points more likely to gain admission to elite high schools and 2.5 percentage points less likely to miss the entrance exam. The findings reveal that non-cognitive investments can reshape peer relationships to improve academic outcomes, demonstrating how social networks function as a mechanism through which empathy development produces academic gains.

## 3. Decoding China's Industrial Policies
**Authors:** Hanming Fang, Ming Li, and Guangli Lu
**Date:** May 2025 (last revised May 24, 2026)
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w33814
**Abstract:** The researchers analyze China's industrial policies spanning 2000–2022 by leveraging large language models to process approximately 3 million government documents from central, provincial, and municipal levels. Through systematic extraction and verification, they classify policy documents and identify key characteristics including policy objectives, targeted industries, policy tones (supportive or regulatory/suppressive), policy tools, implementation mechanisms, and intergovernmental relationships. The study examines four critical areas: economic and political foundations of targeted sectors, deployment of policy instruments, variations across government levels and regions, and policy impacts on firm activities including market entry, production, and productivity. The analysis also explores political economy dimensions such as top-down policy transmission, persistence, and regional diffusion. The authors identify spatial inefficiencies and industry-wide overcapacity as potential drawbacks of these industrial policies.

## 4. China's Global Ownership
**Authors:** Jennie Bai, Yaojun Ke, Luc Laeven, and Hong Ru
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35106
**Abstract:** The research examines Chinese companies' ownership stakes across 159 countries, tracking 161,773 firms from 2012–2021. The authors reconstructed ownership chains through offshore entities to identify ultimate Chinese control. Key findings include: Chinese-controlled foreign assets grew at approximately 20% annually, reaching $2.1 trillion (roughly 3% of worldwide corporate assets) by 2021; state-owned enterprises predominantly targeted firms with strong R&D capabilities and supply chain connections; target firms expanded capital investment and R&D spending after acquisition, but these inputs fail to generate higher patent output and are accompanied by a significant decline in profitability; an "innovation spillback" phenomenon emerged where Chinese parent companies accelerated patent grants following acquisitions in developed economies; and increased Chinese ownership displaced R&D activities at competing peer firms, though overall industry innovation remained stable.

## 5. The Chinese Current Account Imbalances: Puzzles, Patterns, and Possible Causes
**Authors:** Chang Ma and Shang-Jin Wei
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35056
**Abstract:** China's large current account surplus has been an irritant to its trading partners. While industrial and trade policies often lead to sector-level imbalances, they play a relatively limited role in the economy-wide surplus. Structural factors such as an unbalanced sex ratio and uneven access to financing by state-owned and non-state firms are more important determinants of the current account imbalance. While macroeconomic stimulus can boost imports and reduce the surplus in the short run, any long-term solution would need to involve reforms aiming at addressing the structural problems.
