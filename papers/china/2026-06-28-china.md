# China Economics Papers — 2026-06-28

## 1. Barriers to Entry and Regional Economic Growth in China
**Authors:** Loren Brandt (University of Toronto), Gueorgui Kambourov (University of Toronto), Kjetil Storesletten (University of Minnesota)
**Date:** January 2026 (Review of Economic Studies, Vol. 93, No. 1, pp. 286–326)
**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1093/restud/rdaf029
**Abstract:** This paper documents stark cross-regional differences in manufacturing labor productivity in China in the mid-1990s, alongside rapid unconditional convergence in productivity, wages, and non-state firm start-up rates after 1995. The authors estimate a structural model following Hopenhayn (1992) incorporating location-specific capital wedges, output wedges, and entry barriers, using the Chinese Industry Census. The central finding is that entry barriers — not capital or output wedges — are the primary driver of both the initial (1995) regional productivity dispersion and the subsequent convergence in wages and TFP across provinces. The size of the state sector is shown to causally account for variation in entry barriers across regions and over time: regions with a larger state presence maintained higher barriers that suppressed non-state firm formation and productivity growth. The results imply that reducing barriers to entrepreneurship and firm entry, rather than addressing factor market distortions alone, is critical for eliminating regional productivity gaps in China. The findings contribute to debates on the sources of regional inequality and the mechanisms through which market transition generated spatial convergence, with broader implications for transition economies seeking to leverage liberalized firm entry for development.

## 2. Jumpstarting an International Currency
**Authors:** Saleem Bahaj (University College London; Bank of England), Ricardo Reis (London School of Economics)
**Date:** 2026 (Review of Economic Studies, advance access January–February 2026)
**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1093/restud/rdag011
**Abstract:** This paper develops and tests a theory of how a central bank can actively promote its currency's international role. The key mechanism is that central bank policies reducing borrowing-cost volatility for foreign firms in the domestic currency can elevate the currency's international usage, because firms' working-capital currency choices complement their trade invoicing choices, creating reinforcing network effects. Empirical identification uses the People's Bank of China's bilateral currency swap line agreements signed with foreign central banks between 2009 and 2018. Countries signing a swap line with the PBoC are found to be 12 percentage points more likely to adopt RMB payments at all, and experience a four-fold increase in the value of RMB payments. The results provide a unified theory and evidence for how China's RMB gained international usage over this period and have implications for the future trajectory of the international monetary system. The paper suggests that active central bank policies — rather than merely trade and financial integration — can meaningfully accelerate a currency's internationalization, with consequences for dollar dominance and reserve currency competition in a world of intensifying geoeconomic rivalry.

## 3. Cronies in the Courtroom: Political Interference and Judicial Reforms
**Authors:** Hongbin Cai, Heng Chen, Yuyu Chen, Sisi Zhang
**Date:** May 2026 (Journal of Public Economics, Vol. 257)
**Source:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0047272726000514
**Abstract:** This paper documents large-scale political interference in China's civil judiciary using a dataset of 1.54 million enterprise-to-enterprise court judgments filed between 2014 and 2019. The authors find that enterprises connected to municipal Party secretaries or judicial committee secretaries win litigation at significantly higher rates — a political advantage of approximately 3.81 percentage points. To identify the causal effect and evaluate reform effectiveness, the paper exploits China's 2016 circuit court reform, which established supra-regional judicial circuits with oversight authority over local courts, sharply reducing local officials' ability to influence judicial outcomes. The circuit court reform reduced the politically connected litigation advantage by approximately two-thirds, demonstrating that top-down oversight mechanisms can substantially curb locally-embedded cronyism. The results reveal how political networks distort contract enforcement and legal institutions, systematically diverting judicial outcomes in favor of connected firms and imposing aggregate efficiency costs. The paper contributes to the political economy of legal institutions in China and suggests that judicial reforms insulating courts from local political capture can generate significant economic benefits by improving the predictability and impartiality of contract enforcement — a prerequisite for well-functioning markets in a one-party state.

## 4. Cashless Payment and Financial Inclusion
**Authors:** Shumiao Ouyang
**Date:** 2026 (Journal of Financial Economics)
**Source:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X26000486
**Abstract:** This paper exploits the staggered rollout of Alipay-bundled shared bicycles across Chinese cities as a natural experiment to identify the causal effect of cashless payment adoption on financial inclusion. The instrument generates plausibly exogenous variation in in-person mobile payment experience among over one billion Alipay users — China's dominant digital payments platform. The main results show that engaging in in-person cashless payment in a given month raises the probability of gaining credit access in the same month by 56.3 percent; a 1 percent increase in payment flow raises credit lines by 0.41 percent. Effects are concentrated among those traditionally underserved by formal finance: less-educated individuals and older adults experience the largest gains in credit access. The mechanism operates through alternative credit scoring: BigTech payment platforms accumulate transaction-level behavioral data that provides informative signals about creditworthiness where conventional collateral-based assessments fail. The paper advances understanding of how digital payment infrastructure serves as a gateway to formal credit markets in large developing economies, with implications for policy design seeking to leverage fintech platforms to expand financial inclusion without requiring traditional collateral or credit histories.

## 5. Ray of Hope? China and the Rise of Solar Energy
**Authors:** Ignacio Banares-Sanchez, Robin Burgess, Dávid László, Pol Simpson, John Van Reenen (MIT/NBER), Yifan Wang
**Date:** February 2026 (NBER Working Paper 34893)
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34893
**Abstract:** This paper evaluates the welfare consequences of China's large-scale industrial policy for clean energy, using a synthetic difference-in-differences design at the city-region level with data spanning 2004–2020. The authors exploit variation in the timing and intensity of three categories of solar subsidies — production subsidies, innovation subsidies, and demand-side installation subsidies — across Chinese cities to separately identify their economic and environmental effects. The central finding is that production and innovation subsidies were substantially more effective than installation subsidies in generating economic and social value. Social benefits to Chinese citizens — encompassing employment creation, firm entry, and local income gains — exceed total subsidy costs by 65 percent; this ratio doubles when environmental gains from reduced emissions are included. The paper contributes to the empirical literature on industrial policy by providing welfare accounting for a clean energy intervention with global ramifications. The results are notably favorable: domestic social returns exceeded subsidy costs even before accounting for the massive global cost reductions in solar modules attributable to China's manufacturing scale-up, suggesting that the welfare calculus of clean energy industrial policy in China may be more favorable than critics of overcapacity assert.
