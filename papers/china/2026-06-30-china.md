# China Economics Papers — 2026-06-30

## 1. A Double Dose of Reform: Insurance and Centralized Negotiation in Drug Markets
**Authors:** Panle Jia Barwick (Cornell University; NBER), Ashley T. Swanson (University of Pennsylvania; NBER), Tianli Xia
**Date:** May 2025 (NBER Working Paper No. 33832)
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w33832
**Abstract:** This paper studies China's National Reimbursement Drug List (NRDL) Reform, which pairs centralized price negotiation between the government and pharmaceutical manufacturers with expanded insurance coverage for successfully listed drugs. Using data on cancer drugs negotiated under the reform from 2017 to 2023, the authors estimate a flexible structural model of demand and supply featuring heterogeneous households, Nash bargaining with the possibility of breakdown, and a government objective function that balances consumer surplus against insurance expenditures. The reform substantially reduced retail prices by 48 percent and patient out-of-pocket costs by 80 percent, while driving drug utilization upward by approximately 350 percent; approximately 25 percent of negotiations nonetheless failed, and the insurance design proved regressive across income groups. Welfare accounting yields approximately ¥40 billion in annual consumer surplus gains and an estimated 900,000 life-years extended annually among Chinese cancer patients. Counterfactual analysis demonstrates that insurance expansion and centralized price negotiation are mutually reinforcing mechanisms: neither instrument alone approaches the welfare gains achieved when deployed jointly, confirming that the reform's dual design was essential to its aggregate effectiveness. Alternative market-access designs that pair negotiation with optimally calibrated cost-sharing rules could increase aggregate social welfare by a further 19 percent relative to the current policy. The findings provide empirical evidence on government drug procurement and public insurance in a large middle-income country, with direct implications for reimbursement policy design in China and for other developing economies considering similar regulatory approaches to essential medicines.

## 2. Expected Insurance Coverage and Pharmaceutical Innovation: Evidence from China's National Drug Price Negotiation Policy
**Authors:** Chenyuan Liu (Tsinghua University), Yi Lu, Ronghe Sun, Wanyu Yang
**Date:** June 2026 (presented at NBER East Asian Seminar on Economics, June 8, 2026; revise and resubmit, American Economic Journal: Economic Policy)
**Source:** https://chenyuanliu.github.io/files/DrugInnovation_LiuLuYangSun.pdf
**Abstract:** This paper examines whether the prospect of expanded insurance coverage—even when paired with mandatory price reductions—can stimulate upstream pharmaceutical innovation. The empirical context is China's National Drug Price Negotiation Policy, under which drugs that clear government price negotiation are listed on the National Reimbursement Drug List, dramatically expanding their addressable patient market at a lower unit price. The authors exploit variation in the policy's staggered adoption across disease categories using a difference-in-differences design that compares clinical trial activity for drugs subject to negotiation against vaccine trials in the same disease areas as a within-category control unaffected by the pricing reform. The central finding is that the policy significantly increases clinical trial intensity: negotiation eligibility raises the annual number of clinical trials per disease category by approximately 0.56. Innovation effects are concentrated in genuinely novel treatments rather than incremental modifications to existing drugs, and both domestic Chinese pharmaceutical firms and foreign multinationals respond positively and to broadly similar degrees, indicating that the incentive operates primarily through market-size expansion rather than domestic industrial policy favoritism. Beyond the intensive margin, the paper documents structural changes in the innovation ecosystem: smaller research-oriented firms enter affected disease categories following the policy, and inter-firm R&D collaboration rates increase, consistent with market-expansion effects channeled through network complementarities in development activities. The results imply that carefully designed drug reimbursement policies that combine price negotiation with coverage expansion can serve as complements to patent protection in incentivizing pharmaceutical innovation in large developing economies, and offer a model for countries seeking to balance drug affordability objectives against dynamic efficiency in medical research.

## 3. Firm Entry and Market Interdependence
**Authors:** Cheng Chen, Chang Sun, Zhang Chen
**Date:** June 2026 (presented at NBER East Asian Seminar on Economics, June 7, 2026; working paper forthcoming)
**Source:** https://www.zhangchenecon.com/
**Abstract:** This paper examines how trade barriers imposed in one destination market affect exporters' behavior across all markets, through the channel of interdependent firm entry decisions. Using Chinese Customs data merged with a comprehensive panel of anti-dumping investigations targeting Chinese exports to various countries, the authors document a novel fact: when Chinese products face new import duties in an investigated market, exports of the affected products decline not only in that targeted market but also in third-country markets where no investigation was initiated. Decomposing the third-market trade decline, the authors find it is driven predominantly by a reduction in the number of active exporters entering those markets rather than lower sales by incumbents, and is accompanied by higher average unit values — consistent with the exit of lower-productivity firms whose entry decisions span multiple destinations simultaneously. To rationalize these facts, the paper extends the Melitz (2003) industry equilibrium model to incorporate interdependent entry decisions across export destinations: when fixed costs of market entry must be covered globally by the joint returns across all markets, a tariff in one destination raises the productivity threshold for overall profitability, reducing entry into all markets and raising competitive prices in unaffected destinations. Quantitative calibrations suggest that this entry-interdependence mechanism accounts for a substantial fraction of observed third-market spillovers. The findings carry implications for trade policy incidence: unilateral import barriers have global spillovers that reach beyond the bilateral relationship under investigation, and standard models that treat entry decisions as market-specific will systematically underestimate the trade costs imposed on exporting countries by targeted protection.

## 4. Serial Entrepreneurship in China
**Authors:** Loren Brandt (University of Toronto), Ruochen Dai, Gueorgui Kambourov (University of Toronto), Kjetil Storesletten (University of Minnesota), Xiaobo Zhang (Peking University)
**Date:** 2026 (American Economic Journal: Macroeconomics)
**Source:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4069940
**Abstract:** This paper studies serial entrepreneurship in China — the phenomenon of founders who establish more than one firm — using comprehensive firm registry data covering the universe of all Chinese firms since the early 1990s. Serial entrepreneurs account for roughly one-third of all firms in China and control approximately half of total registered capital. Their firms are systematically more productive: first firms operated by serial entrepreneurs are approximately 11.6 percent more productive than those of comparable non-serial entrepreneurs, and second firms show a 19.7 percent productivity advantage. To understand these patterns, the paper develops a theoretical framework that incorporates entrepreneurial ability, wealth endowments, and capital market frictions as determinants of the decision to establish additional firms and of the timing choice between concurrent and sequential operation. The central finding is that entrepreneurial ability is the primary driver of serial entrepreneurship, while capital market frictions substantially distort the concurrent-versus-sequential decision: entrepreneurs with greater liquidity are significantly more likely to operate multiple firms simultaneously rather than waiting until a first venture matures. This capital constraint effect is especially pronounced when entrepreneurs' existing and prospective firms exhibit similar productivity levels, since the forgone returns to concurrent operation are highest in that range. Around 60 percent of serial entrepreneurs diversify into different industries for their second venture, with sector choice shaped by upstream-downstream linkages and input-output complementarities; firms in closely related three-digit industries show productivity premia three times larger than those in more distant sectors. The results illuminate how the quality of capital markets shapes the allocation of entrepreneurial talent and the speed of organizational learning across China's transition economy, with broader implications for the design of credit policies aimed at unlocking productivity gains from serial entrepreneurship.

## 5. Values of China: Toward a Cultural Map
**Author:** Melanie Meng Xue (London School of Economics)
**Date:** December 2024 (SSRN Working Paper No. 5078516; presented at NBER Chinese Economy Working Group Meeting, Spring 2026)
**Source:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5078516
**Abstract:** This paper constructs the first county-level cultural map of China using approximately 20,000 folktales collected in a nationwide survey of oral traditions conducted in the 1980s. Applying a synthetic-archetype approach based on BERT sentence embeddings and large language models, the author measures how strongly each county's local folktale corpus emphasizes thematic clusters associated with distinct value systems: perseverance and effort, social cooperation and reciprocity, hierarchy and deference to authority, clan loyalty and kin obligations, ancestor worship, and benevolent central governance. The resulting cultural indices exhibit pronounced spatial clustering consistent with known historical variation in agrarian practices, ethnic composition, and state-building exposure across Chinese regions. Validation against the Chinese General Social Survey documents strong cross-county covariation between folktale-based cultural measures and contemporary attitudes including trust in strangers, prosocial behavior toward non-kin, beliefs about the returns to effort versus luck, intertemporal patience, religious observance, and views of both local and central political authority. In prediction-oriented analyses of county log GDP per capita in 2010, the strongest positive correlates of contemporary economic performance are folktale narratives emphasizing effort, patience, and reciprocal exchange; the strongest negative correlates are narratives of ancestor veneration and benevolent central authority. Counties whose oral traditions prioritize clan loyalty over individual merit display persistently weaker economic growth and reduced trust in non-family institutions, consistent with diminished capacity for the impersonal market cooperation that sustained industrial development requires. The paper contributes to the empirical literature on the deep determinants of economic performance, providing evidence that culturally transmitted values — encoded in narrative traditions and persistent across centuries of institutional and political change — continue to shape regional disparities in economic behavior and outcomes across contemporary China.
