# China Economics Papers — 2026-07-05

## 1. Equity Financing and Exports: Evidence from IPO Approvals in China
**Authors:** Robin Kaiji Gong, Yao Amber Li, Stephen Teng Sun, Shang-Jin Wei
**Date:** June 2026 (Journal of International Economics, forthcoming; VoxChina summary June 3, 2026)
**Source:** https://voxchina.org/show-3-460.html
**Abstract:** This paper demonstrates that access to public equity through IPOs enables Chinese exporters to expand into more foreign markets by financing the intangible investments and risk-taking that bank credit alone cannot support. The authors exploit China's approval-based IPO system as a natural experiment, analyzing over 900 manufacturing firms over the period 2000–2016. IPO approval increases firm exports by more than 6 percent annually over six years, cumulating to a roughly 37 percent gain. Growth occurs through extensive-margin expansion — firms enter new destination-product markets rather than scaling up sales in existing ones. Unlike bank credit, which primarily affects intensive margins, equity financing enables intangible investments in R&D, brand development, and market exploration. IPO-approved firms enter more volatile destination markets and pioneer product-destination pairs with low prior Chinese exporter penetration. The findings suggest that equity market development is essential for developing economies pursuing export-led growth, particularly those transitioning toward technology-intensive value chains.

## 2. From Heat to High-Tech: How Innovation Responds to Climate Change
**Authors:** Xianling Long (Peking University), Zhiqiang Wang (Peking University)
**Date:** Journal of Development Economics, Vol. 176 (2025), Article 103525; VoxChina July 1, 2026
**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdeveco.2025.103525
**Abstract:** This paper examines how rising temperatures drive technological innovation across economies, with evidence drawn from Chinese firm and patent data. The central finding is that rising temperatures stimulate both adaptation and mitigation technologies — not necessarily in the regions most directly exposed to heat, but through shifting demand patterns transmitted across supply chains and sectors. Quantifying innovation responses using patent-level climate exposure rather than geographic proximity, the authors find that adaptation patents increased by 8.62 percent and mitigation patents rose 10.68 percent in response to heat exposure since 2003, while non-climate patents remained unchanged. Supply chain spillovers amplify direct innovation effects by a factor of four to five. Innovation drivers include citizen awareness, measured through search trends for climate-related terms, and government policy responses, measured through regulatory indices and carbon shadow pricing. The results document a supply-side innovation channel through which climate change reshapes the direction of technological progress, with implications for climate policy design and the geographic distribution of climate-adaptive technology.

## 3. Firm Entry and Market Interdependence: Evidence from Anti-Dumping Tariffs on Chinese Exports
**Authors:** Cheng Chen (Clemson University), Zhang Chen (Hong Kong University of Science and Technology), Chang Sun (University of Hong Kong)
**Date:** June 7, 2026 (presented at NBER East Asian Seminar on Economics, Tsinghua University)
**Source:** https://conference.nber.org/agenda/simple_printable?conf_id=EASE26
**Abstract:** This paper examines cross-market spillovers of anti-dumping tariffs imposed on Chinese exports. The authors document that products subject to anti-dumping investigation experience declines in total sales not only in the tariff-imposing countries but also in third-country markets that did not impose tariffs — a pattern inconsistent with standard trade diversion. A decomposition exercise shows that simultaneous export declines across countries are primarily driven by a reduction in the number of entrants rather than adjustments at the intensive margin: individual exporters' sales fall in focal tariff countries but increase in non-tariff third countries. To rationalize these facts, the authors develop an industry equilibrium framework based on firms' interdependent market entry decisions across multiple destinations, showing that the entry margin alone can generate cross-market policy spillovers and heterogeneous tariff responses consistent with the data. The paper contributes a new mechanism — entry complementarities across markets — through which trade policy in one country propagates to unaffected markets, with implications for the welfare analysis of anti-dumping measures.

## 4. Expected Insurance Coverage and Pharmaceutical Innovation: Evidence from China's National Drug Price Negotiation Policy
**Authors:** Chenyuan Liu, Yi Lu, Ronghe Sun, Wanyu Yang
**Date:** June 8, 2026 (presented at NBER East Asian Seminar on Economics, Tsinghua University)
**Source:** https://conference.nber.org/agenda/simple_printable?conf_id=EASE26
**Abstract:** This paper examines how developing countries can foster pharmaceutical innovation when drug price negotiations risk eroding firms' incentives to invest in new drugs. China's National Drug Price Negotiation (NDPN) policy addresses this challenge by pairing substantial price cuts on approved innovative drugs with expanded national insurance reimbursement coverage, thereby enlarging the effective market size for innovation even as unit prices fall. Using a difference-in-differences design that compares clinical trial activity for new drugs subject to the NDPN policy against vaccines in the same disease therapeutic areas — which face different pricing and coverage rules — the authors identify the causal effect of expected insurance coverage on pharmaceutical innovation. The results show that the NDPN policy significantly increased clinical trial registrations and the novelty of drugs under development in China. The paper contributes to the literature on the market-size determinants of innovation and to debates about the design of drug pricing and reimbursement systems in countries seeking to balance drug affordability with incentives for pharmaceutical R&D.

## 5. When Forward-Looking Accounting Meets an Unexpected Crisis: Does the Expected Credit Loss Model Amplify Economic Downturns?
**Authors:** Chen Chen (Monash University), Difang Huang (Chinese Academy of Sciences)
**Date:** June 2026 (Journal of Accounting Research, 2026; VoxChina June 24, 2026)
**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1111/1475-679X.70030
**Abstract:** This paper examines whether the Expected Credit Loss (ECL) accounting standard — designed to make bank loan provisioning more forward-looking and counter-cyclical — paradoxically amplifies downturns during unexpected crises. Exploiting China's staggered ECL adoption across listed banks and granular loan-level data from the COVID-19 period, the authors find that ECL-adopting banks cut lending more sharply, raised spreads, and became more selective in loan origination compared to banks still using the incurred credit loss model serving identical borrowers. ECL banks reduced loan origination by 1.7 to 3.2 percent and charged spreads approximately 3.3 percent higher post-pandemic onset. Firms dependent on ECL banks experienced 3.0 percent loan declines and 3.3 percent revenue drops, with effects persisting throughout the two-year post-pandemic observation window. Extrapolating to the full sample of 59 listed ECL banks in China, the authors estimate approximately 4.37 trillion RMB in foregone lending attributable to the ECL regime during the crisis period. The findings reveal a critical policy tradeoff: the same forward-looking provisioning that improves screening during normal times may tighten credit supply sharply when an unexpected crisis occurs, with real consequences for borrower firms.
