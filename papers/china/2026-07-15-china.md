# China Economics Papers — 2026-07-15

## 1. Preferential Credit Policy with Sectoral Markup Heterogeneity
**Authors:** Kaiji Chen (Emory University), Yuxuan Huang, Xuewen Liu (Hong Kong University of Science and Technology), Zhikun Lu, Yong Wang (Hong Kong University of Science and Technology)
**Date:** May 2026 (Journal of International Economics, Volume 161, Article 104238)
**Source:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4847111
**Abstract:** This paper studies how preferential credit policies that target selected sectors affect aggregate productivity and welfare in emerging economies, using China as the empirical setting. Using Chinese firm-level data from 2009 to 2020, the authors document two empirical regularities: sectors with higher markups receive larger credit subsidies, and those same sectors exhibit higher revenue-based productivity. The paper develops a multi-sector quantitative general equilibrium model with endogenously determined markups, calibrated to match the joint distribution of sales within and across sectors. Within this framework, preferential credit subsidies directed toward high-markup sectors raise aggregate productivity and welfare by reallocating market shares from low-markup to high-markup producers, partially correcting the static distortion that markup dispersion imposes on resource allocation. A counterfactual exercise shows that removing China's preferential credit policy would nearly double productivity losses relative to the first-best allocation, indicating that in second-best environments characterized by markup heterogeneity, well-targeted preferential credit can improve rather than reduce overall economic efficiency. The findings contribute to debates over the desirability of industrial credit policies in large emerging economies and provide a quantitative benchmark for evaluating the aggregate productivity consequences of China's system of state-directed credit allocation.

## 2. Household Migration and Collateral Constraint: Cash-based Housing Resettlement in China
**Authors:** Zhiguo He (Stanford University; NBER), Zehao Liu, Xinle Pang, Yang Su, Kunru Zou
**Date:** March 2026 (NBER Working Paper No. 34982)
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34982
**Abstract:** This paper examines how relaxing borrowing constraints through cash transfers affects household migration decisions and regional housing market equilibria, exploiting China's cash-based shantytown renovation program (2015–2018) as a natural experiment. Under the program, urban shantytown residents received cash compensation rather than replacement-in-kind housing, providing an unexpected liquidity injection that relaxed the collateral constraints households faced when attempting to purchase homes in more expensive higher-tier cities. Exploiting cross-city variation in the intensity of cash-based versus in-kind resettlement, the authors document that the cash transfer program significantly increased household relocation to higher-tier cities. This endogenous migration response amplified housing expenditure increases through the collateral-relaxation channel: cash recipients increased housing expenditure by approximately 42.5% more than a no-migration counterfactual would predict, because migration enabled purchases in high-price-tier markets where collateral values were higher. The authors develop a dynamic spatial economic model incorporating collateral constraints and endogenous migration to quantify general equilibrium housing market effects. Model-implied estimates suggest that the migration mechanism induced by cash resettlement explains more than 20 percent of observed housing price growth in Tier-1 and Tier-2 cities during 2016–2020, illustrating how liquidity provision can amplify spatial reallocation and generate substantial cross-market price spillovers.

## 3. AI Patents in the United States and China: Measurement, Organization, and Knowledge Flows
**Authors:** Hanming Fang (University of Pennsylvania; NBER), Xian Gu (Durham University), Hanyin Yan (Tsinghua University), Wu Zhu (Tsinghua University)
**Date:** April 2026 (NBER Working Paper No. 35022)
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35022
**Abstract:** This paper develops a high-precision classifier for identifying artificial intelligence (AI) patents by fine-tuning PatentSBERTa on manually labeled data from the USPTO's AI Patent Dataset, achieving 97.0% precision, 91.3% recall, and a 94.0% F1 score; the classifier generalizes well to Chinese patents based on citation and lexical validation. Applying it to granted U.S. patents (1976–2023) and Chinese patents (2010–2023), the authors document rapid growth in AI patenting in both countries and broad convergence in AI patenting intensity and subfield composition, even as China surpasses the United States in recent annual patent counts. The organization of AI innovation nevertheless differs sharply: U.S. AI patenting is concentrated among large private incumbents and established geographic hubs, whereas Chinese AI patenting is more geographically diffuse and institutionally diverse, with larger roles for universities and state-owned enterprises. For publicly traded companies, AI patents command a robust market-value premium in both countries. Cross-border citation patterns reveal continued and substantial technological interdependence rather than decoupling: Chinese AI inventors draw disproportionately on U.S. frontier research even as the two countries' patent portfolios converge in aggregate volume and technical scope. The findings suggest that the trajectory of the U.S.–China technology relationship is characterized by asymmetric knowledge flows rather than full technological decoupling, with implications for industrial policy, technology security, and the governance of cross-border innovation.

## 4. Equity Financing and Exports: Evidence from IPO Approvals in China
**Authors:** Robin Kaiji Gong, Yao Amber Li, Stephen Teng Sun, Shang-Jin Wei (Columbia University; NBER)
**Date:** February 2026 (HKUST Business School Research Paper No. 2025-227; SSRN)
**Source:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5387443
**Abstract:** While finance theory distinguishes the roles of equity and debt financing in supporting firm growth, their differential impacts on international trade remain underexplored. This paper provides the first empirical analysis of how access to equity financing affects firm exports, leveraging the unique institutional setting of China's IPO market, where initial public offerings require stringent regulatory approval at multiple review stages. The empirical strategy compares the subsequent export performance of firms that succeed at the final IPO review meeting with those of "near misses" — applicants rejected at the final stage despite satisfying all prior eligibility hurdles — exploiting the near-random variation generated by the sequential review process. The analysis documents that IPO approval substantially increases firm exports, with effects driven primarily by investments in intangible assets and export market entry that require equity-scale financing beyond what bank credit can sustain. Export diversification across both products and destinations expands particularly strongly, consistent with equity financing enabling the lumpy sunk-cost investments associated with entering new foreign markets. The findings highlight a previously underemphasized channel through which equity market development supports export-led growth and contribute to the literature on finance–trade linkages, with implications for capital market policy in developing economies seeking to accelerate their export trajectories.

## 5. From Heat to High-Tech: How Innovation Responds to Climate Change
**Authors:** Xianling Long, Zhiqiang Wang
**Date:** 2026 (Journal of Development Economics, Volume 176, Article 103525)
**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdeveco.2025.103525
**Abstract:** This paper examines how rising temperatures drive technological innovation across China's industrial sector, challenging the assumption that climate-induced innovation is confined to firms directly exposed to heat stress. The authors measure patent-level climate exposure by linking patents to the downstream products they cover and the geographic distribution of demand for those products, enabling identification of climate-driven innovation at upstream supplier firms that may operate in temperate locations but serve heat-exposed final-good markets. Exploiting variation in temperature shocks across Chinese cities and industry pairs, the authors estimate that heat exposure significantly increases both climate adaptation and mitigation innovation: a standard deviation increase in temperature exposure is associated with an 8.62 percent increase in adaptation patents and a 10.68 percent increase in mitigation patents. Climate-induced patents are of higher quality on average, as measured by forward citation counts. Supply chain spillovers amplify total innovation effects four to five times beyond the direct impacts on immediately exposed firms, with amplification operating through input–output linkages between climate-exposed downstream markets and upstream innovators. Two demand-side mechanisms drive the innovation response: shifts in citizen consumption behavior toward climate-protective products, tracked via online search data, and regulatory policy responses by local governments to temperature shocks, measured through text analysis of government documents. The findings suggest that climate-driven market signals and policy incentives together redirect the direction of technical change toward resilient technologies, with implications for understanding directed innovation in developing economies facing escalating climate risk.
