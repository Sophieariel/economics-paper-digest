# Labor Economics Papers — 2026-05-02

## 1. Gender-Specific Economic Shocks and Household Bargaining Power [Gender]
**Authors:** Rania Gihleb, Osea Giuntella, and Dor Morag
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35118
**Abstract:** This paper develops a machine-learning-based index measuring the "gendered" nature of consumer goods and investigates how labor market shocks affecting men's and women's relative economic positions influence household spending patterns. Exploiting two natural experiments — the adoption of industrial robots, which weakened men's relative economic standing, and fracking expansion, which benefited young male workers — the authors find that robot adoption shifted household consumption toward products favored by single women, consistent with increased female bargaining power. Fracking expansion produced weaker evidence of the opposite effect. Neither shock substantially changed total children's product spending, though robot exposure reallocated spending toward daughters' goods over sons'. The results provide novel evidence on the consumption channel through which gender-specific labor demand shifts alter intra-household resource allocation, contributing to the literatures on household bargaining, gender and labor markets, and automation. NBER Working Paper No. 35118.

## 2. A Gender Quota for Top Executives: Diversity without Disruption [Gender]
**Authors:** David A. Matsa and Amalia R. Miller
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35030
**Abstract:** This paper provides causal evidence on the effects of Germany's mandate requiring major public companies to include at least one woman on their top executive teams. The quota increased female representation among top executives by approximately two-thirds, with women typically appointed to HR or other specialized roles rather than to CEO positions. Using an event-study and difference-in-differences design around the policy's implementation, the authors find no significant effects on female representation in lower management ranks, firm-level gender equality policies, firm value, or financial performance. The results imply that board and executive quotas can achieve meaningful representation gains at the top without disrupting organizational hierarchies or shareholder value, but also without meaningful spillovers to the broader workforce. The paper contributes to the literatures on gender and corporate governance, executive labor markets, and the design of anti-discrimination policy. NBER Working Paper No. 35030.

## 3. The Impacts of Medicaid's Managed Long-Term Services and Supports on Health Outcomes in Medicare [Health]
**Authors:** Ajin Lee, Maya Rossin-Slater, Becky Staiger, and Amanda Su
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35055
**Abstract:** This paper examines how transitioning dual-eligible beneficiaries aged 65 and older from fee-for-service to managed care models for long-term services and supports (MLTSS) affects health outcomes. Exploiting the county-by-county rollout of Managed LTSS programs in Florida and New York as a natural experiment in a difference-in-differences framework, the authors find that MLTSS leads to a 4.2 percent increase in hospitalizations in Florida but no significant change in New York. Declining flu vaccination rates in Florida appear to explain the rise in respiratory hospitalizations, suggesting that managed care transitions can reduce preventive care utilization. The heterogeneity across states points to the importance of implementation details and market structure in determining whether managed care reforms improve or harm health outcomes for vulnerable elderly populations. The paper contributes to the literatures on health economics, aging, and Medicaid managed care policy. NBER Working Paper No. 35055.

## 4. Hospital Billing Regulations and Financial Well-Being: Evidence from California's Fair Pricing Law [Health]
**Authors:** Yaa Akosa Antwi, Marion Aouad, and Nathan E. Blascak
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35080
**Abstract:** This paper examines how California's 2007 Fair Pricing Law — which caps hospital bills for financially vulnerable uninsured and underinsured patients — affects household financial well-being. Using county-level variation in uninsured rates as an instrumental proxy for exposure to the law, the authors find that greater exposure reduces non-medical debt in collections and improves credit scores, with effects concentrated among lower-income households in high-exposure counties. The results suggest that price regulation at the point of health care delivery can have meaningful downstream effects on household balance sheets and financial stability, beyond the direct reduction in medical debt. The paper contributes to the literatures on health insurance, consumer financial protection, and the interactions between health care markets and household financial health. NBER Working Paper No. 35080.

## 5. The Emergence of the Poverty–Mental Health Gradient and the Great Pakistan Earthquake of 2005 [Health]
**Authors:** Tahir Andrabi, Benjamin Daniels, and Jishnu Das
**Date:** April 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35060
**Abstract:** This paper investigates whether and how the relationship between poverty and mental health varies with exposure to a major disaster. Using data from 126 Pakistani villages at varying distances from the fault line of the devastating 2005 earthquake, the authors exploit geographic proximity as a natural experiment. They find that in unaffected areas there is no correlation between household consumption and mental health, but in areas closest to the fault line, mental health improves monotonically with consumption, and this gradient disappears beyond 20 kilometers from the epicenter. The results suggest that large negative shocks activate a poverty–mental health gradient that is otherwise latent, with implications for targeting mental health interventions in post-disaster settings. The paper contributes to the literatures on health economics, development economics, and the long-run consequences of natural disasters for human capital and well-being. NBER Working Paper No. 35060.
