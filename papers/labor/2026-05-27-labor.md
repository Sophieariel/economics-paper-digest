# Labor Economics Papers — 2026-05-27

## 1. Authority Figures and the Polarization of Gender Norms [Gender]
**Authors:** Ali Abboud, Samuel Bazzi, Serena Canaan, Antoine Deeb, Pierre Mouganie
**Date:** May 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35174
**Abstract:** This paper studies how authority figures in higher education influence gender attitudes over extended periods. Using random assignment of first-year students to faculty advisors at an elite Middle Eastern university and tracking outcomes through alumni surveys spanning up to 24 years, the authors find sharply divergent effects by gender: women assigned to female advisors adopt more egalitarian views about politics and work, while men become more conservative. These patterns intensify among religious students and in male-dominated STEM disciplines where female authority figures represent a striking departure from conventional stereotypes. Senior, high-performing female advisors produce the strongest impacts, whereas randomized contact with successful female peers shows minimal effect. The findings point to a paradox: expanding female representation in elite leadership roles simultaneously expands women's opportunities while triggering male resistance, thereby intensifying gender divisions rather than reducing them.

## 2. Bachelors Without Bachelor's: Gender Gaps in Education and Declining Marriage Rates [Gender]
**Authors:** Clara Chambers, Benjamin Goldman, Joseph Winkelmann
**Date:** May 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35179
**Abstract:** This paper examines how the dramatic reversal of the college gender gap — from predominantly male to predominantly female enrollment — correlates with changes in the marriage market. The authors find that college-educated women have maintained stable marriage rates by increasingly marrying high-earning non-college men. At the same time, the share of non-college men who earn above the national median and remain unmarried to college women has fallen substantially, sharply contracting the pool of marriageable partners for non-college women. Using variation across local labor markets, the authors show that education gaps in marriage are smaller where non-college men face lower rates of joblessness and incarceration, suggesting that the deteriorating economic conditions of less-educated men are the primary driver of declining marriage rates among non-college women.

## 3. A Promise Worth Keeping? Impacts of Free Community College on Degrees and Earnings [Education]
**Authors:** Paige Schoonover, Jonathon Attridge, Celeste K. Carruthers, Jilleah G. Welch
**Date:** May 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35226
**Abstract:** This paper evaluates Tennessee Promise, a tuition-free community college initiative that expanded from a pilot program to statewide eligibility. Using the staggered rollout of the program as a quasi-experimental design, the authors find that Tennessee Promise increased college enrollment by 5.4 percentage points among 19-year-olds and meaningfully boosted associate's degree completion. The study documents modest gains in bachelor's degree attainment and earnings by age 24. A cost-benefit analysis concludes the program pays for itself under reasonable assumptions about the returns to college. The paper combines two earlier circulating working papers and provides among the most comprehensive causal evidence on the effects of statewide free community college programs on both educational attainment and early labor market outcomes.

## 4. Building Opportunity: The Intergenerational Effects of Chilean School Construction [Education]
**Authors:** Adrienne Lucas, Patrick McEwan
**Date:** May 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35042
**Abstract:** This paper examines a 1965–1966 Chilean policy that constructed primary school classrooms in supply-constrained areas. Using quasi-experimental methods exploiting the geographic variation in school construction and linked census data, the authors demonstrate that childhood exposure to the school construction program substantially improved the schooling and labor market outcomes of exposed adults. The program also reduced gender disparities in education, with women exposed to the policy generating significant intergenerational benefits for their children's educational progress. A social rate of return calculation yields a return 13 times the public investment, suggesting large positive externalities from school infrastructure investment in developing countries. The results speak to the importance of physical school access as a binding constraint on human capital accumulation.

## 5. The Effects of GLP-1 Use on Mental Health, Self-rated Health, Employment and Marriage [Health]
**Authors:** Robert Kaestner, Cuiping Schiman
**Date:** May 2026
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35198
**Abstract:** This paper examines how the use of GLP-1 medications — a class of drugs that have been rapidly and widely adopted due to their documented weight-loss effects — affects mental health, self-rated health, employment, and marriage among individuals with diabetes. Leveraging the rapid adoption of GLP-1 drugs among diabetic patients as a source of variation and using longitudinal panel methods to track outcomes over time, the authors find that GLP-1 use is not meaningfully associated with mental health, self-rated health, employment, or marriage. The authors note that this null result contributes important evidence about the broader societal impacts of GLP-1 medications beyond their documented clinical effects, which is necessary for comprehensive cost-benefit evaluation of these drugs and related public health policy.
