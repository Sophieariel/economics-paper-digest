# Labor Economics Papers — 2026-06-09

## 1. Caring Dads? Universal Childcare, Paternity Leave, and Fathers' Labor Supply [Fertility]
**Authors:** Mathias Huebener, Malin K. Mahlbacher, Sophia Schmitz
**Date:** May 2026
**Source:** https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/18696
**Abstract:** The researchers investigate how expansions of publicly subsidized childcare influence labor division within families during early childhood, with particular emphasis on paternal responses. Exploiting the staged rollout of childcare services for children under three years old across German counties, they demonstrate that greater childcare availability hastens enrollment and facilitates mothers' workforce reentry. Fathers respond by taking more parental leave and reducing full-time employment, though weekday caregiving involvement does not rise substantially. The analysis reveals that childcare initiatives can reallocate household work patterns but produce smaller aggregate employment gains than maternal participation alone would indicate, since fathers partially withdraw from the labor market alongside maternal returns.

## 2. Dissecting the Gender Gap in Intergenerational Transfers: The Case of Germany [Gender]
**Authors:** Karan Singhal, Markus M. Grabka, Eva Sierminska
**Date:** June 2026
**Source:** https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/18706
**Abstract:** The researchers examine how gender shapes the receipt of family wealth transfers across generations in Germany over four decades of Socio-Economic Panel (SOEP) data. They document persistent asymmetries: while women are in some cases more likely to report receiving an inheritance, men are more likely to receive gifts and obtain larger total transfer amounts. Regional heterogeneity is pronounced—younger West German women face particular disadvantages in gift-receiving, whereas East Germany displays no systematic gender gap. Using Oaxaca–Blinder decompositions and Recentered Influence Function (RIF) regressions across the wealth distribution, the study finds that transfers, especially gifts, meaningfully contribute to gender wealth inequality. Despite legally neutral inheritance rules, the findings indicate that testamentary freedom and persistent social norms generate unequal outcomes, with family wealth transfers serving as an underexamined channel perpetuating gender-based economic inequality across generations.

## 3. Policy or Pandemic? Explaining the Rise in Young Adults' Private Health Insurance Take-Up in Australia [Health]
**Authors:** Nathan Kettlewell, Tiannan Gao
**Date:** June 2026
**Source:** https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/18708
**Abstract:** This study examines how a 2019 Australian age-based discount policy shaped private health insurance adoption among young adults. The policy offered a perpetual premium discount of 2%–10% to individuals who purchased coverage before age 30. Employing synthetic difference-in-differences, the authors find the policy generated modest positive take-up effects in the pre-pandemic period amid an overall trend of declining membership. During COVID-19, however, the treatment group (those eligible for the age discount) experienced substantially higher insurance enrollment. A careful decomposition of the pandemic spike shows that the relative increase in demand among the young is predominantly driven by age-specific pandemic responses rather than by the discount policy itself. The findings highlight the difficulty of disentangling policy effects from contemporaneous shocks when both operate on the same demographic margin.

## 4. Characterizing the File Drawer: Evidence from a Meta-Analysis of Parent-Interventions Around the World [Education]
**Authors:** Peter Bergman, Nat Chowanajin
**Date:** May 2026
**Source:** https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/18701
**Abstract:** The researchers synthesize evidence from 82 randomized controlled trials spanning over 20 countries evaluating low-cost, remote parental engagement interventions delivered through text messages, phone calls, and apps. Using a joint likelihood function that incorporates both published studies and unpublished results from trial registries and research labs, they address publication bias systematically. Bias-corrected effect sizes are 0.05 SD for test scores, 0.07 SD for grades, 0.05 SD for attendance, and 0.03 SD for enrollment. A key methodological finding is that statistically insignificant results are still written up at high rates in the test score literature, suggesting the file drawer problem may be less severe than commonly assumed in this domain. The authors conclude that parental engagement interventions demonstrate genuine positive public value and call for future work to identify the sources of heterogeneity across settings and populations.

## 5. De-biasing or Backlash? Confronting Prejudice Among Police Officers in India [Gender]
**Authors:** Sofia Amaral, Kim Chaney, Victoria Kaiser, Nishith Prakash, Abhilasha Sahay
**Date:** May 2026
**Source:** https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/18699
**Abstract:** This study examines how police officers respond when confronted with evidence of their own biased handling of gender-based violence (GBV) cases. Working with 323 officers in Madhya Pradesh, India, the team conducted a lab-in-the-field experiment centered on complaint-processing decisions. Results reveal sharply divergent effects by officer gender. Female officers increased victim statement prioritization by 23 percentage points—a 27 percent improvement over the control group. By contrast, male officers exhibited a backlash response: they deprioritized victim statements, elevated offender accounts, and assigned more negative stereotypes to GBV victims one week after being confronted with their bias. The divergence is explained by baseline bias levels: 72 percent of female officers exhibited only mild initial bias, while 51 percent of male officers showed strong initial bias. The findings caution that de-biasing interventions can produce counterproductive outcomes for highly biased individuals, with direct implications for the design of institutional anti-discrimination training programs and women's access to justice in the labor market and beyond.
